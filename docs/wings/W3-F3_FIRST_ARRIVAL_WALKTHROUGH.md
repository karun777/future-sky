# W3-F3 — First Arrival: Player Walkthrough

**Status: DRAFT FOR REVIEW — 20 October 2026. Not implemented or adopted final copy.**
Home: W3 Beings / W3-F3 — Who Is Waking Up?
This is a proposed authoring experience followed by a candidate awakening transition. Names of origins, final class eligibility and interface controls remain unresolved.
Choices below concern your fictional character, not personal information about you.

## 1. A direction to begin

> Before you wake, choose a direction for the person you will inhabit.
>
> What draws you: dreams and machines; kinship and healing; rebuilding broken structures; instinct and companionship; language and symbols; stealth and reflection; the movements of the sky; music and disruption?
>
> Each opens a class path. You are choosing an inclination to develop. You may know more about it now than your character will remember on waking.

Interface proposal: show existing Red Book class cards with name, short evocative description, a concrete example of play and “read more”. Preserve distinctive class writing and actual future mechanical differences; do not infer class automatically from a personality quiz.
Unwritten needs an explicit eligibility decision before it appears as an ordinary starting option. No final roster adopted by this draft.

> Choose your starting inclination.
>
> You will discover how it lives in your actions.

## 2. A history with room in it

> Your life did not begin in this room.
>
> Where does your human lineage come from? Where were you raised? Those may be different places.
>
> You can establish a few things now and leave the rest to emerge: an upbringing, a practiced craft, someone who mattered, a place you have left.

Proposed fields:
- Ancestral origin: eventual authored human-origin options, with descriptions and explicit stat/ability effects when designed. No invented options or bonuses here.
- Background: short free text or future authored prompts. Optional unresolved details.
- Prior body and preserved-brain history: include only where appropriate to the selected arrival; do not force Efiishent's particular biography onto every player.
- Name: a starting name or working designation. Whether the character recognises it immediately remains a review choice; a technical record identifier need not become their public name.

> What would you like to establish about your past?
>
> A few lines are enough. You can leave questions unanswered.

No automatic “past revealed” generator or permission to rewrite a player's established history is implied by leaving gaps. Later discoveries need compatible authoring.

## 3. The body you will wake in

> What body will you inhabit?
>
> Begin with the features you want to define: appearance, build and bodily sex characteristics. You can be specific or keep the description broad.
>
> Your body will be yours to encounter through movement, sensation and reflection.

Interface proposal: human-scale range consistent with shared essential biology. Gender and bodily sex characteristics are distinct; neither auto-populates the other. Current embodiment and personal ancestry may differ for a preserved-brain character; how origin abilities attach remains an open design dependency.
No requirement to display intimate anatomy. No diagnosis, dysphoria or emotional response inferred from body choices.

> How would you like your character to be recognised?
>
> You may supply a gender description and pronouns. You may leave either undescribed for now.

Proposed optional “relationships” area, separate from required setup:

> Is there anything you would like to establish about attraction or relationships?
>
> You may describe sexual or romantic orientation, the kinds of connections you welcome, or leave this to play.

This is optional fictional-character authorship. Labels do not force particular attraction or consent and do not establish NPC knowledge.
Do not claim a functioning privacy or social-disclosure system: implementation must establish the difference between author-only details, player-visible profile, chosen public introduction and what others can actually observe.

## 4. Before waking

> You have chosen a beginning.
>
> Review the person you have outlined. You can revise these choices before entering.
>
> Some things may be clear to you and uncertain to them: a name, a skill, an old affection, the shape of the hands they last remember.
>
> When you are ready, awaken.

Interface proposal: concise review of entered choices, distinguishing “established history” from “uncertain”, plus edit/back actions. Do not silently generate missing ancestry or relationship details. Persistence and later-edit rules remain to design; this copy promises no implemented reset/change-body service.

## 5. Awakening — candidate opening beat

> A low thump comes from somewhere beyond you.
>
> Again.
>
> Something supports your weight. You draw a breath.
>
> Your fingers move against woven fabric.
>
> Light begins to resolve.

Stop here for attention rather than automatically narrating a long itinerary.
The sequence illustrates hearing → touch → vision as authored direction; no fixed timing, progression triggers or bypass rules are settled. Taste and smell remain later parts of sensory return, not omitted by a three-sense sample.

Candidate prompt:

> What catches your attention?
>
> The sound. Your breathing. Your hands. The space around you.

These are suggested attention targets, not live button labels or a closed list of permitted actions.

### If you attend to your hands

> You lift them.
>
> Open them. Close them. Turn them over.
>
> They answer your intention.
>
> You do not recognise them.

This is a candidate old-brainer/unfamiliar-body response. It is not universal: body familiarity, memory and arrival context must determine the final response. Do not overwrite a deliberately familiar-body choice.

### If you attend to the sound

> A repeated thump beyond the wall.
>
> Machinery. Probably.

Author truth: ordinary building maintenance. No mystery, compulsory investigation or hidden skill unlock is implied.

## 6. Preparing to meet the world

After sensory access supports surveying the space, room-facing imagery can accompany inspection.

> A berth. A hatch. A frosted viewport.
>
> Storage panels along the wall. A small display.
>
> A clothing compartment within reach.

Existing candidate actions, not new implementation in this draft:
- inspect the body or garment;
- examine panels, viewport, display and other visible fittings;
- browse/inspect the current wardrobe;
- choose a first outfit when eligible, or change owned clothes;
- leave using the existing route, subject to future agreed arrival policy.

The scene should explain practical opportunities through attention. Clothes do not assign class, employment or faction.
The display's performance booking belongs to Efiishent's particular opening; it must not silently give every participant a gig. Other arrival instances need their own obligations and circumstances.

## Review questions and boundaries

The explicit starting class inclination is agreed. The rest of this sequence is proposed placement and writing:
1. Does a short authoring passage before awakening preserve the desired mystery and agency?
2. Which background/body details must exist before entry, and which may remain undescribed?
3. Should name recognition be selectable, assumed or left entirely to roleplay?
4. Should body creation precede awakening, occur through reflection within it, or use a hybrid?
5. Which orientation/relationship details are useful to author now rather than during a specific encounter?

W3-F3 remains REVIEW. No final class/origin roster, stat changes, profile fields, privacy engine, mandatory questionnaire, romance mechanics or deployment.
Shared checkpoints: W1 embodied continuity; W2 contextual pod; W4 author/player agency; W5 sensory timing; W6 evocative presentation; W7 eventual truthful persistence; W8 clear adoption boundaries.
