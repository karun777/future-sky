## What the rehearsal taught us

| Finding | Consequence for creation tools |
| --- | --- |
| Recognition of a body differs from ease of inhabiting it | Do not collapse both into “familiar/unfamiliar body” or infer either from memory loss |
| A few authored details yield a strong character | Support concise free-text authorship; do not require exhaustive questionnaires |
| Dream experience may be certain as experience while its external explanation remains open | Keep character account, memory, belief and verified occurrence distinct |
| Class inclination helped the player begin without a complete skill list | Preserve the agreed starting inclination; do not infer abilities from descriptive prose |
| Appearance includes subtle variation and subjective impression | Preserve prose alongside any later structured fields; do not reduce it to a stat bonus |
| Intimate self-description need not be publicly disclosed | Decide visibility explicitly before storing/rendering optional profile details |
| The author lost track of why questions continued | State the rehearsal's purpose, show a summary, offer stop/review, and establish a short endpoint |

## Process finding

The rehearsal became a sequence of questions without a visible endpoint or consolidated result. Karun asked what we were doing. This is a genuine usability finding: a technically complete field list can still be a poor creation experience.

Proposed correction: one short screen explaining purpose, a small set of prompts with skip options, then a review summary and explicit transition to play. Do not automatically keep asking because another field could be filled. This is a design recommendation, not implemented UI.
