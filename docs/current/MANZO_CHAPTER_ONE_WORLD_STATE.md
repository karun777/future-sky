# Future Sky — Manzo Chapter One: The World Before the Comet

**Feather:** 56  
**Status:** Proposed worldbuilding scaffold — authorial review required  
**Era:** Manzo  
**Primary stage:** Triton Central and Neptune Lounge  
**Depends on:** `docs/current/THE_ETERNAL_STRUGGLE.md`, Red Book v7.2  
**Runtime impact:** None. No faction or social-cycle engine is activated by this document.

## Opening proposition

Triton Central is not a failed civilisation. It is a remarkable one.

Its inhabitants live amid technologies that would have seemed supernatural to their ancestors. Ordinary life rests on systems of astonishing precision, including capacities that earlier generations had to cultivate through knowledge, discipline, community or direct experience.

Many inhabitants enjoy real prosperity, freedom and possibility. These accomplishments matter. The story must not dismiss them merely to manufacture dystopia.

Yet progress has changed the forms of dependency, power and struggle rather than abolishing them.

**Chapter One begins in a civilisation that can do extraordinary things but is becoming less certain what those things are for.**

This is a proposed dramatic premise, not yet settled historical fact.

## 1. What ordinary people experience

The opening should be playable before it is explained.

A newcomer enters Neptune Lounge and encounters hospitality, commerce, humour, labour, indulgence, social judgment, gossip, tenderness and friction. The place is neither a propaganda stage nor a miniature parliament. People are living their lives.

A bar shift pays a few credits. A drink costs something. A patron notices who works and who consumes. A rumour begins with one observer's interpretation of a stranger. Those are small events, but they demonstrate that **economic and narrative power operate even in apparently trivial encounters**.

The first chapters should make the player curious about this world before asking them to choose sides.

## 2. The question of power

Before naming a ruling body, the setting must distinguish at least six forms of influence:

| Domain | Questions to settle | How a player might encounter it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Material and economic | Who owns, allocates or restricts essential resources? | Prices, work, debts, access |
| Technical and infrastructural | Who understands and maintains life-supporting systems? | Breakdowns, permissions, expert testimony |
| Administrative and coercive | Who makes binding decisions and enforces them? | Rules, permits, policing, disputes |
| Intellectual and historical | Who controls archives, education and accepted accounts of the past? | Contradictory records, missing histories |
| Cultural and spiritual | Whose values shape belonging, dignity and aspiration? | Ritual, art, social exclusion, acts of care |
| Narrative and social | Who is believed, remembered, dismissed or feared? | Rumours, reputations, testimony |

These domains overlap. A single institution may possess several. Power need not have one obvious centre.

## 3. The Social Cycle is moving

The worker → warrior → intellectual → merchant cycle should be visible through institutions, incentives and daily experience, not through explanatory speeches.

One **hypothesis for authorial consideration** is that Triton Central has achieved great prosperity under a technologically sophisticated commercial order, while technical and intellectual authorities increasingly contest the meaning and distribution of that prosperity. But this is not yet canon: the present phase and direction of movement remain to be decided.

The important question is not which group is inherently virtuous. It is whether people can exercise responsible stewardship as the balance of collective power changes.

Sadvipra leadership may be present in uncelebrated places: someone who teaches a younger colleague, makes room for dissent, refuses an exploitative bargain, or steps aside so others can act. Its absence may be felt in institutions that reward compliance over judgment.

## 4. Established factions, unresolved political positions

The Red Book names the **Anamnesis Circle**, **Glitchbound**, **Woven Archive**, **Spiral Engineers** and **Divergents**. Their existing identities should be preserved while their current interests, resources, internal disagreements and relationships are developed.

The following are **questions, not new faction canon**:

- **Anamnesis Circle:** What practices of remembrance remain alive? Who regards them as necessary, obsolete or dangerous?
- **Glitchbound:** Which apparent irregularities are failures, discoveries or deliberate interventions?
- **Woven Archive:** What is recorded faithfully, what is contested, and who can access it?
- **Spiral Engineers:** Where does genuine technical service end and dependence on technical authority begin?
- **Divergents:** What forms of alignment do they resist, and what do their members disagree about?

Do not assume any faction is uniformly heroic, corrupt, spiritually enlightened or socially representative.

Do not yet canonise a Civic Authority, Commercial Syndicate, Senate, security apparatus or monopoly without further authorial decisions.

## 5. The capacities that have atrophied

Manzo's ubiquitous technology may make certain extraordinary feats commonplace while leaving their underlying human capacities unpractised.

Possible areas for exploration include direct perception, memory, navigation, embodied skill, interpersonal attention, collective deliberation, ecological knowledge and the capacity to tolerate uncertainty.

These are **candidate tensions**, not a settled list of lost powers. Distinguish what people *cannot* do from what they *have never needed to learn*, and from what they *could recover*.

Avoid the simplistic conclusion that technology is evil. Its gifts are real. The dramatic question is whether people remain capable of governing their relationship with those gifts.

## 6. The protagonists' predicament

The protagonists awaken separated, unable to remember who they are or how they arrived. They must find each other.

Their rediscovery is not merely a return to individual power. Each becomes capable through others, and each has something to offer the people they meet.

What the protagonists once did unconsciously may now require painstaking understanding. What Manzo's people regard as effortless technological routine may conceal extraordinary histories and forgotten faculties.

This makes **leadership that multiplies agency** a playable principle: progress can depend on helping another person act, not simply accumulating personal abilities.

## 7. Vitriol and the comet

Lord Vitriol acts overtly in Manzo in service of a dark cosmic entity, whose ultimate significance is more complex than the characters initially know.

He should not be the sole source of the civilisation's problems. He can exploit existing contradictions, institutions and fears, but these must have their own histories and motives.

The comet is approaching. The protagonists ultimately do more than dodge it: they **catch** it. Vitriol is overcome; Earth receives a new moon; an apparent catastrophe becomes a gift.

The physical mechanism, political consequences and precise sequence remain unresolved. Do not explain them away with unearned certainty.

## 8. Chapter One as lived experience

A possible progression for play, **not a mandatory questline**:

1. **Arrival:** A bewildered stranger in Neptune Lounge; the world seems socially familiar and cosmically strange.
2. **Belonging:** Work, exchange, hospitality, friction, friendship, minor embarrassment and gossip.
3. **Contradiction:** Two people interpret the same event differently; each has reasons.
4. **Connection:** Evidence of other displaced protagonists and capacities they cannot yet explain.
5. **Pressure:** A local problem exposes a larger dependency or institutional conflict.
6. **Choice:** A chance to exercise courage and enable someone else's agency without resolving the entire crisis.

The world should move independently of the protagonists. NPCs and factions have purposes beyond serving as quest dispensers.

## 9. Decisions for the next worldbuilding pass

These are the highest-value unanswered questions:

1. What is Triton Central's **present form of governance**, and where does legitimate authority actually reside?
2. Which Social Cycle tendency is **currently predominant**, and what evidence would an ordinary inhabitant recognise?
3. What does **material abundance** mean in practice? What remains scarce?
4. Which technology has most profoundly altered the relationship between **human capacity and dependency**?
5. What is the **first local conflict** that reveals the larger social situation without requiring an exposition dump?
6. What do different groups **know, suspect or deny** about the comet?
7. Which NPC can demonstrate **one step forward, two steps back** by enabling someone else to act?

## Boundaries

This document is a working Chapter One scaffold. It does not supersede the Red Book, implement a Social Cycle engine, assign fixed moral alignments, alter the current Neptune Lounge economy or settle the cosmic explanation of the comet.

Its next revision should answer these questions through specific people, institutions and scenes, while keeping ordinary life vivid and playable.
