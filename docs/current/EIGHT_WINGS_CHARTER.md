# Future Sky — Eight Wings of the Cosmic Golden Dragon

Version 1.1 — adopted 17 October 2026.
Development framework, explicitly adopted by Karun. This does not establish a literal eight-winged creature in world canon.

Future Sky is one living project carried by eight wings. Each wing has an enduring trajectory; feathers are bounded, executable contributions with evidence of completion.

## One body, coordinated flight

The body represents shared purpose, coordination and project state; it is not a ninth wing. Stewardship maintains this view. Existing constitutional direction remains in [Trajectory](TRAJECTORY_v2.2.md); ontology remains in [Architecture](ARCHITECTURE_v1.1.md); world canon remains in the [Red Book](../RedBook_V7.2.md).

Content-first development follows the [approved phase closure](PHASE_CLOSURE_EPISTEMIC_FOUNDATIONS_2026-10-15.md). The earlier conversational “Meaningful Arrival” suggestion is not an adopted replacement milestone: reconcile it with [First Night](NEPTUNE_LOUNGE_FIRST_NIGHT_v0.1.md) before changing priorities.

Choose a shared outcome when needed, then link contributions from the wings involved. All eight need not advance simultaneously. One feather at a time remains the default.

## Feather identity and counting

New wing feathers use W1-F1, W2-F1 … W8-F1. W8-F777 will always identify Stewardship's 777th allocated feather.

- Each feather has exactly one home wing; other affected wings are links, not duplicate counts.
- Allocate the next unused integer within that wing when a feather is accepted. Never reuse an allocated number.
- Count only completed and verified feathers. An active, abandoned or parked ID does not increase the completed count; the highest allocated ID may therefore differ from the count.
- Preserve existing project-wide Feather 1–55 references and any later legacy numbering. They are historical lineage, not retrospectively assigned wing counts.
- New counters start at adoption. Zero means no completed feather in the new register, not absence of historical work.
- A feather records outcome, boundary, dependencies, status, evidence and return checkpoint. Complete files and commits provide evidence; chat memory alone does not.
- Proposed → ready → active → review → complete. Blocked or parked work retains its context.
- Update the register in the same change that seals a feather. Main-branch and deployed-server availability are separate facts.

## Switching wings

Before switching, leave where we reached, links to results, settled decisions, uncertainties, dependencies and one candidate next step. Record the source revision and verification date.

On return: reset ritual → Knowledge Atlas → wing index → selected checkpoint → authoritative sources. Verify anything that may have changed. Surface the current wing and feather to Karun.

Do not require the author to reconstruct available history. Do not manufacture certainty when a checkpoint is missing. A wing checkpoint routes evidence; it does not override the Atlas authority rules.

This is development navigation, not a new world ontology or runtime subsystem.

## Shared checkpoints (“noding”) — adopted 18 October 2026

When a feather substantially changes another wing's knowledge or direction, leave a small shared checkpoint: what was learned or decided, affected wings and implications, the authoritative record, and what to carry forward on return. Keep the decision in one home record and link it from each affected wing checkpoint. One home feather and one count remain the rule; acknowledgement does not allocate another feather.

These are development knowledge checkpoints, distinct from in-world Thread Nodes. The first example is [W2 ↔ W6 — Cinematic storytelling](../wings/W2-F3_THE_OPENING_WE_CAN_INHABIT.md#shared-checkpoint--w2--w6-cinematic-storytelling).
