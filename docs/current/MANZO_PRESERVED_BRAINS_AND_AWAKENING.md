## Efiishent's preservation and return

Towards the end of his life, Efiishent placed his brain in stasis. It is preserved in a cube that he designed and manufactured himself.

He left instructions to be brought to life once a colony was established on Triton. His awakening there is the imaginative process now being developed, and an invitation to others to consider its ramifications.

Karun frames this as personal future-scenario planning, drawing on his understanding of P. R. Sarkar's statements about laboratory-grown human beings and the brain as the seat of mind. This records the author's stated inspiration and adopted fictional premise; it does not verify a quotation, establish current scientific feasibility or predict Karun's actual future.

## Birth, soul and samskaras

By Manzo, humans have mastered the technology for manufacturing human bodies and bringing human beings to life in them.

Ordinarily manufactured humans are individual souls continuing their journey and the unfolding of their samskaras. As in ordinary natural birth, they ordinarily have no access to memories of past lives.

Manufactured birth does not make a being soulless, a lesser person or an interchangeable copy.

## Preserved-brain humans — “old brainers”

Humans brought to life using an existing brain preserved through technology equivalent to Efiishent's retain their memories, skills and faculties.

Their bodies need to be akin to the body most recently inhabited for close continuity. They may nevertheless choose whatever type of body they wish to inhabit: greater bodily difference requires more effort to bring into **parallelism**. Similarity is therefore a relationship to the difficulty of embodiment, not an absolute prohibition on choosing a foreign body.

Within Manzo's in-world terminology, **“players” are those with brains being reborn**. This is a world-context meaning; it does not erase the existing distinction between a real human participant, a Character, a Role and a Driver.

Old brainers are a curiosity and still a new phenomenon, rather than the ordinary form of human birth.
