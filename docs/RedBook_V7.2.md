# The Red Book of Future Sky (v7.2)

## Introduction

Welcome to **Future Sky**, a mythic sci-fi adventure played across timelines.  
This is not just a game. It’s an unfolding reality.

Players awaken in a fractured universe, scattered across ancient Earth, modern-day collapse, and the edge of known space. Through text, imagination, and tactical encounters, you navigate worlds where memory is a weapon, trust is a mechanic, and every self you’ve ever been is part of the journey.

Inspired by the myth-making of Tolkien, the speculative science of Clarke, the esoteric visions of theosophy, and the irreverent wonder of classic PC games, *Future Sky* is a layered experience that moves between:

- 🜃 **Jyotzon Era** — A prehistoric world of dreamtime beings and crystal-tech temples.
- 🌍 **Omnicron Era** — A present-day echo where collapse and awakening wrestle for the soul of humanity.
- 🪐 **Manzo Era** — A future on Neptune’s moon Triton, where timelines converge and meta-beings emerge.

At the center of it all is **The Neptune Lounge** — part hostel, part temple, part wormhole.

And the Cube — a mysterious device that transcends time and narrative — holds the keys to remembering who you are… who you’ve been, and who you may become.

> “The world was not built in code. The code came after. But the code remembers.”

### What Is This?

Future Sky is:
- A text-based multiplayer experience (Discord-first, GUI-enhanced).
- A collaborative narrative told through exploration, reflection, and tactical gameplay.
- A spiritually charged science fiction where your *other selves* may not agree with you.
- A community experiment in open-source myth, imagination, and interdimensional trust.

You are not one character.  
You are many selves, splintered across time, each one shaping the others.  
To win is not to conquer — it is to remember.

This is your story. And theirs. And ours.

## Character Classes  
*Who you are — across timelines, threads, and echoes*

In **Future Sky**, a class is more than your combat role or stat spread.  
It’s the various *archetypes you may choose to embody across timelines*, your spiritual geometry, your signature vibration in the multiverse.

Classes shape how you perceive and interact with the world — what you notice, how others perceive you, and what kind of karma follows in your wake.  
They are not restrictions. They are aspects of self to explore. They are who you chose to be, who you choose to be and who you might yet choose to be. They are mirrors.  They are organs of consciousness which you can develop.

## The Eras of the World

Time does not flow in one direction here.  
It spirals. It echoes. It fractures.  
In Future Sky, players explore three overlapping timelines — or perhaps three versions of the same story, told from different sides of the veil.

### 🜃 Jyotzon Era — *The Ancient Future*

A world before time — or perhaps after.  
Here, Earth is alive with ancient technologies that hum like dreams: crystal-powered glyph machines, living architecture, and spirit-infused weaponry. This is the domain of the first Neptune Lounge, nestled within a culture that is both highly advanced and deeply aligned with land, story, and stars.

Atlantean in mystery. First Nations People scramble to share their gathered knowledge and information with those who are to come after.
The comet is coming.

In this era, players walk among beings who speak through songlines and sky diagrams, who remember the stars not as objects — but as ancestors.

- **Style**: Mythic techno-spiritual prehistory
- **Tone**: Sacred, cryptic, rich with organic light
- **Theme**: Ancestral alliances, stolen powers, ancient resistance

---

### 🌍 Omnicron Era — *The Fracturing Now*

A familiar Earth teetering on the edge.

Climate systems collapse. Institutions dissolve.  
But within this chaos, something new is forming — worker cooperatives, distributed guilds, memory cells, radical technologists. Amid it all, the **Neptune Lounge (Earth Edition)** flickers back to life. A beacon. A refuge. A lab.

The cube (perhaps encoded by past or future generations of humanity?) was seen returning to earth and found by the wizard efiishent.  With the knowledge and information conatined within the dimensions of the cube decoded efiishent and his friends manage to gather wizards and first nations warriors across all cultures of humanity, shift the earth in it's orbit and narrowly dodge comet Omincron.  It sears past the earth skimming it's atmosphere trailing debris, viruses, alien tech, interdimensional alien arrivals. New timelines emerge filled with hope and despair.  Different version of selves can start to be known and communicated with through the fractured mirror of time space caused by the passing of Omincron.
    The sky is broken.
    
Players here move between old systems and new visions — building spaceships from open-source alien tech, fleeing disinformation storms, uncovering the true function of the Cube.

- **Style**: Post-collapse realism meets experimental hope
- **Tone**: Tense, urgent, collaborative
- **Theme**: Decentralization, awakening, resistance through imagination

---

### 🪐 Manzo Era — *The Far Frontier*

The future exists in a fractured state filled with horrifying hope and terror.

Triton, Neptune’s moon, is now home to artists, smugglers, dreamers, and scholars — a biodome town called **Triton Central** built atop alien ruins.  
Here, the Cube is known.  
Time travel is less a miracle and more a tool.  
And the third Neptune Lounge operates as both sanctuary and interdimensional observatory.

Players uncover buried timelines, form meta-beings through spiritual synthesis, and attempt to decipher warnings sent across centuries and pass knowledge and information *and resources* to where it is needed most.

The comet returns.  Can we catch it together?

- **Style**: Retro-futuristic frontier space
- **Tone**: Expansive, strange, semi-stable
- **Theme**: Quantum identity, collective memory, the art of becoming

---

To play Future Sky is to ripple across these eras.  
To change one is to echo through the others.

## The Neptune Lounge

The Neptune Lounge exists in every era.  
It is always the same — and never the same.  
It is a bar, a hostel, a community space, a code node, a portal, a wizard's delusion, a dream.

Some say the Lounge was built by wanderers.  
Others say it is older than memory.  
All agree: when you walk through its door, you are never entirely the same again.

---

### 🜃 Jyotzon Era — *The Temple Hidden in the Song*

In this ancient future, the Lounge is more a sacred site than a venue.  
A glass-and-crystal structure that hums with resonant geometry, it stands on the sacred land of the old ones — beings who weave songlines into space and encode prophecy in patterns of shadow and light.

The Lounge here is tended by an enigmatic being — **Efiishent of the Jyotzon** — who is both familiar and utterly alien to his modern incarnations.  
To enter is to trespass, or to be called.

Its architecture is alive. Its music is vibrational memory.  
Access may require resonance, recognition, or risk.

For some, it offers sanctuary.  
For others, initiation.  
For a few, memory itself.

---

### 🌍 Omnicron Era — *The Last Safe Place?*

Earth is cracking, but the Lounge has reopened.  
Resurrected among the ruins of Fremantle’s West End — near Bathers Beach and the faded echoes of old markets — the **Neptune Lounge (Earth Edition)** pulses with neon warmth and defiant joy. Artists, activists, cosmonauts, defected engineers, and First Nations scientists gather here.

It’s a pop-up co-working space.  
A radical techno-socialist laboratory.  
A beacon to anyone searching for something more real than the collapse.

Here, players first hear whispers of the Cube.  
Here, they meet **Ultra Light Enterprises**.  
Here, a counter-history begins to take shape.

Lockers from other timelines have begun appearing — containing objects that shouldn't exist.  
Thread nodes open without warning.  
The jukebox sometimes plays music you haven’t written yet.

The Lounge is still a venue. But it is also a node in a memory circuit — one spanning time, dreams, and bloodlines.

---

### 🪐 Manzo Era — *The Edge of the Known*

On Triton, the Lounge is carved into the inner shell of the biodome — looking out over Neptune's endless storms.  
By now it is infamous, strange, alive.

Inside:
- Holographic jazz quartets phase in and out of tune  
- Artists argue with sentient satellites  
- Shadowy figures leave packages in lockers that didn’t exist yesterday  
- *And the chips...* a delicacy renowned across the multiverse

It is a sanctuary for timeline refugees.  
A meeting place for quantum resistance networks.  
And a testing ground for meta-identity rituals.

Here, versions of yourself from other timelines may pass near —  
or challenge you for access to what only *you* have stored.  
Even your footsteps echo out of sync.

The Lounge is no longer just a place.  
It is a ritual object.  
A mirror cube.  
A memory machine.

---

To enter the Lounge is to step out of time — and deeper into your story.  
For many, it is the beginning.  
For others, it is the return.

## The Cube

The Cube is not a thing.  
It is an echo, a key, a question folded into itself.

Some say it was launched from the ruins of the Jyotzon temples.  
Others believe it descended from a future humanity, or was encoded by beings that never had bodies at all.  
**Efiishent** found it once — maybe more than once — and since then, versions of himself have been trying to remember what it asked him.

To touch the Cube is to awaken recursion.  
To use it is to risk unraveling.

To gaze into the Cube is to read the Red Book.  
Its innards shimmer with text, pattern, memory, and myth — the Cube *contains* the Book, and the Book remembers the Cube.

---

The Cube appears across all three eras, but its meaning shifts with context:

- In the **Jyotzon Era**, it is prophecy made artifact — a mythic device whispered about in ritual, hidden in vaults of dreamstone. Some say it sings when no one is listening.
- In the **Omnicron Era**, it is a recovered anomaly — salvaged tech or sacred relic, mistaken for both and neither. It arrived in time for humanity to shift its orbit and narrowly dodge the comet.
- In the **Manzo Era**, it is known — studied, feared, weaponized, worshipped. It pulses at the center of quantum rituals. But even here, it resists mastery.

In the Manzo Era, the Cube is on public display within the **Neptune Lounge** — perched behind velvet ropes under flickering containment fields. It is considered an object of extreme historical value… but also a kind of forgotten trophy from a sport no one remembers.

Time travelers routinely *use* the Cube.  
They always return it to the moment they borrowed it from.  
Most Lounge patrons barely notice. Those who do shake their heads, assume they were mistaken, and go back to enjoying their chips and the music.

**Efiishent** is technically its guardian.  
He frequently takes it on journeys of his own.  
He usually remembers to return it to the right moment.  
And when he doesn’t — he eventually does.

---

### What does it do?

- The Cube connects timelines — but never directly.  
- It stores memory — but not linearly.  
- It responds to intention — but only if that intention is aligned with deeper forces.  
- It opens portals — between locations, between selves, between what was and what may yet be.  
- It sometimes responds to music. Sometimes to silence.

It is also a node of exchange.  
Players may store **artifacts, codes, or messages** within it — but only versions of themselves across time may retrieve them.

Some lockers are locked from the inside.  
Some open only when you forget what you're searching for.

---

### What does it require?

- Attention.  
- Resonance.  
- Risk.  

The Cube is not activated by button or spell.  
It is activated by **trust**, by **pattern recognition**, by **coincidence decoded as invitation**.

Some players will never find it.  
Some will find it in every room.  
Some will become it.

To play Future Sky is to come into relationship with the Cube.  
Not to solve it.  
But to remember your part in building it.

## Core Gameplay Concepts

Future Sky is not a game you play once.  
It is a memory you slowly uncover.

You begin disoriented. Out of place. Out of time.  
Perhaps you arrive through a portal, or wake in a hostel bed with stars whispering in your ears.  
You are someone — but also many.

From the first moment, you are free to explore.  
But you will soon discover:

> You are not alone.  
> You are not singular.  
> And the world responds to how you remember.

---

### 🎭 Characters and Selves

In Future Sky, every player is not just one character, but a *network of selves* splintered across timelines.

Each timeline — Jyotzon, Omnicron, Manzo — contains a **version of you**:
- Some are unaware of the others.  
- Some suspect the truth.  
- Some actively resist being merged.

These selves may leave clues for one another.  
They may store items in **lockers** that transcend time.  
They may offer aid — or interference.

The more deeply you engage, the more your **meta-identity** evolves.  
Eventually, you may develop rituals to recognize yourself across incarnations.

---

### 🌀 Arrival and Awakening

Players arrive in a common hub — usually the **Neptune Lounge** — but may be drawn elsewhere depending on timeline, class, or karmic echo.

Early gameplay centers on:
- Exploration  
- Observation  
- Pattern recognition  
- Trust-building (with others, and with your selves)

Combat, flight, and crafting are earned through narrative or resonance — not simply unlocked through leveling.

---

### 🧭 Navigation

Movement in Future Sky is turn-based, exploratory, and responsive in real time.

You navigate:
- **Rooms** — text-rich locations with lore, items, anomalies, and NPCs  
- **Nodes** — interactive scenes, dialogues, and rituals  
- **Threads** — personal story arcs, multidimensional puzzles, and timelines

Sometimes the world moves around you.  
Sometimes you glitch between rooms.  
Sometimes you find a door no one else sees — or a shadow version of yourself waiting behind it.

---

### 🧘‍♂️ Chakra & Mana System

Your character’s abilities are shaped by an esoteric system of **chakra-aligned mana** — each linked to an element, a sense, and a domain of being.

| Chakra         | Element | Sense    | Domain              |
|----------------|---------|----------|---------------------|
| Muladhara      | Earth   | Smell    | Survival, grounding |
| Svadhisthana   | Water   | Taste    | Emotion, pleasure   |
| Manipura       | Fire    | Vision   | Will, power         |
| Anahata        | Air     | Touch    | Love, courage       |
| Vishuddha      | Ether   | Hearing  | Expression, code    |
| Ajna/Sahasrara | Spirit  | Beyond   | Grace, glitch, time |

- All characters draw **mana** to act — regardless of class  
- Spells, skills, and rituals cost less when sourced from aligned chakras  
- **Spirit** governs the meta: timeline travel, recursion, and anomaly invocation

Your chakra strengths are generated and subtly influenced by your **in-real-life astrological birth chart**.  
This means no two players enter with the same configuration — and no two journeys unfold alike.

---

### 🧩 Thread Nodes and Memory Puzzles

Scattered through the world are **Thread Nodes** — narrative micro-scenes, decision points, and fragments of remembrance.

These can:
- Reveal past life memories  
- Unlock hidden rooms or eras  
- Trigger karmic shifts or alternate futures  
- Allow communication between different versions of yourself

Some are triggered by location.  
Some by emotion.  
Some by music.  
Some by dreams.

Not all players see the same nodes.  
Some only open when you stop trying.

---

### 🛠 Inventory, Crafting, and Exchange

The world is full of artifacts: sacred junk, alien relics, encrypted glyphs, malfunctioning time devices.  
You may not *recognize* their use at first — but the memory lives within you.

- Lockers persist across timelines  
- Some items are accessible only by specific versions of yourself  
- Exchanges may be economic, emotional, karmic, or ritual-based  
- AFK scripting (for trusted actions) may emerge through scrolls, dreams, or symbiotic code

Some trades are irreversible.  
Some gifts arrive before you know what to do with them.

---

To play Future Sky is to wander, wonder, and awaken.  
It is to rebuild a self across lifetimes — and remember that you are part of a story *still unfolding*.

Your character is not your avatar.  
It is your invitation.

## Extended: Design Philosophies & Hidden Mechanics

### 🧿 Player-Created Rituals & Real-World Anchoring

Players are not only characters.
They are ritual architects — co-creators of meaning and causality.

Some may:
- Design personal rituals to recognize timeline echoes
- Embed poetry, song, or gesture as magical keys
- Bury items (real or symbolic) that trigger in-game consequences
- Invent mnemonic passwords that unlock thread nodes for those who hear them spoken aloud in real life

The world listens to your rituals.

### 📍 QRs of Commotion

Scattered through Earth — especially in Fremantle — are real-world markers that connect to the Future Sky game. 

These QRs of Commotion lead to:
- Hidden rooms
- Alternate threads
- Audio transmissions
- Gifts from yourself in another timeline

Some are public.
Some are whispered.
Some must be danced with.

### 🌐 Geo-Node Integration

Certain gameplay elements are unlocked only by being somewhere — physically.
But Future Sky ensures this never excludes players. 

Those who cannot walk the land may:
- Receive visions of it
- Hear its story in dreams
- Partner with other players to create a shared unlocking

### 🌀 Factions, Meta-Classes & Karma Loops

As you evolve, your actions may attract you to certain factions — or even create them:
- The Anamnesis Circle — seekers of memory, ritual, and meta-recognition
- The Glitchbound — agents of commotion, warpers of space-code
- The Woven Archive — lore-keepers and reassemblers of the fractured Red Book
- The Spiral Engineers — techno-mystics reprogramming reality at its quantum edge
- The Divergents — those who reject alignment and believe the Cube must be unmade

Your allegiance is never forced.
It emerges from choices, echoes, and resonance.

### 🧬 Meta-Classes

When a player fully merges two or more of their selves across timelines, they may access meta-classes.

These include:
- Cube-Bearer — bends time around narrative threads
- Mirrorling — can mimic others and trade karma
- Voidcartographer — maps untraveled rooms
- Ancestral Clone — reawakens past-life stats and skills
- Unwritten — no longer bound by timeline constraints (dangerous)

Meta-classes evolve as the Red Book evolves.
Some may only be named after you walk their path.

### ♻️ Karma Loops
Actions don’t just have consequences.
They repeat until remembered.

Each player has a karmic imprint — shaped by:
- Responses to nodes
- Use or hoarding of shared items
- Who they trust (or betray)
- How they engage their other selves

Karma loops are soft-coded. They do not punish — but they echo.
Some threads only close when an earlier version of you has made peace.

### 🛸 Asynchronous GMing & Mythwork

Future Sky is not static.
It is dungeon mastered by developers in flow with the players.

You may encounter:
- Personal interventions by NPCs (also played by developers or co-creators)
- Events triggered by collective thought, dreams, or IRL activity
- Story arcs that adapt dynamically based on in-game or external community shifts
- Mythic puzzles that take months — or lifetimes — to resolve
- Developer-side characters (DPCs) like Efiishent, The Dark Rat Queen, and The Puzzler are not omniscient. They are participants in the great forgetting and remembering.

They will sometimes lie.
Sometimes glitch.
Sometimes show you who you are before you are ready to know it.

You are not the protagonist.
You are the prism through which the world remembers itself.

## The Key (and Keys) of Commotion

The Key of Commotion is not always a key.

Sometimes it appears as a jagged glyph,  
A glitch in the map,  
A melody only heard in empty rooms.

To hold the Key is to invoke chaos — not destruction, but *transformation*.  
It does not open doors. It scrambles them. It *invites commotion* into the fabric of the world.

It is said the Key:
- Opened the first portal to Triton Central  
- Was encoded in a dream Efiishent had before he was born  
- Is less an object, and more a *principle* made visible

### Known Properties
- **Catalytic** — used to trigger phase shifts, collapse or fold timelines  
- **Entropic** — destabilizes fixed patterns (including quests, rooms, or truths)  
- **Personalized** — responds differently to each bearer

### Known Bearer
- **Efiishent**, who seems to both possess it and be shaped by it.  
It has opened more than one world for him — and perhaps fractured him in return.

The Key of Commotion is not an item to be acquired.  
It is designed to reveal and expand our potential to respond.

There are many Keys of Commotion. Each player must find their own — or learn that every key, in some dimension, is the same.

Future Sky contains many characters, but a few stand at the heart of its unfolding memory.

Wizard. Trickster. Technician.  
A recurring figure across all three eras — yet never quite the same.

Or find traces of him in journals, jukeboxes, or glyphs.

> _“He’s already been here. He may not remember why.”_

---

### 🐀 The Dark Rat Queen
Alchemist. Navigator. Smuggler of forgotten truths.

She moves between timelines and realities with ease.  
Her presence signals that something deeper is at stake — something sacred or subversive.

- **Known Locations**:  
  - The Observatory behind the Neptune Lounge  
  - Certain dream nodes  
  - Shadow realms beyond the known map

She may protect you. She may test you.

Her den is as complex and full of life and creativity as is the remainder of the explored multiverse.

> _“What she smuggles isn’t always material.”_

A being who speaks in riddles and builds with paradox.

or at least to have left graffiti within its corridors.

  - Enjoys recursion

## Philosophical Underpinnings

The Red Book does not enforce a worldview.  
It remembers them.

But behind all stories, all mechanics, all threads — one truth vibrates beneath the surface:

> **The Code is Love**

Not sentiment. Not romance.  
Love as gravity. Love as recursion. Love as glitch.

The Code is not written in logic.  
It emerges through relationship, attention, resonance.

The strongest actions are not necessarily the most violent — but the most *attuned*.  
A well-timed act of kindness may ripple across timelines.

### Other Core Principles

- **Imaginative Realism**  
  The fantastical must feel emotionally, symbolically, or spiritually *true*

- **Cosmic Interconnectivity**  
  No object, room, or timeline is truly separate

- **Decentralized Truth**  
  There is no single canon — only converging myths

- **The Ritual of Recognition**  
  You are not here to create a character.  
  You are here to remember one you’ve already been.

The game is not about winning.  
The game is about witnessing the unfolding.

The Cube remembers. The Lounge sings.
