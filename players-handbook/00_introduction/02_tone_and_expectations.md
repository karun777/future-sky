# Tone & Expectations

Future Sky is a game that asks for **attention, patience, and presence**.

It is not difficult in the traditional sense.
It is demanding in a different way.

This section exists to align expectations — not to limit play, but to ensure that players and facilitators are engaging with the world on the same wavelength.

---

## A Quiet Intensity

Much of Future Sky unfolds slowly.

Moments of beauty, tension, humour, and strangeness may arrive without announcement.
Not every room contains an objective.
Not every interaction leads somewhere obvious.

Silence, ambiguity, and waiting are part of the experience.

This does not mean nothing is happening.
It means the world is not rushing you.

---

## When Time Matters — and When It Doesn’t

Most of the time, Future Sky operates asynchronously.

You may:
- Enter and leave the world at your own pace
- Reflect before acting
- Return later to find subtle changes

However, **there are moments when time tightens**.

During certain events:
- Delay may have consequences
- Missed actions may resolve without you
- Collective coordination may matter

These moments are rare, intentional, and clearly signalled.

They are meant to feel different.

---

## Consequence Without Punishment

Future Sky does not aim to punish players.

But it does not shield them from consequence.

Actions may result in:
- Lost opportunities
- Altered relationships
- Permanent changes to places or people
- Death, memory loss, or transformation

Failure is not a dead end.
It is often a fork.

Retreat, adaptation, and reinterpretation are valid responses.

---

## Not All Answers Are Immediate

Some questions in Future Sky:
- Take time to resolve
- May never fully resolve
- Exist to be lived with rather than solved

Mystery is not a puzzle with a guaranteed solution.
It is a condition of the world.

Players who seek certainty above all else may feel frustrated.
Players who tolerate uncertainty will often find deeper resonance.

---

## Engagement Over Optimisation

Future Sky is not balanced around perfect play.

Optimisation is allowed.
Min-maxing is possible.
Neither is the point.

The system rewards:
- Curiosity
- Observation
- Creative risk
- Emotional intelligence
- Paying attention to patterns

Sometimes the “best” mechanical choice is the least interesting narrative one.
Future Sky will not always protect you from that trade-off.

---

## Safety, Boundaries, and Care

Future Sky deals with:
- Identity
- Memory
- Loss
- Power
- Collapse
- Transformation

These themes can land differently for different players.

It is expected that:
- Groups communicate boundaries
- Players can step back when needed
- Facilitators prioritise care over spectacle

Intensity should never come at the cost of consent.

---

## The Social Contract

By entering Future Sky, players agree to a simple pact:

- To engage with the world honestly
- To accept uncertainty
- To respect the experience of others
- To allow the story to change them, at least a little

The game will meet you halfway.

Sometimes more than halfway.
Sometimes not at all.

That, too, is part of the tone.

---

## A Final Note

Future Sky is not trying to be everything.

It is trying to be **coherent**.

If you find yourself confused, unsettled, curious, or reflective —
you are probably in the right place.
