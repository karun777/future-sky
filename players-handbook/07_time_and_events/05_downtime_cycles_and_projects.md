# Downtime Cycles and Projects

Not all progress happens under pressure.

Between moments of urgency lie stretches of relative calm — periods where characters recover, reflect, build, train, and prepare. Downtime cycles formalise these stretches and give them mechanical and narrative weight.

This chapter explains how downtime works, how long-term projects function, and why what you do between crises matters.

---

## What Is Downtime?

Downtime is a phase of play where:
- Immediate threats are absent or reduced
- Time is abstracted
- Characters act asynchronously
- Long-term intentions take precedence

Downtime is not inactivity.
It is redirected focus.

---

## Downtime Cycles

Downtime occurs in cycles.

A cycle may represent:
- Days
- Weeks
- Months
- Longer spans between major events

The length of a cycle depends on:
- Campaign pace
- World state
- Character location
- Narrative context

Cycles provide rhythm.

---

## Downtime Actions

During a downtime cycle, characters may:
- Recover physically and mentally
- Train skills or abilities
- Work on long-term projects
- Gather information
- Tend relationships
- Manage resources
- Reflect or process events

Actions taken during downtime still carry consequence.

---

## Projects as Structured Intent

Projects represent sustained effort toward a goal.

A project might include:
- Building or modifying something
- Researching a mystery
- Cultivating influence
- Developing a technique
- Establishing a base or abode
- Healing from trauma
- Teaching or mentoring others

Projects take time.
They may require resources, access, or cooperation.

---

## Project Progress

Progress is tracked in stages rather than turns.

Each stage may require:
- Successful effort
- Time investment
- Overcoming obstacles
- Responding to complications

Interruptions can stall or reshape a project.

---

## Complications and Drift

Downtime is not risk-free.

Complications may arise due to:
- World events
- Faction interference
- Resource shortages
- Internal conflict
- Unresolved strain

Ignoring complications allows drift.

---

## Asynchronous Participation

Downtime supports asynchronous play.

Characters may:
- Enter downtime at different moments
- Complete cycles independently
- Return to shared scenes with changed states

The world synchronises around results, not schedules.

---

## Rest, Recovery, and Integration

Downtime is when:
- Strain dissipates
- Chakra balance is restored
- Memories integrate
- Identity stabilises

Skipping downtime has long-term cost.

---

## Projects and World Impact

Completed projects may:
- Alter environments
- Shift faction dynamics
- Unlock new options
- Create lasting structures
- Generate new threads

Projects change the world quietly.

---

## Ending a Downtime Cycle

A downtime cycle ends when:
- A pressure window opens
- A project reaches resolution
- An external event interrupts
- Characters choose to re-engage

Transitions matter.

---

## Why This Matters

This system ensures that:
- Growth feels lived-in
- Long-term goals matter
- Async play remains meaningful
- Characters have interior lives
- The world evolves between crises

What you do in the quiet shapes what you survive later.

---

## A Final Orientation

Do not ask:
- “What should I grind?”

Ask instead:
- “What do I want to become ready for?”
- “What am I building toward?”
- “What have I not processed yet?”

Downtime is where intention becomes structure.
