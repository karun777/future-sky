# Travel, Time, Distance, and Logistics

Travel in Future Sky is not a cutscene.

Distance represents time, exposure, preparation, and opportunity cost. This chapter explains how travel is handled across scales, how logistics apply pressure, and why journeys matter even when nothing dramatic happens along the way.

---

## Distance as Commitment

Distance is not just space.

Traveling far commits you to:
- Time passing elsewhere
- Resource expenditure
- Exposure to risk
- Reduced responsiveness to other events
- Possible missed opportunities

Choosing to go somewhere means choosing not to be elsewhere.

---

## Scales of Travel

Travel operates at multiple scales.

- **Local travel** — Within a zone or cluster  
- **Regional travel** — Between settlements or regions  
- **Long-range travel** — Across worlds, eras, or systems  

As scale increases:
- Abstraction increases
- Logistics become more important
- Preparation matters more than precision

---

## Soft Travel

Soft travel applies when:
- Routes are known
- Danger is low
- Time pressure is minimal

Soft travel:
- Advances time
- May trigger ambient effects
- Consumes resources
- Allows reflection or downtime

Details emerge only if attention is drawn.

---

## Hard Travel

Hard travel applies when:
- Routes are dangerous or unknown
- Time pressure exists
- Pursuit or surveillance is active
- Conditions are unstable

Hard travel introduces:
- Structured choices
- Risk checks
- Environmental hazards
- Opportunity for discovery or loss

Hard travel makes the journey part of the story.

---

## Logistics and Load

Logistics include:
- Food and water
- Power or fuel
- Equipment condition
- Medical or survival supplies
- Information and access

Running out rarely kills immediately.
It creates compounding problems.

---

## Preparation and Route Choice

Preparation may include:
- Securing transport
- Gathering intelligence
- Lightening load
- Establishing contacts
- Timing departure

Route choice is often a strategic decision.

---

## Interruptions and Diversions

Journeys rarely go exactly as planned.

Interruptions may:
- Create new threads
- Delay arrival
- Reveal hidden locations
- Force difficult decisions
- Alter destination entirely

Deviation is not failure.

---

## Travel and the Wider World

While you travel:
- Events continue elsewhere
- Factions act
- Pressure windows may open or close
- Resources shift

Travel trades immediacy for reach.

---

## Arrival and Aftermath

Arrival is not neutral.

After travel:
- Characters may be fatigued or strained
- Gear may require attention
- Information may be outdated
- Situations may have evolved

Arrival resets context, not consequence.

---

## Why This Matters

This approach ensures that:
- Travel feels consequential
- Distance matters without bogging play down
- Logistics create meaningful choices
- Exploration remains alive
- The world continues beyond the party

Journeys shape stories as much as destinations.

---

## A Final Orientation

Do not ask:
- “How long will this take?”

Ask instead:
- “What changes while we’re gone?”
- “What are we prepared to lose?”
- “What might we find along the way?”

Distance always costs something.
