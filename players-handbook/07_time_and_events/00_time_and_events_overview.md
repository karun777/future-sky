# Time and Events

Time in Future Sky is not uniform.

It stretches, compresses, pauses, and surges depending on pressure, attention, and consequence. Events do not simply occur — they emerge from conditions, actions, and accumulated momentum.

This section explains how time flows, how events are triggered, and how the world continues to move even when characters are not actively acting.

---

## Time as a Living Medium

Time is not just a clock.

It is shaped by:
- Urgency
- Danger
- Focus
- Collective action
- Narrative weight

Some moments demand precision.
Others allow drift.

---

## Asynchronous Play

Future Sky supports asynchronous play.

Characters may:
- Act at different times
- Be absent while the world continues
- Return to changed conditions
- Miss moments without missing meaning

The world does not wait — but it does adapt.

---

## Events as Responses

Events are not random interruptions.

They arise from:
- Environmental pressure
- Player actions
- Unresolved tensions
- Faction agendas
- Temporal instability

An event is the world responding.

---

## Background Motion

Even when players are idle:
- Factions act
- Resources shift
- Environments degrade or recover
- Information spreads
- Tensions escalate or cool

Stillness is temporary.

---

## Pressure and Urgency

Not all time carries equal weight.

Pressure increases when:
- Stakes rise
- Deadlines approach
- Resources dwindle
- Surveillance tightens
- Consequences compound

Pressure changes how actions resolve.

---

## Shared and Individual Time

Time operates at multiple layers:
- Personal (individual recovery, projects, reflection)
- Local (zones, communities, factions)
- Global (world events, era shifts)
- Temporal (echoes across timelines)

These layers interact.

---

## Events Without Players

Some events occur regardless of player presence.

These may:
- Change access
- Alter relationships
- Close opportunities
- Create new threats
- Reshape the world

Absence is itself a choice.

---

## Reading the World’s Tempo

The world communicates its tempo through:
- Environmental cues
- NPC behaviour
- Shifts in opportunity
- Repeated signals
- Escalating cost

Learning to read tempo is a form of mastery.

---

## Why This Matters

This approach ensures that:
- Time feels alive
- The world remains coherent
- Urgency emerges naturally
- Asynchronous play feels fair
- Consequences are persistent

The story does not pause between sessions.

---

## A Final Orientation

Do not ask:
- “How long do we have?”

Ask instead:
- “What is accelerating?”
- “What is about to break?”
- “What happens if we wait?”

Time is already moving.
