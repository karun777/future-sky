# Skill Lists and Categories

Skills in Future Sky are organised to be familiar, flexible, and future-proof.

This chapter explains how skills are grouped, how to read a skill list, and why categories matter more than exhaustive enumeration.

---

## Why Categories Matter

Rather than presenting a single, ever-expanding list, skills are grouped into categories.

Categories:
- Provide conceptual clarity
- Prevent list bloat
- Support multiple eras and cultures
- Allow new skills to be introduced cleanly
- Help players understand *what kind* of capability they are invoking

A category tells you *how* a skill operates in the world.

---

## Core Skill Categories

All skills belong to one of the following broad categories.

These categories remain consistent across eras, even as specific skills evolve.

---

### Physical Skills

Physical skills govern bodily action, coordination, and endurance.

They include:
- Athletics and movement
- Combat techniques
- Manual labour and exertion
- Stealth and agility
- Physical resilience

Physical skills are most affected by:
- Strength, Dexterity, and Constitution
- Fatigue and injury
- Equipment and terrain

---

### Technical Skills

Technical skills govern tools, systems, and constructed environments.

They include:
- Engineering and repair
- Hacking and interfaces
- Crafting and fabrication
- Vehicles and machinery
- Analysis of artificial systems

Technical skills depend heavily on:
- Tools and access
- Knowledge and preparation
- Time and safety

---

### Cognitive Skills

Cognitive skills govern reasoning, memory, and perception.

They include:
- Investigation and deduction
- Strategy and planning
- Pattern recognition
- Academic or esoteric knowledge
- Situational awareness

Cognitive skills are sensitive to:
- Information quality
- Stress and distraction
- Time pressure

---

### Social Skills

Social skills govern interaction with others.

They include:
- Persuasion and negotiation
- Deception and performance
- Leadership and coordination
- Empathy and insight
- Reputation management

Social skills are shaped by:
- Context and culture
- Relationship history
- Stakes and power dynamics

---

### Survival and Environmental Skills

These skills govern interaction with the world outside controlled spaces.

They include:
- Navigation and orientation
- Tracking and concealment
- Foraging and resource use
- Environmental awareness
- Hazard mitigation

These skills change dramatically across eras, but the category remains stable.

---

### Mystical and Resonant Skills

These skills govern interaction with non-ordinary forces.

They include:
- Chakra-based abilities
- Energetic manipulation
- Symbolic or ritual action
- Perception beyond the mundane
- Reality-adjacent phenomena

These skills are tightly linked to:
- Chakra alignment
- Mana availability
- Resonance and strain
- Narrative consequence

---

## Broad Skills, Specific Expression

Skills are intentionally broad.

For example:
- “Athletics” may cover climbing, swimming, or leaping
- “Engineering” may cover repair, construction, or sabotage
- “Influence” may cover persuasion, intimidation, or inspiration

Specific expression is determined by context.

---

## Era-Specific Skill Expression

The same skill may look different across eras.

For example:
- Navigation may involve stars, maps, satellites, or intuition
- Engineering may involve stone, circuitry, biotech, or mythic devices
- Influence may operate through ritual, media, or networks

The category stays the same.
The expression evolves.

---

## Adding New Skills

New skills may be introduced when:
- A domain becomes central to play
- A culture or era demands specificity
- A character invests deeply in a niche

New skills should:
- Fit a category
- Have clear narrative purpose
- Avoid redundancy
- Be teachable and learnable

---

## What Skill Ratings Mean

A skill rating represents:
- Reliability under pressure
- Familiarity with common scenarios
- Ability to adapt within that domain

It does not represent perfection.

High skill reduces risk.
It does not remove it.

---

## Reading a Skill Entry

Each skill entry will describe:
- What the skill broadly covers
- Typical applications
- Common risks or costs
- How mastery changes use
- Which abilities or chakras commonly apply

You do not need exhaustive lists to play effectively.

---

## A Final Orientation

Do not ask:
- “Is there a skill for this exact thing?”

Ask instead:
- “Which domain does this fall under?”
- “What am I actually trying to do?”
- “What matters most in this situation?”

Skills exist to support action — not constrain imagination.
