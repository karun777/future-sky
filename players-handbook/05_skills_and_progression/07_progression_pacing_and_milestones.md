# Progression Pacing and Milestones

Growth in Future Sky is deliberate.

It is not measured by how many encounters you survive or how much experience you accumulate in a short time. It is measured by how your relationship to the world changes.

This chapter explains how progression is paced, why milestones matter, and how long-term play remains coherent and meaningful.

---

## Why Pacing Matters

Progression that is too fast:
- Flattens consequence
- Encourages optimisation over judgement
- Reduces narrative weight
- Burns out both characters and campaigns

Progression that is too slow:
- Feels stagnant
- Discourages experimentation
- Undermines investment

Pacing is a design choice, not a byproduct.

---

## Milestones Over Grinding

Future Sky favours milestones over accumulation.

Milestones represent:
- Narrative turning points
- Meaningful achievement
- Survival through sustained pressure
- Completion of long-term objectives
- Integration of hard-earned experience

A milestone marks change.
It is recognised by the world.

---

## Types of Milestones

Milestones may occur through different modes of play.

Examples include:
- Resolving a major conflict
- Surviving a severe loss
- Completing a long-term project
- Achieving mastery in a domain
- Crossing an era boundary
- Accepting a permanent consequence

Not all milestones are triumphant.

---

## What Milestones Unlock

Reaching a milestone may unlock:
- Skill advancement
- Ability growth
- Chakra integration
- New talents or signatures
- Reduced strain costs
- Era-specific capabilities
- Narrative authority

Unlocks should feel proportionate to what was endured.

---

## Short-Term and Long-Term Growth

Progression operates on multiple timescales.

- **Short-term growth** reflects adaptation and learning  
- **Long-term growth** reflects transformation  

Short-term gains are flexible.
Long-term changes reshape identity.

Both matter.

---

## Player Agency in Growth

Players influence pacing through choice.

Choices that affect pacing include:
- Seeking risk or avoiding it
- Investing in training or action
- Committing to long-term projects
- Accepting or resisting change
- Engaging deeply with consequences

The system responds to how you play.

---

## Diminishing Returns and Saturation

Growth slows as mastery increases.

At higher levels:
- Improvement requires greater effort
- Stakes increase
- Mistakes carry heavier cost
- Change becomes more selective

This prevents runaway escalation and preserves tension.

---

## Setbacks as Part of the Curve

Not all progression is upward.

Setbacks may:
- Delay growth
- Redirect focus
- Force reassessment
- Introduce new paths

Setbacks are not failures.
They are part of the curve.

---

## Campaign Rhythm

A healthy campaign includes:
- Periods of intensity
- Periods of consolidation
- Time for recovery and reflection
- Space for characters to change direction

Constant escalation erodes meaning.

---

## Why This Matters

This pacing ensures that:
- Growth feels earned
- Power remains contextual
- Characters stay recognisable
- Long-term play remains compelling
- The system respects time and investment

You do not rush becoming.

---

## A Final Orientation

Do not ask:
- “How fast should we level?”

Ask instead:
- “What have we survived?”
- “What has changed because of us?”
- “What can we now carry that we couldn’t before?”

Progression is not a race.

It is a record of becoming.
