# Talents, Perks, and Signature Capabilities

Not all growth is incremental.

Some moments mark a clear shift — a new way of acting, a recognisable style, a capability that sets a character apart. Talents, perks, and signature capabilities represent these inflection points.

This chapter explains how distinctive abilities emerge without turning characters into collections of disconnected bonuses.

---

## What Talents Represent

A talent represents a learned pattern of action.

It reflects:
- A breakthrough in understanding
- A refined technique
- A repeated response under pressure
- A way of solving problems that has become instinctive

Talents change *how* you act, not just how well.

---

## Perks as Passive Advantages

Perks represent persistent advantages.

They may:
- Reduce costs
- Expand options
- Alter default assumptions
- Modify outcomes subtly but consistently

Perks rarely define a character on their own.
They support a broader style of play.

---

## Signature Capabilities

Signature capabilities are defining expressions of mastery.

They often:
- Combine multiple skills or chakras
- Represent a class-defining move
- Alter the flow of a scene
- Carry narrative weight
- Signal reputation or recognition

A signature capability says something about *who you are*.

---

## How Talents Are Gained

Talents are not selected from a universal menu.

They are gained through:
- Sustained use of a skill
- Deliberate training or experimentation
- Narrative milestones
- Overcoming significant challenge
- Instruction from a mentor or tradition

Talents should feel earned.

---

## Limits on Accumulation

Talents and perks are intentionally limited.

A character can only meaningfully integrate a small number at any given time. Accumulating too many:
- Dilutes identity
- Increases cognitive load
- Weakens narrative clarity

Depth is favoured over hoarding.

---

## Synergy and Conflict

Some talents reinforce each other.
Others introduce tension.

Synergy:
- Reduces cost
- Improves reliability
- Enables creative combinations

Conflict:
- Increases strain
- Forces prioritisation
- Creates hard choices

Both are valuable.

---

## Signature Moments

Signature capabilities often emerge from signature moments.

These moments may involve:
- Risk taken willingly
- Consequence endured
- A line crossed
- A limit surpassed
- A truth accepted

The system recognises moments that change how a character acts in the world.

---

## Evolution and Replacement

Talents may evolve.

Over time, a character may:
- Refine a talent
- Replace an old pattern
- Let a capability fade
- Integrate multiple techniques into one expression

Growth is not additive forever.

---

## Visibility and Reputation

Some talents are visible.

The world may:
- Recognise a style
- Attribute outcomes to you
- React differently once your capabilities are known

Reputation is a form of mechanical effect.

---

## Why This Matters

This approach ensures that:
- Characters feel distinct
- Progression produces playstyle change
- Optimisation does not eclipse identity
- Abilities emerge from experience
- Power has narrative texture

You are not collecting abilities.

You are developing a way of acting.

---

## A Final Orientation

Do not ask:
- “What perk should I take?”

Ask instead:
- “What do I keep doing?”
- “What pattern is forming?”
- “What would the world recognise me for?”

Talents reveal who you are becoming.
