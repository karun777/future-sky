# Ability Growth and Training

In Future Sky, ability scores are not static.

They represent lived capacity — physical, cognitive, emotional, and perceptual — and like all living systems, they can grow, adapt, and degrade over time.

This chapter explains how ability scores change, why growth is slow and meaningful, and how training differs from simple accumulation.

---

## What Ability Scores Represent

Ability scores describe *capacity*, not skill.

They reflect:
- Physical conditioning and resilience
- Cognitive flexibility and focus
- Emotional regulation and presence
- Perceptual acuity
- Willingness to endure strain

Abilities shape how easily skills can be applied under pressure.

---

## Abilities Are Not Fixed

Characters are not frozen at creation.

Over time, abilities may:
- Improve through sustained effort
- Adapt to repeated stress
- Recover from limitation
- Decline through neglect or trauma
- Change due to transformative events

Ability growth is slow by design.

---

## Growth Through Use

Abilities improve when they are:
- Consistently engaged
- Tested under meaningful pressure
- Supported by rest and recovery
- Reinforced through deliberate training

Occasional use is insufficient.
Habitual engagement matters.

---

## Training Ability Scores

Training an ability requires:
- Time
- Focus
- Repetition
- Appropriate conditions
- Often guidance or structure

Examples include:
- Physical conditioning and endurance work
- Cognitive drills or study disciplines
- Emotional regulation practices
- Sensory refinement exercises
- Will-building through sustained effort

Training improves *capacity*, not technique.

---

## Ability Growth Versus Skill Growth

Ability growth and skill growth are related but distinct.

- **Skills** improve how well you do something specific  
- **Abilities** improve how much strain you can carry while doing it  

A skilled character with weak capacity burns out.
A capable character with low skill acts inefficiently.

Balance matters.

---

## Thresholds and Plateaus

Ability growth is nonlinear.

Characters may experience:
- Long periods of slow change
- Sudden breakthroughs
- Plateaus requiring new approaches
- Setbacks due to injury or exhaustion

Plateaus are signals, not failures.

---

## Costs and Trade-offs

Improving one ability may:
- Require neglecting others
- Increase expectations and pressure
- Change how the world treats you
- Introduce new vulnerabilities

Growth reshapes identity.

---

## Degradation and Injury

Abilities can be temporarily or permanently reduced.

This may occur through:
- Severe injury
- Chronic strain
- Psychological trauma
- Environmental exposure
- Prolonged neglect

Recovery is possible — but not always complete.

---

## Era and Context

Ability expression changes across eras.

The same score may represent:
- Raw physical strength
- Augmented capability
- Bio-engineered resilience
- Ritual conditioning
- Mythic endurance

The number stays consistent.
The story changes.

---

## Why This Matters

This approach ensures that:
- Characters feel alive and adaptive
- Training choices matter long-term
- Capacity becomes a strategic concern
- Growth is earned, not assumed
- Decline is meaningful when it occurs

Abilities tell the story of what your body and mind have endured.

---

## A Final Orientation

Do not ask:
- “What should I raise next?”

Ask instead:
- “What am I training for?”
- “What kind of strain can I now carry?”
- “What does my body remember?”

Ability growth is not optimisation.

It is adaptation.
