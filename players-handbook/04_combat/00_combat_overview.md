# Combat — Overview

Combat in Future Sky is not the centre of the game.

But when it happens, it is real.

Combat exists as one expression of conflict — a pressure state where time tightens, intent collides, and consequence sharpens. The goal of these rules is not to turn violence into a mini-game. The goal is to make conflict legible, meaningful, and survivable without flattening it into numbers.

This section explains how combat works in practice: the loop, the flow of turns, attacks and defence, positioning, conditions, group tactics, retreat, and what happens after the dust settles.

---

## What Combat Is For

Combat is for moments when:
- Goals become incompatible
- The environment becomes hostile
- Negotiation fails or cannot occur
- A threat must be contained quickly
- Escape is contested

Combat is not required for progression.
Combat is a tool — sometimes necessary, sometimes catastrophic.

---

## Combat Is Context-Driven

In Future Sky, combat is shaped by context.

The outcome of a fight is influenced heavily by:
- Preparation
- Terrain and environment
- Surprise and awareness
- Resources and strain
- Coordination with allies
- Alignment and resonance
- Whether you know when to leave

If you ignore context, the dice will not save you.

---

## The Core Combat Promise

Combat rules must satisfy three promises:

1. **Clarity**  
   You always understand what is happening and what your options are.

2. **Consequences**  
   Combat leaves residue — physical, social, and systemic.

3. **Creativity**  
   Combat is not limited to “attack again.” Players can disrupt systems, use the environment, change objectives, and win without annihilation.

---

## Combat Has Objectives

Not every fight is “win or die.”

Many combats are better understood as:
- Escape
- Delay
- Capture
- Protect
- Disable
- End the threat long enough to move

Choosing an objective is often more important than choosing a weapon.

---

## Violence Draws Attention

Violence is rarely invisible.

After combat, expect:
- Noise and witnesses
- Escalation or retaliation
- Changed reputation
- New obligations
- Resource depletion

Winning a fight can still create a problem.

---

## Retreat Is a Skill

Disengaging is not failure.

Retreat is:
- A tactical choice
- A narrative choice
- A survival choice

Many characters live long enough to become legends because they mastered retreat.

## A Final Orientation

Before you fight, ask:
- “What is the objective?”
- “What is the exit?”
- “What is the cost if this continues?”

Combat is not a test of bravery.

It is a test of judgement.
