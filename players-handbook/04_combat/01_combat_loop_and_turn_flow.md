# The Combat Loop and Turn Flow

Combat in Future Sky follows a clear loop.

This loop exists to make pressure legible — not to trap players in procedure. Once you understand the flow, combat becomes fast, tense, and expressive.

---

## When Combat Begins

Combat begins when:
- Violence is initiated
- A threat engages directly
- Escape becomes contested
- A scene enters a pressure state where time matters

Combat does not require a formal declaration.  
If actions collide and consequences accelerate, the loop begins.

---

## The Core Combat Loop

Every combat round follows the same structure:

1. **Situation Check**
2. **Intent Declaration**
3. **Turn Resolution**
4. **Outcome & State Update**
5. **Continuation or Exit**

This loop repeats until the conflict ends.

---

## 1. Situation Check

At the start of each round, the table establishes:
- Who is present
- Who is aware
- What has changed since the last round
- Any new threats, hazards, or opportunities

This is not bookkeeping — it is orientation.

If something meaningful changes the situation, acknowledge it before actions are chosen.

---

## 2. Intent Declaration

On their turn, each character declares intent.

Intent answers the question:
**“What are you trying to accomplish right now?”**

Examples:
- “I drive them back”
- “I get to cover”
- “I disable the device”
- “I protect her”
- “I break contact and escape”

Intent matters more than the exact mechanics used.

---

## 3. Turn Resolution

Once intent is declared, the character acts.

A turn may include:
- One primary action
- One or more minor movements or interactions
- Reactive effects based on conditions or abilities

Resolution may involve:
- A roll
- Opposed rolls
- Environmental effects
- Automatic outcomes if uncontested

Not every action requires a roll.  
If success or failure is obvious, resolve it and move on.

---

## Initiative and Order

Initiative determines **when** you act, not **what** you can do.

Initiative may be influenced by:
- Awareness and surprise
- Speed and positioning
- Preparation and readiness
- Environmental factors

Initiative order is established at the start of combat and may shift if conditions change.

---

## Simultaneity and Overlap

Combat actions often overlap.

Characters do not act in isolation.  
Actions may:
- Interrupt each other
- Resolve simultaneously
- Cascade into new situations

If two actions directly oppose each other, resolve them together.

---

## 4. Outcome and State Update

After actions resolve:
- Apply damage or conditions
- Update strain and resources
- Shift positioning
- Introduce consequences
- Clarify what has changed

Combat is dynamic.  
State updates ensure the next round reflects reality.

---

## 5. Continue or Exit

At the end of each round, ask:
- Is combat still the best frame?
- Has an objective been achieved?
- Has the situation shifted to escape, pursuit, or negotiation?
- Is anyone incapacitated or surrendering?

Combat ends when:
- One side disengages
- Objectives are achieved
- All threats are neutralised
- The scene de-escalates

Combat does not need to end in death.

---

## Actions Under Pressure

Time pressure changes behaviour.

Under combat conditions:
- Hesitation increases risk
- Overcommitment increases strain
- Poor positioning compounds danger

Act decisively — but not blindly.

---

## Reaction and Response

Some abilities allow characters to:
- React outside their turn
- Interrupt actions
- Protect allies
- Exploit openings

Reactions represent readiness, not extra turns.

---

## Keeping Combat Moving

Combat should feel tense, not slow.

If players hesitate:
- Prompt with the situation
- Clarify stakes
- Offer options
- Allow default defensive actions

If combat stalls, escalate or resolve.

---

## A Final Orientation

Do not ask:
- “What can I do on my turn?”

Ask instead:
- “What am I trying to change?”

The combat loop exists to serve intent — not replace it.
