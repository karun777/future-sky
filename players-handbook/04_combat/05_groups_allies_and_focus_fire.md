# Groups, Allies, and Focus Fire

Combat rarely involves isolated individuals.

Most conflicts are shaped by coordination — between allies, enemies, and the environment itself. This chapter explains how groups function in combat, why coordination matters, and how focus fire changes the dynamics of a fight.

---

## Combat as a Group Activity

Fights involving multiple participants behave differently.

Groups introduce:
- Shared objectives
- Divided attention
- Opportunity for coordination
- Increased chaos

A group that acts together is far more dangerous than a group that simply fights side by side.

---

## Roles Emerge Naturally

In group combat, roles tend to emerge.

Examples include:
- Front-line pressure
- Area control
- Support and protection
- Disruption and interference
- Observation and coordination

Roles are not fixed classes.
They are situational responses.

---

## Coordinated Action

Coordination allows groups to:
- Cover weaknesses
- Exploit openings
- Control space
- Protect vulnerable members
- End fights efficiently

Coordination requires communication, awareness, and trust.

---

## Focus Fire

Focus fire occurs when multiple attackers concentrate on a single target.

This can:
- Overwhelm defences
- Accelerate collapse
- Force immediate retreat
- Remove key threats quickly

Focus fire is effective — and dangerous.

---

## The Cost of Focus Fire

Focusing attention creates vulnerability.

A group that commits to focus fire:
- Exposes itself elsewhere
- Risks overextension
- May neglect objectives
- Can be disrupted by control or positioning

Smart opponents exploit this.

---

## Breaking Enemy Coordination

Disrupting a group is often easier than defeating it.

Tactics include:
- Separating allies
- Suppressing key members
- Creating confusion
- Forcing reactive decisions
- Eliminating leadership or coordination

A disorganised group collapses quickly.

---

## Supporting Allies

Support actions are not passive.

Support may include:
- Providing cover
- Blocking attacks
- Sharing resources
- Removing conditions
- Enabling movement
- Coordinating timing

Support changes outcomes without dealing damage.

---

## Shared Strain and Risk

Group combat amplifies strain.

Noise, chaos, and divided attention:
- Increase cognitive load
- Accelerate fatigue
- Create cascading failure

A single mistake can endanger everyone.

---

## Allies Falling

When an ally is incapacitated:
- Objectives shift
- Pressure increases
- Options narrow
- Risk escalates

Decisions become urgent.

Protecting allies is often more important than attacking enemies.

---

## Ending Group Conflicts

Group combats often end through:
- Loss of coordination
- Objective completion
- Forced retreat
- Surrender or negotiation

Total elimination is rarely optimal.

---

## A Final Orientation

Do not ask:
- “Who can I hit?”

Ask instead:
- “Who matters most right now?”
- “How do we act together?”
- “What breaks if one of us falls?”

Combat is not won by individuals.

It is resolved by groups under pressure.
