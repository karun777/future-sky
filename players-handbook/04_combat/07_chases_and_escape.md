# Chases and Escape

Not all conflicts are meant to be won.

Sometimes the correct objective is to get away.

This chapter explains how chases work, how escape is resolved, and why survival often depends more on judgement than speed.

---

## When a Chase Begins

A chase begins when:
- One side attempts to disengage
- Movement becomes the primary objective
- Space and distance matter more than damage
- Time pressure escalates rapidly

Chases are combat under a different priority.

---

## Escape as an Objective

Escape is not failure.

Escape may be the objective when:
- The threat is overwhelming
- Resources are depleted
- An objective has already been achieved
- Reinforcements are expected
- Survival outweighs victory

Knowing when to flee is a skill.

---

## The Shape of a Chase

Chases are defined by:
- Terrain
- Obstacles
- Lines of sight
- Routes and choke points
- Endurance and strain

A chase is rarely a straight line.

---

## Establishing Distance

At the start of a chase, determine:
- Who has the lead
- Who is pursuing
- What routes are available
- What hazards exist

Distance is a dynamic state, not a fixed measurement.

---

## Actions During a Chase

During a chase, characters may:
- Sprint or slow
- Take shortcuts
- Create obstacles
- Disrupt pursuers
- Change elevation
- Hide or misdirect

Attacks are possible but risky.

---

## Environmental Interaction

The environment is the chase.

Use:
- Crowds
- Tight spaces
- Vertical movement
- Vehicles or mounts
- Darkness or weather
- Collapsing structures

Creativity matters more than raw speed.

---

## Strain and Endurance

Chases are exhausting.

Sustained pursuit:
- Accumulates strain
- Narrows options
- Increases the risk of collapse
- Forces hard decisions

Those who overcommit burn out first.

---

## Breaking Line of Sight

Breaking pursuit often requires:
- Obscuring vision
- Changing direction
- Using cover or concealment
- Creating confusion
- Reaching safety

Distance alone is rarely enough.

---

## Capture and Cornering

If escape fails, consequences follow.

Failure may result in:
- Capture
- Confrontation
- Forced surrender
- Escalated violence
- New objectives under pressure

A failed escape is still a story.

---

## Group Chases

Chases involving groups are chaotic.

Group dynamics introduce:
- Slower members
- Split decisions
- Sacrificial delays
- Protective manoeuvres

Leaving someone behind is a choice.
So is staying.

---

## Ending a Chase

A chase ends when:
- One side disengages successfully
- Pursuers abandon the attempt
- The pursued are caught
- The situation escalates into combat or negotiation

Endings should feel earned.

---

## A Final Orientation

Do not ask:
- “Can I outrun them?”

Ask instead:
- “Where can I disappear?”
- “What will slow them down?”
- “What am I willing to abandon?”

Escape is not about speed.

It is about understanding space, pressure, and consequence.
