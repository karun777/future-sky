# Conditions, Status, and Control

Not all victories are won by damage.

In Future Sky, control of a fight often comes from changing what others are able to do — not simply how much harm they can absorb.

This chapter explains conditions, status effects, and the mechanics of control.

---

## What Conditions Represent

A condition represents a meaningful change in a character’s state.

Conditions may affect:
- Movement
- Awareness
- Action economy
- Defence or offence
- Strain accumulation
- Access to abilities

Conditions alter the shape of the fight.

---

## Temporary and Persistent Conditions

Conditions vary in duration.

Some last:
- Until the end of a turn
- Until a specific action is taken
- Until the conflict ends

Others persist:
- Until treated or recovered from
- Until sufficient rest occurs
- Until narrative circumstances change

Persistent conditions are signals, not punishments.

---

## Common Types of Conditions

Conditions may include (but are not limited to):

- **Impaired** — reduced effectiveness or reliability  
- **Disoriented** — difficulty perceiving or reacting  
- **Pinned** — restricted movement or action  
- **Exposed** — increased vulnerability  
- **Overloaded** — increased strain costs  
- **Suppressed** — limited options due to threat or pressure  
- **Prone** — disadvantaged positioning  

The exact expression depends on context and era.

---

## Stacking and Escalation

Conditions may stack or escalate.

Multiple conditions can:
- Compound penalties
- Narrow options
- Accelerate collapse
- Force retreat

Unchecked escalation is dangerous.

---

## Control Without Damage

Many actions aim to control rather than injure.

Examples include:
- Suppression fire
- Grappling or restraint
- Disabling systems
- Environmental manipulation
- Psychological pressure
- Forcing separation or isolation

Control creates opportunity.

---

## Control as Objective

Some combats are won by:
- Preventing an action
- Holding a position
- Delaying movement
- Breaking coordination
- Forcing surrender

In these cases, damage is secondary.

---

## Breaking Free

Most conditions can be resisted or removed.

Breaking free may require:
- An action
- A successful check
- Assistance from an ally
- A shift in position
- Enduring additional strain

Breaking free often costs momentum.

---

## Immunities and Resistances

Some characters or enemies may:
- Resist specific conditions
- Ignore certain effects
- Transform conditions into different outcomes

No immunity is absolute.

Every defence has a cost or trade-off.

---

## Conditions and Narrative Authority

Conditions shape the story of the fight.

A character who is:
- Pinned
- Disarmed
- Cornered
- Overextended

Is experiencing a different narrative moment than one who is simply injured.

Lean into that distinction.

---

## Ending a Fight Through Control

Many fights end when:
- One side loses the ability to act effectively
- Control becomes overwhelming
- Escape becomes the only viable option

Total annihilation is rare.
Collapse of control is common.

---

## A Final Orientation

Do not ask:
- “How much damage can I deal?”

Ask instead:
- “What do I want them to be unable to do?”
- “How do I change the terrain of this fight?”
- “What pressure will force a decision?”

Control is often quieter than violence — and far more decisive.
