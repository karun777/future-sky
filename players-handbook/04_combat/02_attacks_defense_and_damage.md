# Attacks, Defence, and Damage

Violence in Future Sky is not abstract.

When an attack lands, something changes.

This chapter explains how attacks are made, how defence works, and how damage is applied — without reducing combat to an exchange of hit points.

---

## What an Attack Represents

An attack is not just an attempt to deal damage.

It may represent:
- Striking an opponent
- Applying pressure
- Forcing movement
- Creating an opening
- Disrupting a system
- Overwhelming defences

An attack is a declaration of intent backed by force.

---

## Making an Attack

When you attack, you are attempting to overcome a defence.

This usually involves:
- An appropriate ability or skill
- A weapon, tool, or method
- The current situation and positioning
- Any relevant conditions or advantages

Attacks may be:
- Physical
- Energetic
- Environmental
- Technological
- Psychological

Choose the approach that matches your intent.

---

## Defence Is Active

Defence is not passive.

Defending may involve:
- Dodging or repositioning
- Blocking or parrying
- Using cover
- Redirecting force
- Absorbing impact
- Interfering with the attacker

Defence reflects awareness, readiness, and context.

---

## Types of Defence

Defence can be expressed through:
- Physical protection (armour, barriers)
- Mobility and evasion
- Tactical positioning
- Environmental advantage
- Resonance or alignment effects

Multiple defensive factors may apply at once.

---

## When to Roll

Not every attack requires a roll.

Roll when:
- The outcome is uncertain
- Both sides are actively contesting
- Failure would meaningfully change the situation

If an attack is uncontested and decisive, resolve it narratively.

---

## Damage and Harm

Damage represents harm, not just injury.

It may affect:
- Physical integrity
- Systems or equipment
- Energy reserves
- Focus and composure
- Structural stability
- Social or psychological footing

Damage accumulates — and so does risk.

---

## Strain and Damage

Not all harm is physical.

Some attacks generate:
- Strain
- Fatigue
- Cognitive overload
- Emotional destabilisation

Strain represents the cost of staying functional under pressure.

Ignoring strain invites collapse.

---

## Armour and Mitigation

Armour reduces harm, but does not negate danger.

Armour may:
- Reduce damage
- Absorb specific types of attacks
- Degrade over time
- Impede movement or flexibility

Armour is a trade-off, not a shield against consequence.

---

## Critical Outcomes

Exceptional success or failure may result in:
- Increased damage
- New conditions
- Forced movement
- Disruption of equipment
- Narrative shifts in control

Critical moments reshape fights.

---

## Escalation and Attrition

Combat rarely ends cleanly.

As damage accumulates:
- Options narrow
- Mistakes become lethal
- Retreat becomes urgent
- Objectives shift

Winning early is safer than winning late.

---

## Killing Blows and Lethality

Lethal force is a choice.

Some attacks are designed to incapacitate.
Others are designed to kill.

Make that distinction intentionally.

The system will honour it.

---

## A Final Orientation

Do not ask:
- “How much damage do I deal?”

Ask instead:
- “What harm am I willing to cause?”
- “What risk am I accepting?”
- “What will change if this lands?”

Damage is not just a number.

It is a consequence.
