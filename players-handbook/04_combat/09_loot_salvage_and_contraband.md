# Loot, Salvage, and Contraband

Combat leaves things behind.

Sometimes that is debris.
Sometimes it is equipment.
Sometimes it is evidence.
Sometimes it is a problem.

This chapter explains what remains after conflict — and why taking something is often more consequential than winning the fight.

---

## What Loot Represents

Loot is not just reward.

Loot represents:
- Resources gained through risk
- Tools removed from circulation
- Proof of action
- Future leverage
- Lingering obligation

What you take changes what follows.

---

## Salvage Versus Loot

Not everything taken is intended for use.

**Loot** is something you want to keep, use, trade, or rely on.

**Salvage** is something extracted for value:
- Components
- Materials
- Information
- Evidence
- Parts or fragments

Salvage often requires time, tools, or safety.

---

## Time and Exposure

Recovering items after combat is not free.

Lingering may:
- Attract attention
- Trigger alarms
- Increase strain
- Escalate consequences
- Invite retaliation

The longer you stay, the more the world responds.

---

## Ownership and Claim

Not everything is unclaimed.

Loot may:
- Belong to someone
- Be watched or tracked
- Carry identifiers
- Be contested by others
- Be illegal to possess

Taking something is a declaration.

---

## Contraband

Some items are dangerous to carry.

Contraband may include:
- Restricted technology
- Stolen or marked goods
- Forbidden artifacts
- Controlled substances
- Evidence of wrongdoing

Possessing contraband creates ongoing risk.

---

## Hidden Costs

Items can carry consequences beyond their function.

A weapon may:
- Draw suspicion
- Invite challenge
- Escalate future encounters

A device may:
- Be monitored
- Require maintenance
- Attract unwanted attention

Value is never neutral.

---

## Transport and Storage

Not everything can be carried.

After combat, players must consider:
- Weight and bulk
- Concealment
- Security
- Where items will be kept
- Who knows about them

The Cube may help.
It does not solve everything.

---

## Distribution and Dispute

Groups must decide:
- Who takes what
- Who bears risk
- How value is shared
- What is left behind

Disagreements here often matter more than the fight itself.

---

## Evidence and Aftermath

What you leave behind also matters.

Evidence may:
- Identify participants
- Trigger investigations
- Shape narratives
- Change reputations

Clean exits are rare.
Careless ones are costly.

---

## When Not to Take Anything

Sometimes the correct choice is to leave everything.

Walking away may:
- Reduce exposure
- Preserve deniability
- Prevent escalation
- Protect allies
- Maintain moral or narrative position

Restraint is a form of discipline.

---

## A Final Orientation

Do not ask:
- “What do we get?”

Ask instead:
- “What does this bind us to?”
- “Who will notice this is gone?”
- “What story does this object carry forward?”

Combat ends when the fighting stops.

Consequences begin when you decide what to take with you.
