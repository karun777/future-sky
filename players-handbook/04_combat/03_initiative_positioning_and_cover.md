# Initiative, Positioning, and Cover

In Future Sky, *where* you are often matters more than *what* you roll.

This chapter explains how initiative is established, why positioning shapes outcomes, and how cover transforms danger into survivability.

---

## Initiative Is Readiness

Initiative measures readiness, not speed alone.

It reflects:
- Awareness of danger
- Mental and physical preparedness
- Position relative to the threat
- Environmental advantage
- Momentum at the moment conflict begins

Initiative answers the question:
**“Who acts while the situation is still fluid?”**

---

## Establishing Initiative

Initiative is determined at the start of combat and may be influenced by:
- Surprise or ambush
- Preparation and intent
- Existing strain or impairment
- Environmental constraints
- Abilities, skills, or resonance

Those who are unprepared act later — or not at all.

---

## Shifting Initiative

Initiative is not fixed.

It may change when:
- Someone gains or loses awareness
- The environment shifts dramatically
- A decisive action disrupts the flow
- Control changes hands

Smart play can steal momentum.

---

## Positioning Is Power

Positioning defines what is possible.

Position affects:
- Who can target whom
- What can be reached or protected
- Which actions are risky or safe
- How easily you can retreat

Poor positioning compounds danger.
Strong positioning multiplies options.

---

## Distance and Engagement

Distance shapes intent.

Close range favours:
- Physical control
- Disruption
- High risk, high impact actions

Medium range favours:
- Tactical movement
- Area control
- Coordinated fire

Long range favours:
- Precision
- Suppression
- Delayed consequence

Choose distance deliberately.

---

## Cover Is Not Binary

Cover is situational.

Cover may:
- Reduce incoming damage
- Block line of sight
- Limit movement
- Force repositioning
- Break targeting locks

Cover can be partial, shifting, or fragile.

Do not assume it will hold.

---

## Using Cover Actively

Cover is most effective when used deliberately.

Effective use of cover involves:
- Movement between positions
- Timing exposure
- Coordinating with allies
- Anticipating enemy response

Staying still invites escalation.

---

## High Ground and Angles

Verticality matters.

Height can:
- Improve visibility
- Extend effective range
- Limit approach vectors
- Increase vulnerability if exposed

Angles create advantage.
Blind spots create opportunity.

---

## Environmental Features

The environment is part of the fight.

Consider:
- Doorways and choke points
- Hazards and unstable terrain
- Light, sound, and visibility
- Obstacles and elevation
- Objects that can be moved, broken, or repurposed

If you are not using the environment, someone else is.

---

## Forced Movement

Positioning can be changed against your will.

Actions may:
- Push
- Pull
- Knock prone
- Displace
- Separate allies
- Trap opponents

Forced movement is often more decisive than damage.

---

## Retreat and Repositioning

Leaving a bad position is a skill.

Repositioning may:
- Avoid lethal exposure
- Break enemy focus
- Create escape routes
- Reset initiative

Staying put is not bravery.
It is often inertia.

---

## A Final Orientation

Do not ask:
- “Whose turn is it?”

Ask instead:
- “Who controls the space?”
- “Who is exposed?”
- “Where will this be unsafe next?”

Combat is fought in space before it is decided by force.
