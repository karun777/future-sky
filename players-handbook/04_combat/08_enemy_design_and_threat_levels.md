# Enemy Design and Threat Levels

Not all enemies are meant to be fought.
Not all fights are meant to be won.

In Future Sky, enemies are defined less by statistics and more by the kind of pressure they introduce into a scene.

This chapter explains how to read threat, assess danger, and understand what an enemy represents.

---

## What an Enemy Is

An enemy is not just something that attacks you.

An enemy represents:
- A competing objective
- A hostile system
- A force enforcing boundaries
- A pressure you must respond to

Enemies exist to create decisions.

---

## Threat Is Contextual

Threat is not absolute.

An enemy’s danger depends on:
- Environment
- Preparation
- Numbers
- Positioning
- Resources
- Objectives

The same enemy can be trivial or lethal depending on circumstance.

---

## Threat Levels

Threat levels describe **how dangerous engagement is**, not how strong an enemy is in isolation.

### Minimal Threat
- Easily avoided or controlled
- Low risk if engaged deliberately
- Often environmental or incidental

### Moderate Threat
- Dangerous if ignored
- Requires attention and coordination
- Punishes mistakes

### Severe Threat
- Capable of incapacitation or death
- Forces objective-driven play
- Escalates rapidly if mishandled

### Overwhelming Threat
- Not intended to be defeated head-on
- Requires escape, avoidance, or clever resolution
- Signals a shift in scale

Overwhelming threats are warnings, not punishments.

---

## Reading the Signs

The world communicates danger.

Threat may be signalled through:
- Behaviour and posture
- Environmental effects
- Reputation or rumours
- Previous encounters
- Narrative framing

If something feels wrong, it probably is.

---

## Enemy Roles

Enemies often fill roles within a conflict.

Common roles include:
- Enforcers
- Controllers
- Strikers
- Disruptors
- Support units
- Leaders or coordinators

Understanding roles matters more than counting numbers.

---

## Elite and Singular Enemies

Some enemies represent focal points.

These may:
- Shape the entire battlefield
- Command or empower others
- Require specific tactics
- Resist straightforward damage

Defeating them often ends the conflict — or changes it completely.

---

## Non-Combat Enemies

Not all enemies fight.

Some exert pressure through:
- Surveillance
- Bureaucracy
- Social control
- Environmental dominance
- Economic leverage

These enemies are no less dangerous.

---

## Escalation and Reinforcement

Enemies rarely exist alone.

Combat may trigger:
- Reinforcements
- Alarms
- Environmental hazards
- Political consequences
- Long-term retaliation

Winning a fight may escalate the war.

---

## Fair Warning

The game will not ambush players with unavoidable death.

Severe and overwhelming threats are telegraphed.

Ignoring warnings is a choice.
The consequences of that choice are real.

---

## Why This Matters

Understanding threat allows players to:
- Choose battles wisely
- Set appropriate objectives
- Avoid unnecessary loss
- Recognise when to flee
- Engage creatively rather than reflexively

Good judgement is a survival skill.

---

## A Final Orientation

Do not ask:
- “Can we kill it?”

Ask instead:
- “What does this threaten?”
- “What happens if we engage?”
- “What happens if we don’t?”

Enemies exist to be understood — not merely defeated.
