# Eras and Timelines

Future Sky does not unfold along a single, continuous line of history.

Instead, it exists across **eras** and **timelines** that overlap, echo, and interfere with one another. These layers form the structural reality of the world. They explain why places repeat, why memories persist, and why actions may have consequences that arrive late, sideways, or unexpectedly.

This section describes that structure without attempting to catalogue its full history.

---

## Eras, Not Ages

An era in Future Sky is not merely a period of time.

It is a **mode of reality** — a set of assumptions about how the world works, what is possible, and what is believed to be true.

Each era shapes:
- How technology and magic are understood
- How knowledge is stored or lost
- How individuals relate to systems of power
- How time itself is experienced

Eras are distinct, but not sealed. They bleed into one another. Artifacts, ideas, and even people may cross their boundaries.

---

## The Jyotzon Era — The Ancient Future

The Jyotzon Era is ancient, but it is not primitive.

It is a world where elemental forces are close to the surface, where environments are alive with intention, and where time behaves more like **myth** than sequence. Knowledge exists, but it is embedded in ritual, landscape, and story rather than stored in archives.

Civilisations rise and fall without leaving neat records. Technology exists, but it is often indistinguishable from spiritual practice. Power is personal, unstable, and frequently misunderstood by later eras.

The Jyotzon Era feels:
- Dreamlike
- Dangerous
- Reverent

Many systems that later eras rely upon originate here, though their true purposes are often forgotten or distorted.

---

## The Omnicron Era — The Fracturing Now

The Omnicron Era is the most recognisable to modern players.

It is a world of failing institutions, fragmented truths, and competing narratives. Technology is widespread, but trust is scarce. Information moves quickly, but meaning does not always follow.

This is the era where fractures become visible:
- Temporal anomalies emerge
- Systems behave unpredictably
- Attempts to control time accelerate its instability

The Omnicron Era is defined by tension between what is known and what can be acted upon. Decisions are urgent, but rarely feel complete.

Its tone is:
- Pressurised
- Fragmented
- Restless

Many of the tools that enable movement between timelines are first developed or rediscovered here, often without full understanding of their cost.

---

## The Manzo Era — The Far Frontier

The Manzo Era exists after collapse, but not after consequence.

It is a frontier world shaped by survival, improvisation, and coexistence. Multiple species, cultures, and timelines intersect openly. Time travel is no longer a secret, but it is far from mastered.

Institutions exist, but they are uneven. Memory is preserved through both technology and tradition. Some truths are curated; others are lived daily.

The Manzo Era feels:
- Lived-in
- Improvised
- Open-ended

Here, the long-term consequences of earlier eras become unavoidable. The past is present, not as history, but as infrastructure.

---

## Timelines and Parallelism

Within each era exist multiple **timelines**.

These timelines are not cleanly separated alternate universes. They are variations, echoes, and forks within a shared reality. Some are stable. Others degrade, loop, or collapse.

Timelines may:
- Diverge from key decisions
- Overlap at specific locations
- Rejoin unexpectedly
- Leave residual traces even after ending

Players may encounter contradictions not because the world is inconsistent, but because **multiple truths are active at once**.

---

## Memory, Echoes, and Persistence

In Future Sky, memory is not confined to individuals.

Actions can leave behind:
- Echoes
- Imprints
- Resonant disturbances

Some places remember more than people do. Some events refuse to settle into the past. Memory may persist across bodies, timelines, or eras, reshaping how the present unfolds.

These phenomena are not rare anomalies.
They are natural consequences of the world’s structure.

---

## Player Movement Through Eras

Players are not fixed to a single era or timeline.

Movement may occur:
- Physically
- Narratively
- Symbolically
- Accidentally

Not all characters experience eras in the same way. Access is contextual, shaped by choice, circumstance, and resonance. Some doors open once. Others close permanently.

Traversal is never neutral.
Where and when you act matters.

---

## What This Means in Play

This structure explains why:
- Information may be incomplete or contradictory
- Returning to a place can matter
- Choices echo beyond their immediate outcomes
- The world sometimes responds slowly, or indirectly

Confusion is not always a mistake.
It is often a signal that multiple layers are in motion.

Future Sky rewards players who pay attention to patterns rather than seeking immediate certainty.

---

## A Closing Note

Eras and timelines are not a puzzle to be solved.

They are a framework to be inhabited.

Understanding will deepen through play, repetition, and consequence. Certainty is rare. Coherence is not.

The world is layered.
So are you.
