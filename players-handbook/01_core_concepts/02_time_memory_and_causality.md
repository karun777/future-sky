# Time, Memory, and Causality

Time in Future Sky is not a background condition.
It is an active participant.

It stretches, compresses, overlaps, and occasionally fractures depending on context. Events do not simply happen *in* time — they alter it. Memory, intention, and consequence shape how moments unfold and how they are later understood.

This chapter explains how time behaves in the world, how memory persists beyond individuals, and how cause and effect operate across eras and timelines.

---

## Time as a Flexible Medium

Time in Future Sky is experienced, not absolute.

Different systems operate on different temporal scales. A conversation may feel immediate while a political shift unfolds slowly. A single action may take seconds to perform but years to fully resolve.

Stillness is not emptiness.
Delay is not absence.
Waiting is often meaningful.

The world does not rush unless it has a reason to.

---

## Asynchronous Play and Soft Time

Most of Future Sky operates under **soft time**.

Players act at different moments. The world continues between actions. Events resolve even when no one is watching.

Soft time allows:
- Reflection before action
- Long-form consequences
- Asynchronous participation
- Multiple players existing in the same world without perfect synchronisation

This is the default state of the world.

Nothing is paused.
Nothing is frozen.
It is simply unfolding at a gentle pace.

---

## Urgency and Hard Time

Occasionally, time tightens.

During certain moments, Future Sky enters **hard time**:
- Events may have countdowns or windows
- Delays carry sharper consequences
- Collective timing may matter

These moments are intentional and rare.
They are clearly signalled when they occur.

Hard time creates pressure, not punishment.
It exists to heighten attention, coordination, and meaning.

When the moment passes, the world softens again.

---

## Memory Beyond the Individual

Memory in Future Sky is not confined to minds.

The world itself remembers.

Actions may leave behind:
- Echoes
- Imprints
- Resonant disturbances

Places can accumulate memory. Objects may carry traces of past use. Some events refuse to settle fully into the past.

Memory can persist across bodies, timelines, and eras, shaping how the present behaves even when its origins are forgotten.

---

## Forgetting, Distortion, and Loss

Memory is not perfectly preserved.

Forgetting is an active force.
Distortion is common.
Loss is inevitable.

Memories degrade, merge, or mutate over time. Stories change as they are retold. Records decay or are curated selectively.

What is remembered is not always what happened.
What is forgotten is not always gone.

---

## Death, Return, and Continuity

Death in Future Sky is not always an ending.

Return is not always restoration.

Continuity is negotiated, not guaranteed.

A character may persist through memory, echo, or consequence even when their body does not. Conversely, survival does not ensure continuity of identity.

This chapter does not define the mechanics of death or return.
It defines the **philosophical space** in which those mechanics operate.

---

## Cause and Effect Across Time

Cause and effect in Future Sky are not strictly linear.

Actions may:
- Produce delayed consequences
- Resolve indirectly
- Matter only in hindsight

Some causes are invisible when they occur.
Some effects arrive without clear origins.

This is not randomness.
It is layered causality.

Understanding often comes after the fact.

---

## Contradiction and Incomplete Knowledge

Conflicting information is normal.

Different timelines, perspectives, and memories produce different truths. Players may hold partial, outdated, or contradictory understandings of events.

This does not mean the world is inconsistent.
It means it is layered.

Coherence exists at the structural level, not always at the surface.

---

## What This Means in Play

This structure explains why:
- Patience is often rewarded
- Revisiting places can matter
- Timing changes outcomes
- Certainty is rare

Future Sky favours:
- Attention over speed
- Interpretation over optimisation
- Pattern recognition over mastery

The world responds to how you engage with it, not just what you do.

---

## A Closing Note

Time is not a neutral container.
Memory is not passive.
Causality is not simple.

They are active forces that shape the world and the stories that emerge within it.

Observe carefully.
Act with intent.
Let meaning arrive when it is ready.
