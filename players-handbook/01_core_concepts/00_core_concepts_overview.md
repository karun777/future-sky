# Core Concepts Overview

Before learning how to create a character, roll dice, or resolve conflict, it is important to understand **how reality works in Future Sky**.

The rules of Future Sky do not sit on top of the world.
They arise from it.

This section introduces the **foundational concepts** that shape every interaction, system, and story within the game. These ideas apply regardless of era, platform, or play style.

They are not optional.
They are the ground everything else stands on.

---

## A World Defined by Structure, Not Setting

Future Sky is not defined by a single location, genre, or historical period.

It is defined by:
- How time behaves
- How memory persists
- How choices propagate
- How systems interact

These structures remain consistent even as the surface details of the world change.

If something feels unfamiliar, contradictory, or incomplete, it is often because you are encountering the edges of these structures.

---

## Conceptual First, Mechanical Later

This section avoids numbers, statistics, and procedures.

Instead, it explains:
- Why multiple eras coexist
- Why timelines overlap
- Why actions have delayed or indirect consequences
- Why the world sometimes responds subtly rather than immediately

Mechanical rules later in the book will reference these concepts repeatedly. Understanding them now will make those rules easier to interpret and apply.

---

## Coherence Over Certainty

Future Sky values **coherence**, not absolute certainty.

Not all information is reliable.
Not all events resolve cleanly.
Not all causes have visible effects.

This is not a flaw in the system.
It is a reflection of the world the game depicts.

Players are not expected to fully understand the world at once.
They are expected to notice patterns, adapt, and act with intent.

---

## How to Read This Section

Each chapter in Core Concepts introduces one major idea.

You may:
- Read them in order
- Return to them as questions arise
- Skip ahead and come back later

No single chapter explains everything.
Together, they form a lens through which the rest of the game becomes legible.
