# Timelines, Echoes, and Parallels

Time in Future Sky is not a straight line.

It branches, overlaps, interferes with itself, and leaves residue behind. Characters do not simply move forward through time — they move *through layers of consequence* shaped by what has already happened, what almost happened, and what may still occur.

This chapter explains how timelines coexist, how echoes form, and how parallel outcomes shape the world.

---

## Multiple Timelines, One World

Future Sky contains multiple timelines that share a common world.

These timelines:
- Are not isolated universes
- Do not overwrite each other cleanly
- Interact through memory, artefact, and event
- Are shaped by player action

A timeline is a dominant pattern, not an exclusive truth.

---

## Echoes

An echo is a trace of an event that occurred — or nearly occurred — in another timeline.

Echoes may appear as:
- Déjà vu
- Familiar ruins
- Conflicting historical records
- Characters who feel “out of place”
- Artefacts with impossible provenance

Echoes are not mistakes.
They are evidence.

---

## Parallel Outcomes

Some choices create parallel outcomes rather than a single result.

In these cases:
- One outcome becomes dominant
- Others persist as dormant or unstable layers
- Pressure may cause layers to resurface

The world chooses what it can sustain.

---

## Timeline Interference

When timelines intersect:
- Reality becomes unstable
- Cause and effect blur
- Memory becomes unreliable
- Systems behave unpredictably

Interference often precedes major change.

---

## Comets and Convergence

Comets act as convergence points.

They:
- Pull timelines toward alignment
- Force unresolved differences into the open
- Accelerate interference
- Create unavoidable pressure

A comet does not erase timelines.
It forces them to reconcile.

---

## Characters and Temporal Identity

Characters persist across timelines.

They may:
- Remember fragments
- Feel drawn to certain outcomes
- Encounter alternate versions of themselves
- Carry skills or scars that lack clear origin

Identity is resilient — but not untouched.

---

## Artefacts Across Time

Some objects do not belong to one timeline alone.

These artefacts:
- Resist erasure
- Carry contradictory histories
- Behave inconsistently
- Anchor echoes

The Cube is one such artefact.

---

## Player Choice and Temporal Weight

Not all choices affect timelines equally.

Temporal weight increases when:
- Stakes are collective
- Outcomes are irreversible
- Choices align with larger forces
- Events are witnessed or recorded

Small choices still matter.
Some just echo further.

---

## Repair, Collapse, and Acceptance

Timelines can:
- Stabilise
- Collapse
- Merge
- Remain unresolved

Not all damage can be repaired.
Not all contradictions can be resolved.

Acceptance is sometimes the only path forward.

---

## Why This Matters

This system ensures that:
- Player choice has mythic scale
- Consequence extends beyond scenes
- Repetition becomes meaning
- History feels alive
- Inevitability feels earned

Time remembers what you tried to avoid.

---

## A Final Orientation

Do not ask:
- “Which timeline is real?”

Ask instead:
- “Which one is winning?”
- “What is being suppressed?”
- “What will resurface under pressure?”

The past is not finished with you.
