# Optional Rules Modules

Future Sky is designed to be modular.

Not every table wants the same density of systems, the same level of meta interaction, or the same relationship between narrative and mechanics. Optional rules modules allow groups to tune the experience without breaking coherence or balance.

This chapter explains how optional modules work and how to adopt them responsibly.

---

## Modularity by Intention

Optional modules are not patches or fixes.

They exist to:
- Adjust emphasis
- Change texture
- Support different play styles
- Scale complexity up or down

The core system remains intact with or without them.

---

## Categories of Optional Modules

Optional modules generally fall into a few categories:

- **Complexity Modules**  
  Increase or decrease mechanical depth.

- **Narrative Weight Modules**  
  Adjust how strongly story, memory, and consequence persist.

- **Meta Interaction Modules**  
  Expand or restrict Cube, timeline, or IRL systems.

- **Pacing Modules**  
  Modify how quickly pressure builds and resolves.

- **Mode Modules**  
  Tailor play for tabletop, digital, asynchronous, or hybrid formats.

Modules should be chosen deliberately.

---

## The Cube: Expanded or Minimal

Some tables may want:
- Deep Cube interiors
- Extensive unlock trees
- Complex memory interactions

Others may prefer:
- Simple vault functionality
- Minimal rule exceptions
- Narrative-only Cube presence

Both are valid.

---

## Timeline Density

Timeline interaction can be:
- Central and explicit
- Subtle and backgrounded
- Limited to specific arcs
- Deferred entirely

Reducing timeline density simplifies play without erasing consequence.

---

## Ambient and Pressure Tuning

Groups may tune:
- Frequency of ambient effects
- Length of pressure windows
- Severity of escalation
- Recovery speed

Lower pressure favours exploration.
Higher pressure favours urgency and risk.

---

## Combat and Lethality

Optional modules may adjust:
- Combat complexity
- Injury persistence
- Death and return mechanics
- Nonviolent resolution emphasis

Choose lethality to match tone.

---

## Downtime and Projects

Downtime systems may be:
- Fully structured
- Loosely narrated
- Abstracted entirely

Long-term projects can be simplified or expanded depending on campaign length.

---

## IRL and Transmedia Elements

IRL nodes and external elements are always optional.

Modules may:
- Enable full transmedia play
- Allow symbolic participation only
- Disable IRL elements entirely

Consent and safety always override mechanics.

---

## Introducing Modules Mid-Campaign

Modules may be introduced gradually.

When doing so:
- Communicate clearly
- Allow adjustment periods
- Avoid stacking multiple changes at once
- Review impact after use

Stability matters more than novelty.

---

## Removing Modules

Modules can be removed.

Removal should:
- Be discussed openly
- Respect existing consequences
- Avoid retcon unless necessary

Removing a module does not erase what already happened.

---

## Why This Matters

This modular approach ensures that:
- The system scales
- Tables retain agency
- Complexity remains intentional
- Different play cultures are supported
- The game stays resilient over time

Future Sky adapts without fracturing.

---

## A Final Orientation

Do not ask:
- “Which modules are correct?”

Ask instead:
- “What experience do we want?”
- “What do we want to feel more of?”
- “What are we willing to track?”

Choose the weight you want to carry.
