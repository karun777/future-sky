# DPC Tools and World Shaping

Future Sky is not run by an invisible, infallible authority.

It is guided.

Developer Player Characters (DPCs), facilitators, or world stewards act as caretakers of tone, continuity, and pressure. This chapter explains how those roles shape the world without overriding player agency or collapsing uncertainty.

---

## What Is a DPC?

A DPC is not an adversary or a narrator.

A DPC:
- Represents forces larger than the party
- Acts within the world, not above it
- Maintains continuity across time and scale
- Responds to player action rather than scripting outcomes

DPCs are participants in the system, not referees outside it.

---

## World Shaping, Not World Control

World shaping involves adjusting conditions, not dictating results.

This may include:
- Introducing or escalating threads
- Modifying world mood
- Advancing faction agendas
- Opening or closing access
- Triggering pressure windows
- Revealing consequences

Players still decide what to do.
The world decides how it responds.

---

## Soft Guidance and Hard Boundaries

Most guidance is subtle.

Soft guidance includes:
- Environmental signals
- NPC behaviour shifts
- Opportunity framing
- Information asymmetry

Hard boundaries exist but are rare:
- Physical impossibility
- Structural constraints
- World-level consequences already in motion

Hard boundaries should feel inevitable, not arbitrary.

---

## Maintaining Tone and Consistency

DPCs act as stewards of tone.

This includes:
- Protecting thematic coherence
- Preventing tonal drift
- Reinforcing consequences
- Ensuring stakes remain legible

Tone is a shared agreement.

---

## Scaling Pressure

Pressure must scale with player capability.

DPCs may:
- Increase complexity rather than lethality
- Introduce layered problems
- Shift stakes from personal to collective
- Reward restraint as much as aggression

Escalation should feel earned.

---

## Intervening Without Rescuing

DPCs do not rescue players from consequence.

Intervention may occur to:
- Clarify stakes
- Reveal unseen options
- Prevent miscommunication
- Preserve consent and safety

Intervention is not absolution.

---

## Transparency and Trust

Good world shaping relies on trust.

This means:
- Clear communication when stakes shift
- Consistent application of rules
- Respect for player agency
- Willingness to acknowledge error

Trust is cumulative.

---

## Meta Knowledge and Restraint

DPCs know more than players.

Restraint involves:
- Revealing information slowly
- Allowing discovery to occur
- Letting players be wrong
- Avoiding premature explanation

Mystery is a resource.

---

## World Shaping Across Eras

DPCs maintain continuity across time.

This includes:
- Tracking echoes
- Preserving unresolved threads
- Allowing history to matter
- Ensuring actions echo forward

The world remembers what players forget.

---

## Why This Matters

This approach ensures that:
- The game remains responsive
- Player agency is preserved
- Stakes feel grounded
- The world evolves coherently
- Facilitation feels invisible but present

A well-shaped world feels inevitable in hindsight.

---

## A Final Orientation

For facilitators, do not ask:
- “What should happen next?”

Ask instead:
- “What pressure already exists?”
- “What is overdue?”
- “What would change if no one intervened?”

The world will tell you what it needs.
