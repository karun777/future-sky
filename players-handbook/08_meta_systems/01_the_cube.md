# The Cube

Every character in Future Sky is bound to a Cube.

No two Cubes are exactly alike. Some are ancient. Some are damaged. Some are barely understood even by those who carry them. All of them break rules — quietly, persistently, and without apology.

This chapter explains what the Cube is, what it does, and why it matters without fully explaining where it came from.

---

## What the Cube Is

The Cube is a personal meta-artefact.

It exists:
- Partially outside normal space
- Across time layers
- In relation to the character, not the world

The Cube is not an item in the usual sense.
It is an interface.

---

## Core Functions

At its most basic, the Cube functions as:

- **A Vault**  
  A place to store items, knowledge, and artefacts safely outside immediate risk.

- **A Record**  
  The Cube remembers things the world may forget — choices made, paths taken, truths learned.

- **A Map**  
  The Cube maintains internal representations of places visited, routes taken, and nodes discovered.

- **A Threshold**  
  It allows entry, exit, and transition between states that would otherwise be inaccessible.

These functions are not always available at once.

---

## Entering the Cube

Characters can enter their Cube.

Inside, time behaves differently.

The Cube may contain:
- Spaces that feel lived-in
- Fragmented environments
- Impossible geometries
- Memories rendered as places
- Tools or interfaces not found elsewhere

The Cube reflects its bearer.

---

## Unlocks and Permissions

The Cube does not reveal itself all at once.

Functions unlock through:
- Progression milestones
- Era transitions
- Key narrative moments
- Chakra integration
- Exposure to specific meta systems

Some unlocks are permanent.
Others are conditional.

---

## Rule Exceptions

The Cube bends rules.

Examples include:
- Storage beyond physical limits
- Access to locations without travel
- Preservation of fragile states
- Interaction across time layers
- Recovery that defies normal constraints

These exceptions are intentional — and limited.

The Cube never solves everything.
It changes what is possible.

---

## Loss and Instability

Cubes can be damaged, restricted, or lost.

Loss may result in:
- Fragmented access
- Memory gaps
- Locked functions
- World-level consequences

A lost Cube is not erased.
It becomes a problem.

---

## Cubes and Identity

The Cube is keyed to the character.

It responds to:
- Habit
- Alignment
- Choice
- Resonance
- Contradiction

Two characters using similar Cubes will experience them differently.

---

## Cubes in the World

The world knows about Cubes — imperfectly.

Some factions:
- Seek them
- Fear them
- Regulate them
- Worship them
- Attempt to replicate them

Very few understand them.

---

## The Cube and Death

The Cube persists beyond death.

It may:
- Anchor return
- Preserve memory
- Fracture identity
- Carry unresolved strain
- Become contested

Death interacts with the Cube differently each time.

---

## Mystery by Design

The Cube is not fully explained.

Its origin is:
- Fragmented
- Contested
- Possibly plural
- Possibly recursive

Understanding the Cube too early would flatten it.

---

## Why This Matters

The Cube exists to:
- Anchor long-term play
- Support asynchronous systems
- Enable narrative continuity
- Hold rule-breaking safely
- Give players a personal mythic object

It is a system you grow into.

---

## A Final Orientation

Do not ask:
- “What does my Cube let me do?”

Ask instead:
- “What does my Cube remember?”
- “What has it not shown me yet?”
- “What happens if it stops responding?”

The Cube is not a tool.

It is a companion.
