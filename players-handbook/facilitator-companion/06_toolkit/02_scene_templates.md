# Scene Templates

Scenes are the unit of play.

They are not locations.
They are not encounters.
They are moments of pressure where something can change.

This chapter provides reusable scene templates you can drop into play at any time — prepared or improvised.

---

## How to Use These Templates

Each template includes:
- a **purpose**
- a **setup**
- one or more **pressure points**
- a **resolution vector**

You do not need to follow them exactly.
They exist to give shape when play feels diffuse.

---

## Template 1: Arrival Under Pressure

**Purpose:**  
Introduce a location while immediately presenting stakes.

**Setup:**  
The characters arrive somewhere new.

**Pressure Points:**
- something is already going wrong
- time is limited
- someone notices them immediately

**Resolution Vectors:**
- intervene
- avoid attention
- escalate the situation
- gather information

**Use When:**  
Players are entering a new zone and you want to avoid slow exposition.

---

## Template 2: The Complication

**Purpose:**  
Interrupt momentum with consequence.

**Setup:**  
Players are mid-task or mid-success.

**Pressure Points:**
- a new variable appears
- a cost becomes visible
- a hidden factor asserts itself

**Resolution Vectors:**
- adapt plans
- double down
- retreat
- accept loss

**Use When:**  
Things feel too smooth.

---

## Template 3: The Offer

**Purpose:**  
Force a value judgment.

**Setup:**  
An NPC, system, or situation presents a choice.

**Pressure Points:**
- gain vs cost
- short-term benefit vs long-term consequence
- loyalty vs advantage

**Resolution Vectors:**
- accept
- negotiate
- refuse
- exploit

**Use When:**  
You want players to reveal priorities.

---

## Template 4: The Countdown

**Purpose:**  
Create urgency without combat.

**Setup:**  
Something will happen if no action is taken.

**Pressure Points:**
- visible timer
- escalating signs
- narrowing options

**Resolution Vectors:**
- act decisively
- split the party
- sacrifice resources
- fail forward

**Use When:**  
Energy is flagging or indecision is stalling play.

---

## Template 5: The Revelation

**Purpose:**  
Change understanding, not situation.

**Setup:**  
Players learn something important.

**Pressure Points:**
- the information reframes past events
- it creates new risk
- it contradicts beliefs

**Resolution Vectors:**
- pursue truth
- conceal knowledge
- confront someone
- reassess goals

**Use When:**  
You want to deepen the narrative without adding threats.

---

## Template 6: The Confrontation (Non-Combat)

**Purpose:**  
Resolve tension socially or strategically.

**Setup:**  
Two or more interests collide.

**Pressure Points:**
- incompatible goals
- power imbalance
- hidden leverage

**Resolution Vectors:**
- persuasion
- intimidation
- compromise
- withdrawal

**Use When:**  
Violence would flatten the situation.

---

## Template 7: The Aftermath

**Purpose:**  
Let consequences land.

**Setup:**  
Something significant just ended.

**Pressure Points:**
- fallout
- emotional cost
- new obligations

**Resolution Vectors:**
- reflect
- repair
- prepare
- move on

**Use When:**  
You want the world to feel responsive and alive.

---

## Linking Scenes

Scenes gain power when linked:
- success in one creates pressure in the next
- failure opens unexpected paths
- choices echo forward

Avoid isolated scenes.
Think in chains.

---

## Scene Length Guidance

As a rough guide:
- short scene: 5–10 minutes
- medium scene: 15–30 minutes
- long scene: full session anchor

End scenes earlier than you think.
Momentum beats completeness.

---

## Facilitator Prompt

Before framing a scene, ask:
> “What could change here?”

If the answer is “nothing,” skip the scene.

---

## Final Orientation

You do not need more content.

You need moments where choices matter.

These templates help you create them — again and again — without burning out.
