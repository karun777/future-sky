# Loot, Rewards, and Unlocks  
*(Progress Without Inflation)*

Rewards shape behaviour.

If rewards only increase numbers, players chase optimisation.
If rewards expand possibilities, players chase meaning.

This chapter reframes loot and rewards as **access, permission, and capability** rather than raw power.

---

## What Rewards Are For

Rewards should:
- acknowledge risk
- reflect choices
- open new options
- deepen engagement with the world

They should not:
- invalidate previous challenges
- trivialise future threats
- create runaway power curves

---

## Types of Rewards

Think in categories, not hierarchies.

### 1. Material Rewards
Traditional loot:
- tools
- weapons
- resources
- currency

Use sparingly.
Material rewards should:
- enable actions
- solve problems
- carry trade-offs

---

### 2. Informational Rewards

Knowledge is powerful.

Examples:
- maps
- access codes
- rumours
- forgotten techniques
- partial truths

Information changes *how* players act without changing stats.

---

### 3. Social Rewards

Status matters.

Examples:
- reputation
- favours owed
- faction access
- safe havens
- allies

Social rewards often outlast material ones.

---

### 4. Systemic Unlocks

These are Future Sky’s signature rewards.

Unlocks may include:
- new actions
- reduced costs
- expanded interfaces
- altered rules in specific contexts
- access to hidden subsystems

They do not make characters stronger.
They make them *more capable*.

---

## Unlocks vs Upgrades

**Upgrades** increase effectiveness.  
**Unlocks** increase possibility.

Prefer unlocks.

Example:
- Upgrade: +2 damage
- Unlock: can target multiple foes under specific conditions

Unlocks preserve tension.

---

## Conditional Rewards

Many rewards should only function:
- in certain locations
- during specific events
- under defined states
- after narrative triggers

Conditional rewards encourage exploration and timing.

---

## The Cube as Reward Space

The Cube is a natural container for:
- unlocks
- permissions
- strange items
- system anomalies

A Cube reward might:
- reveal a new interface
- increase storage capacity
- allow cross-era transfer
- surface hidden data

Do not fully explain these at once.

---

## Delayed Gratification

Some rewards should:
- unlock later
- mature over time
- activate under pressure
- require investment

Delayed rewards create anticipation and continuity.

---

## Partial Rewards

Failure does not mean no reward.

Consider:
- damaged items
- incomplete information
- temporary access
- unstable unlocks

Partial rewards support fail-forward play.

---

## Reward Signalling

Players should:
- sense when something mattered
- feel when progress occurred
- recognise meaningful change

This can be:
- narrative acknowledgement
- system feedback
- environmental response

Do not hide all progress.

---

## Avoiding Power Creep

To prevent inflation:
- cap numerical bonuses early
- favour breadth over depth
- rotate reward relevance
- let old tools become situational

Let mastery come from *use*, not accumulation.

---

## Facilitator Prompt

When designing a reward, ask:
> “What does this let the players do that they couldn’t do before?”

If the answer is “the same thing, but bigger,” reconsider.

---

## Final Orientation

Progress is not a straight line upward.

It is a widening field of choice.

If rewards expand how players engage with the world — rather than how hard they hit it — the game stays alive.
