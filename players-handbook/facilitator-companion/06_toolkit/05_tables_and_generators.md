# Tables and Generators

Tables are not answers.

They are sparks.

This chapter provides lightweight generators you can use to:
- break creative blocks
- add texture on the fly
- introduce uncertainty
- keep play moving without prep

Roll if you want.
Choose if you want.
Ignore results that don’t fit.

Momentum matters more than fidelity.

---

## How to Use These Tables

Use tables when:
- you’re improvising under pressure
- players go somewhere unexpected
- you need colour, not complexity
- the world feels flat

Do not overuse them.
One good prompt is better than five random ones.

---

## Table: Scene Complications (d8)

1. Someone important notices
2. A resource runs low
3. The situation attracts attention
4. A hidden cost emerges
5. Time becomes a factor
6. An ally hesitates or defects
7. Information is incomplete or wrong
8. The environment shifts unexpectedly

Use to add friction mid-scene.

## Customising Tables

Feel free to:
- reskin entries
- add era-specific flavour
- cross tables for complexity
- create your own

The best tables grow with your campaign.

---

## Facilitator Prompt

When rolling on a table, ask:
> “How does this complicate the situation — not solve it?”

Let the result add texture, not closure.

---

## Final Orientation

Generators exist to support your intuition — not replace it.

If a result surprises you in a good way, follow it.
If it feels wrong, discard it without guilt.

The table is there to serve the story, not the other way around.
