# Encounter Templates

Encounters are moments of friction.

They are not synonymous with combat.
They are situations where:
- interests collide
- resources are tested
- consequences become immediate

This chapter provides encounter templates you can adapt for combat, social conflict, environmental danger, or hybrid situations.

---

## What Makes an Encounter?

An encounter has:
- **stakes** (something can be lost or gained)
- **pressure** (time, danger, uncertainty)
- **choice** (multiple viable responses)

If any of these are missing, you may not need an encounter.

---

## Template 1: The Ambush (Seen or Unseen)

**Purpose:**  
Test awareness, preparation, and response under surprise.

**Setup:**  
Threat emerges suddenly — or is noticed just in time.

**Pressure Points:**
- limited reaction time
- unclear enemy intent
- uneven positioning

**Resolution Paths:**
- fight
- flee
- turn the ambush
- negotiate under fire

**Variants:**  
- social ambush (public accusation)
- informational ambush (revelation mid-action)

---

## Template 2: The Standoff

**Purpose:**  
Create tension without immediate action.

**Setup:**  
Two sides are aware of each other.

**Pressure Points:**
- escalation risk
- external observers
- limited patience

**Resolution Paths:**
- intimidation
- compromise
- deception
- calculated strike

**Use When:**  
You want players to *choose when violence begins* — or if it does at all.

---

## Template 3: The Chase

**Purpose:**  
Resolve pursuit without full combat.

**Setup:**  
One side is trying to escape or catch another.

**Pressure Points:**
- terrain hazards
- exhaustion
- splitting the group

**Resolution Paths:**
- outpace
- outthink
- obstruct
- abandon pursuit

**Note:**  
Chases are encounters, not skill checks. Let them evolve.

---

## Template 4: The Swarm

**Purpose:**  
Overwhelm without individual threat.

**Setup:**  
Many small dangers instead of one major foe.

**Pressure Points:**
- action economy
- positioning
- attrition

**Resolution Paths:**
- area control
- retreat
- environmental solutions
- leadership targeting

**Use When:**  
You want urgency without a boss fight.

---

## Template 5: The Singular Threat

**Purpose:**  
Focus attention and resources.

**Setup:**  
One powerful adversary or force.

**Pressure Points:**
- durability
- special abilities
- psychological impact

**Resolution Paths:**
- coordinated attack
- exploit weakness
- delay
- escape

**Reminder:**  
A singular threat does not need high damage to feel dangerous.

---

## Template 6: Environmental Hazard

**Purpose:**  
Shift danger away from enemies.

**Setup:**  
The environment itself is hostile.

**Pressure Points:**
- collapsing structures
- toxic conditions
- unstable terrain
- limited oxygen, light, or power

**Resolution Paths:**
- mitigation
- creative use of tools
- sacrifice resources
- abandon objectives

---

## Template 7: The Moral Collision

**Purpose:**  
Force a values-based decision.

**Setup:**  
Two outcomes cannot both be achieved.

**Pressure Points:**
- time
- emotional cost
- irreversible consequences

**Resolution Paths:**
- choose one
- attempt compromise
- delay and accept fallout

**Use When:**  
You want the encounter to echo long after it ends.

---

## Scaling Encounters

To increase difficulty:
- add time pressure
- restrict resources
- introduce observers
- layer complications

To reduce difficulty:
- widen exits
- offer partial successes
- allow negotiation

Avoid simple numerical scaling when possible.

---

## Ending Encounters Well

End encounters when:
- the core tension resolves
- a clear direction emerges
- continued play adds nothing new

Do not drag resolution.
Let momentum carry forward.

---

## Facilitator Prompt

Before running an encounter, ask:
> “What happens if the players do nothing?”

If the answer is “nothing,” rethink the setup.

---

## Final Orientation

Encounters are not about defeating enemies.

They are about revealing character under pressure.

Design them to ask questions — not just deal damage.
