# Threads, Fronts, and Long Pressure

A Future Sky campaign stays alive because things keep moving.

Threads and fronts are how you track that movement — not as plots to be enforced, but as pressures that advance over time.

This chapter shows you how to run a world that acts even when players do not.

---

## What Threads Are

A thread is an unresolved situation with momentum.

Threads usually involve:
- a goal that is not yet achieved
- an obstacle that resists resolution
- actors with intent
- consequences if ignored

Threads are questions the world is asking.

---

## What Fronts Are

A front is a **cluster of related threads** driven by a shared force.

A front might represent:
- a faction’s agenda
- an environmental collapse
- a technological rollout
- a political shift
- a cosmic or temporal phenomenon

Fronts operate at a higher level than individual scenes.

---

## Why Use Threads and Fronts

Threads and fronts allow you to:
- track pressure without scripting
- escalate logically
- respond to player inaction honestly
- avoid improvising everything from scratch

They are memory aids, not mandates.

---

## Building a Thread

A simple thread needs:
- a description
- who is driving it
- what happens if it advances
- what slows or stops it

That’s enough.

You do not need a timeline or outcome chart.

---

## Building a Front

A front is built from:
- a core impulse (“what wants to happen?”)
- 2–4 related threads
- an escalation pattern

Fronts should feel inevitable — but not unstoppable.

---

## Advancing Threads

Threads advance when:
- time passes
- pressure accumulates
- players ignore them
- other threads interact

Advancement does not need to be dramatic.
Small shifts are often more unsettling.

---

## Soft Time Advancement

In soft time:
- threads advance unevenly
- some stall
- others surge forward quietly

Use this to surprise players later with:
- changed conditions
- lost opportunities
- new complications

---

## Hard Time Advancement

In hard time:
- threads advance rapidly
- decisions lock in outcomes
- fronts collide

Hard time is where arcs turn.

---

## Player Interaction with Threads

Players may:
- engage directly
- undermine indirectly
- delay intentionally
- abandon entirely

All responses are valid.
Threads respond — they do not judge.

---

## Letting Threads Collide

Some of the best moments occur when:
- two threads intersect unexpectedly
- fronts interfere with one another
- a solution to one problem worsens another

You do not need to plan these collisions.
They emerge naturally if threads are alive.

---

## Ending Threads

A thread ends when:
- its core question is answered
- it transforms into something else
- it is rendered irrelevant by change

Ended threads should leave residue.
The world remembers.

---

## Tracking Without Overhead

You can track threads with:
- a sentence
- a few bullet points
- a short note per session

If tracking becomes work, simplify.

---

## A Facilitator Prompt

Between sessions, ask:
> “What advanced while the players were busy?”

Advance one thing.
That is enough.

---

## Final Orientation

Threads and fronts are not there to control the campaign.

They exist so the world can keep its promises:
- time moves
- pressure accumulates
- inaction has meaning

If the world feels like it has a will of its own, your threads are working.
