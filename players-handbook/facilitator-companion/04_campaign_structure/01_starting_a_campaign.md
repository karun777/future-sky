# Starting a Campaign

The beginning of a Future Sky campaign is not an introduction to a story.

It is an introduction to a **situation**.

This chapter helps you start with momentum, clarity, and space for emergence — without over-preparing or locking the future in place.

---

## Start with Pressure, Not Plot

Do not begin with a long backstory or a grand mystery.

Begin with:
- something unstable
- something unresolved
- something that demands attention

Pressure gives players something to push against.

---

## Choose a Focused Starting Context

Pick a starting context that is:
- contained
- legible
- meaningful

Examples:
- a neighbourhood under strain
- a failing institution
- a contested resource
- a brewing conflict

Small starts create room to grow.

---

## Give Players Immediate Agency

Within the first session, players should:
- make a real decision
- feel a consequence
- see the world respond

Avoid long “warm-up” phases.
Agency should be felt quickly.

---

## Establish Tone Through Action

Tone is not declared — it is demonstrated.

Show players:
- how dangerous the world is
- how flexible outcomes can be
- how seriously consequences are treated

Let them learn by doing.

---

## Introduce Only What Matters Now

Resist the urge to explain everything.

Introduce:
- only the factions present
- only the systems in play
- only the pressures that matter

You can always expand later.

---

## Create Asymmetry Early

Give players:
- incomplete information
- uneven resources
- differing priorities

Asymmetry generates discussion, strategy, and tension.

---

## Seed Threads, Don’t Resolve Them

In early sessions:
- raise questions
- hint at larger forces
- introduce unresolved problems

Do not rush resolution.
Threads should pull forward.

---

## Use NPCs Sparingly but Clearly

Early NPCs should:
- want something
- represent a force
- have a clear stance

One strong NPC is better than five vague ones.

---

## Allow Early Failure

Early failure:
- teaches the system
- lowers fear of experimentation
- establishes consequence

Keep early failures recoverable — but real.

---

## End the First Session with Motion

Try to end Session One with:
- a choice made
- a situation altered
- a question sharpened

Avoid static endings.
Movement creates anticipation.

---

## Facilitator Preparation Checklist

Before Session One, you should know:
- the starting pressure
- what happens if players do nothing
- who benefits from the status quo
- one likely escalation

You do not need more than this.

---

## A Facilitator Reminder

Players will care about what they touch.

Let them touch something quickly.

---

## Orientation Going Forward

Once the campaign has begun:
- let the world react
- track consequences
- escalate gradually

A strong start is not about control.

It is about **invitation**.
