# Factions, Power, and World Motion

Factions are how power expresses itself in the world.

They are not static organisations waiting to be interacted with — they are moving forces with momentum, memory, and blind spots.

This chapter helps you run factions that feel alive without requiring constant micromanagement.

---

## What a Faction Is

A faction is any group that:
- shares intent
- controls resources
- exerts influence
- responds to pressure

Factions can be:
- formal institutions
- criminal networks
- cultural movements
- ideological collectives
- distributed or decentralised groups

Scale does not matter.
Agency does.

---

## Factions Want Something

Every faction should want something clear.

That want might be:
- expansion
- stability
- control
- survival
- transcendence
- secrecy

If you know what a faction wants, you can predict how it reacts.

---

## Power Is Relational

Power does not exist in isolation.

A faction’s power depends on:
- who supports them
- who opposes them
- what resources they control
- what legitimacy they claim

Change one relationship and power shifts.

---

## Faction Motion

Factions are always doing something.

Between sessions, factions may:
- consolidate gains
- test boundaries
- recruit allies
- apply pressure
- exploit weakness

You do not need to simulate everything.
One meaningful action per faction is enough.

---

## Faction Posture

A faction’s posture describes how it acts right now.

Examples:
- aggressive
- defensive
- covert
- desperate
- complacent
- opportunistic

Posture changes under pressure.
Track posture more than numbers.

---

## Faction Memory

Factions remember:
- who helped them
- who humiliated them
- who betrayed agreements
- who disrupted plans

Memory does not require revenge.
It shapes future choices.

---

## Players and Faction Power

Players can:
- strengthen factions
- weaken them
- fracture them
- redirect them
- replace them

Do not protect factions from player action.
Let power respond honestly.

---

## When Factions Collide

Factions often conflict indirectly:
- proxy actions
- economic pressure
- misinformation
- legal manoeuvring

Violence is not always the first move.
Sometimes it is the last.

---

## Scaling Faction Response

When players interfere:
- small actions draw subtle response
- repeated actions draw attention
- major disruption triggers escalation

Signal escalation early.
Surprise should come from consequence, not opacity.

---

## Factions and Threads

Factions drive threads.
Threads reveal factions.

Use threads to show:
- internal tension
- strategic missteps
- ideological fractures

This makes factions feel human rather than monolithic.

---

## Removing or Toppling Factions

If a faction falls:
- power does not vanish
- resources redistribute
- vacuums form

The aftermath is often more interesting than the fall itself.

---

## Facilitator Prompt

When unsure how a faction acts next, ask:
> “What would this faction do to protect what it cares about most?”

Act accordingly.

---

## Final Orientation

Factions are engines, not set dressing.

If the world feels like it keeps moving even when players look away, your factions are doing their work.
