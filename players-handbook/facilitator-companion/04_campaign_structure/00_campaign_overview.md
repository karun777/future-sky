# Campaign Overview

A Future Sky campaign is not a plot.

It is not a sequence of quests, not a solved mystery, and not a story with a predetermined ending.

A Future Sky campaign is a **living system** — one that changes in response to player action, inaction, timing, and consequence.

This chapter outlines what that means in practice.

---

## What a Future Sky Campaign Is

At its core, a campaign is:
- a set of pressures acting on a world
- a group of characters navigating those pressures
- a timeline shaped by choice

The world moves whether the players act or not.
Their actions determine *how* it moves.

---

## Campaigns Are About Change

The measure of a successful campaign is not survival or victory.

It is transformation.

By the end of a campaign:
- places should feel different
- factions should have shifted
- characters should not be who they were
- unresolved questions should matter

If nothing has changed, the campaign has not truly happened.

---

## No Central Plot, Only Forces

Future Sky campaigns do not rely on a single central storyline.

Instead, they are driven by:
- competing agendas
- environmental instability
- social and political pressure
- temporal forces

Plots emerge from interaction.
They are not assigned.

---

## Player Agency Is Structural

Agency in Future Sky is not cosmetic.

Players can:
- ignore major threats
- ally with destabilising forces
- accelerate collapse
- prevent disasters
- create entirely new problems

The system must be able to respond honestly.

---

## Soft Time and Hard Time Across Campaigns

Most of a campaign exists in **soft time**:
- events advance unevenly
- focus shifts
- pressure simmers

At key moments, **hard time** appears:
- crises demand action
- deadlines matter
- consequences lock in

A campaign breathes by moving between these states.

---

## Campaign Scope and Scale

Campaigns may be:
- local (one city, one enclave)
- regional (multiple factions, travel)
- planetary
- cross-era

Scale can grow organically.
You do not need to decide everything at the start.

---

## Episodic and Long-Form Play

Future Sky supports:
- episodic sessions
- rotating player availability
- long pauses between arcs

Continuity is maintained through:
- world state
- faction memory
- persistent consequences

The campaign remembers even if sessions are irregular.

---

## Failure Is Not the End

Campaigns do not end because players fail.

They end when:
- transformation has occurred
- an era closes
- a question is answered
- a new equilibrium forms

Loss can be a powerful ending.

---

## The Facilitator’s Role

As facilitator, you are:
- a steward of the world
- a signaler of pressure
- an enforcer of consequence
- a protector of agency

You are not the author.
You are the medium.

---

## Preparing for Emergence

Preparation in Future Sky is about:
- knowing what forces exist
- understanding what they want
- deciding how they advance without interference

You prepare *systems*, not scenes.

---

## A Campaign Promise

When you begin a Future Sky campaign, you are promising players:
- their choices matter
- the world will respond
- time will move
- nothing is free
- endings will mean something

If you hold that promise, the campaign will carry itself.
