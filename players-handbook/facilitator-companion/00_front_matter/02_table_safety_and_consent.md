# Table Safety and Consent

Future Sky deals with pressure, consequence, loss, power, and change.

That does not mean discomfort is required — or that harm is a feature.

This chapter outlines how to hold a table that is intense, meaningful, and safe.

---

## Safety Is Not a Separate System

Safety is not an optional rule module.

It is embedded in:
- how pressure is applied
- how failure is framed
- how loss is handled
- how mystery is revealed

A game that respects agency must also respect consent.

---

## Informed Play

Before play begins, players should have a clear sense of:
- the tone of the game
- the kinds of themes that may arise
- how consequence operates
- how to pause or redirect play

Surprise should live *inside the fiction*, not in expectations about play.

---

## Session Zero Is a Tool, Not a Formality

A Session Zero does not need to be long or ceremonial.

It should establish:
- tone boundaries
- themes to avoid or soften
- how intense play should feel
- how disagreements will be handled

Return to these agreements as the game evolves.

---

## Consent Is Ongoing

Consent is not a one-time checkbox.

It may change:
- as characters grow
- as stakes escalate
- as real-life circumstances shift

Facilitators should remain attentive to:
- hesitation
- withdrawal
- sudden silence
- humour used as deflection

These are signals, not failures.

---

## Pause Tools

Every table should agree on at least one way to pause play.

This may be:
- a spoken phrase
- a hand signal
- a text message
- a simple “let’s pause”

Pausing is not disruptive.
Ignoring discomfort is.

---

## Content and Intensity

Future Sky can include:
- violence
- loss
- systemic collapse
- moral ambiguity

It should not include:
- humiliation
- coercion of players
- sexualised harm
- real-world targeting

When in doubt, soften or abstract.

Intensity comes from consequence, not explicitness.

---

## Failure Without Shame

Failure is part of play.

It should:
- move the world
- reveal information
- open new paths

It should not:
- punish players personally
- mock decisions
- remove agency entirely

If failure feels embarrassing rather than interesting, something has gone wrong.

---

## Facilitator Authority and Responsibility

Facilitators have influence.

With that comes responsibility to:
- listen
- clarify stakes honestly
- acknowledge mistakes
- adjust when needed

You do not lose authority by admitting uncertainty.
You gain trust.

---

## Repair Matters

If something goes wrong:
- pause
- acknowledge it
- talk briefly
- adjust course

Do not explain it away in-fiction without consent.
Do not push through discomfort for momentum.

Repair restores safety faster than silence.

---

## Ending Well

Sessions should end with:
- space to decompress
- optional check-ins
- room for reflection

This can be brief.

A simple “how was that?” is often enough.

---

## A Final Orientation

Future Sky asks players to engage with uncertainty and consequence.

It does not ask them to sacrifice comfort, dignity, or care.

If safety and consent are maintained, the game can go deep without doing harm.

That depth is the point.
