# Running Future Sky: Overview

Running *Future Sky* is less about managing rules and more about stewarding motion.

Sessions unfold as a rhythm of intention, response, pressure, and consequence. This chapter outlines how that rhythm works in practice, and what to focus on when you are holding the space.

---

## The Shape of a Session

Most sessions follow a loose arc:

1. **Orientation**  
   Re-establish where the characters are, what has changed, and what feels unresolved.

2. **Exploration and Action**  
   Players pursue goals, follow curiosity, and test boundaries in soft time.

3. **Escalation**  
   Pressure increases. Stakes become clearer. Hard time may emerge.

4. **Resolution or Deferral**  
   Conflicts resolve, threads advance, or consequences are deferred.

5. **Aftermath**  
   The world settles into a new state. Memory is formed.

This arc is descriptive, not prescriptive. Sessions may loop, stall, or fracture — all are valid.

---

## Your Primary Responsibilities

As Facilitator, your core responsibilities are:

- **Frame situations clearly**  
- **Surface stakes honestly**  
- **Translate player intent into world response**  
- **Track what the world remembers**

You are not responsible for:
- solving problems
- protecting characters from consequence
- ensuring success

Your role is to ensure *meaning*.

---

## Framing Situations

A good situation includes:
- context (where, who, when)
- tension (what could change)
- uncertainty (what is not yet known)

Avoid framing outcomes.

Instead of:
> “You are ambushed.”

Try:
> “The street narrows. Movement above. Someone is watching.”

Let players decide what it becomes.

---

## Intent Before Mechanics

Always ask for intent first.

Before rolls, damage, or outcomes, clarify:
- What are you trying to achieve?
- What does success look like?
- What are you risking?

Mechanics exist to serve intent, not replace it.

---

## Soft Time as Default

Most play happens in soft time.

In soft time:
- actions resolve narratively
- order is flexible
- attention shifts naturally
- pressure accumulates quietly

Soft time supports exploration, social play, and investigation.

---

## Hard Time as a Tool

Hard time is introduced deliberately.

Use it when:
- timing matters
- multiple actors compete
- escalation is imminent
- hesitation has cost

Announce the shift clearly.
Hard time should feel different.

---

## Pressure Creates Meaning

Pressure is not urgency for its own sake.

Pressure exists to:
- narrow options
- clarify priorities
- force trade-offs

If players feel overwhelmed without understanding why, pressure has been applied too early.

---

## Consequence Is the Engine

Every meaningful action should leave a trace.

Consequence may be:
- immediate or delayed
- visible or subtle
- personal or systemic

Track consequences lightly but consistently.
The world becomes coherent through memory.

---

## You Are Allowed to Adjust

If something is not working:
- slow down
- reframe the situation
- clarify stakes
- reduce complexity

Consistency matters more than momentum.

---

## Endings Matter

Try to end sessions with:
- a changed situation
- a new question
- a looming consequence
- a moment of reflection

Closure does not mean resolution.
It means orientation for next time.

---

## A Final Orientation

If you ever feel lost mid-session, return to three prompts:

- What is under pressure right now?
- What choice is being asked for?
- What will the world remember?

Answer those, and the game will move forward.
