# Rulings and Resolution

No rule system can anticipate every situation.

In *Future Sky*, facilitators are expected to make rulings — not as acts of control, but as acts of translation between player intent and world response.

This chapter explains how to do that confidently and consistently.

---

## Rulings Before Rules

When uncertainty arises, do not reach for mechanics first.

Begin with:
- intent
- stakes
- tone
- consequence

Only then decide whether a roll, cost, or outcome is required.

A good ruling preserves flow and meaning, even if it is mechanically imperfect.

---

## The Intent–Risk–Outcome Triangle

Most rulings can be resolved by clarifying three things:

1. **Intent**  
   What is the player actually trying to achieve?

2. **Risk**  
   What could go wrong if they attempt it?

3. **Outcome**  
   What changes in the world if they succeed or fail?

If all three are clear, resolution follows naturally.

---

## When to Call for a Roll

Call for a roll when:
- the outcome is uncertain
- the risk is meaningful
- failure would change the situation

Do not call for a roll when:
- success is guaranteed
- failure would stall play
- the action is purely expressive

Rolling is a tool, not a reflex.

---

## Choosing the Right Mechanic

When a roll is needed, choose the *simplest* mechanic that fits:

- Ability check
- Skill check
- Advantage / disadvantage
- Mana or strain cost
- Countdown or clock advancement

Avoid stacking mechanics unless the moment truly demands it.

---

## Partial Success Is Your Friend

Not all outcomes need to be binary.

Partial success:
- advances intent
- introduces complication
- preserves momentum

Use partial success to keep the world moving even when dice resist.

---

## Failure That Moves Play

Failure should:
- reveal information
- escalate pressure
- alter relationships
- close one door while opening another

Avoid failures that simply negate action.

If nothing changes, nothing was learned.

---

## Being Transparent

Whenever possible:
- explain why a roll is needed
- state the stakes before the roll
- describe what success and failure look like

Transparency builds trust and reduces anxiety.

---

## Consistency Over Correctness

Players will adapt to almost any ruling if it is consistent.

If you realise a ruling was flawed:
- acknowledge it
- adjust going forward
- avoid retroactive correction unless necessary

Momentum matters more than precision.

---

## Inviting Player Input

You are allowed to ask:
- “What are you hoping happens here?”
- “What feels like a fair risk?”
- “Would a cost make sense?”

Shared authorship strengthens rulings.

---

## Edge Cases and Rule Breaks

Some moments warrant exception.

If you bend a rule:
- name it
- note it
- decide whether it is repeatable

This keeps exception from becoming confusion.

---

## When You’re Unsure

If stuck:
- pause briefly
- summarise the situation
- propose a ruling
- ask for objections

Most tables value decisiveness paired with humility.

---

## A Final Orientation

A ruling is successful if:
- intent was honoured
- consequence was meaningful
- play continued smoothly

Perfection is not required.
Clarity is.

Trust yourself.
