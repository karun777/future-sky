# Consequence Design

Consequence is the primary engine of *Future Sky*.

It is how the world learns.
It is how actions gain weight.
It is how stories persist without scripts.

This chapter explains how to design, apply, and pace consequence so that it deepens play rather than constraining it.

---

## Consequence Is Not Punishment

Consequence is not about teaching players a lesson.

It is about:
- acknowledging action
- reflecting impact
- preserving continuity

Punishment closes doors.
Consequence reshapes the space.

---

## Think in Ripples, Not Reactions

The most effective consequences are rarely immediate.

Instead of asking:
- “What happens now?”

Also ask:
- “What changes later?”
- “Who notices?”
- “What becomes harder or easier next time?”

Let consequences ripple outward.

---

## Scales of Consequence

Consequences operate at different scales.

### Personal
- Injury
- Strain
- Emotional shifts
- Changed self-perception

### Social
- Reputation
- Trust
- Faction standing
- Rumours

### Environmental
- Location instability
- Resource depletion
- Increased danger
- Altered access

### Systemic
- Threads advance
- Pressure increases
- Timelines shift
- World events trigger

Not every action affects every scale.

---

## Visible and Invisible Consequence

Some consequences should be obvious.
Others should be felt later.

Visible consequence:
- reinforces cause and effect
- teaches the system
- grounds player understanding

Invisible consequence:
- creates suspense
- rewards attention
- supports long arcs

Use both.

---

## Delayed Consequence Is Still Consequence

Delayed consequence is not a loophole.

It should:
- return eventually
- connect clearly when it does
- feel earned rather than arbitrary

Foreshadow when possible.

---

## Choosing the Weight

Match consequence weight to:
- risk taken
- pressure level
- tone of the game
- frequency of action

Small actions deserve small consequences.
Big choices deserve lasting ones.

Overweight consequence exhausts players.
Underweight consequence drains meaning.

---

## Failure With Momentum

Failure should not stall play.

Design failure so that it:
- redirects action
- escalates tension
- reveals new information

If failure stops the story, redesign it.

---

## Success With Cost

Not all success should be clean.

Success with cost:
- preserves challenge
- creates trade-offs
- avoids trivialisation

Cost can be:
- time
- exposure
- strain
- obligation

---

## Consequence and Player Agency

Always preserve agency *after* consequence.

Even when things go badly:
- players should still have choices
- paths should still exist
- intent should still matter

Loss without agency becomes despair.

---

## Recording Consequence

You do not need to track everything.

Record:
- unresolved threads
- altered relationships
- changed locations
- major costs paid

Memory creates coherence.

---

## Repairing Overreach

If a consequence lands too hard:
- soften future ripples
- introduce support
- allow recovery paths

Correction is not weakness.
It is stewardship.

---

## A Design Check

Before applying a consequence, ask:

- Does this acknowledge the action?
- Does it change something meaningful?
- Does it preserve future choice?
- Does it fit the tone?

If yes, proceed.

---

## A Final Orientation

Consequence is how *Future Sky* tells the truth about the world.

Actions matter.
Time matters.
Care matters.

Design consequences that honour that.
