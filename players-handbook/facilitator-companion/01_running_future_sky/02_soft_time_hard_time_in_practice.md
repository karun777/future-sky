# Soft Time and Hard Time in Practice

*Future Sky* uses two complementary modes of play: **soft time** and **hard time**.

These are not separate systems.  
They are lenses.

This chapter explains how to move between them smoothly and intentionally.

---

## Soft Time: The Default State

Soft time is where most play happens.

In soft time:
- actions are resolved narratively
- order is flexible
- time is abstracted
- focus shifts naturally between characters

Soft time supports:
- exploration
- investigation
- social interaction
- planning
- reflection

If you are unsure which mode you are in, you are probably in soft time.

---

## What Soft Time Feels Like

Soft time feels:
- conversational
- spacious
- responsive
- exploratory

Players speak in intent rather than procedure.
You respond with world reaction rather than countdowns.

---

## Hard Time: When Timing Matters

Hard time is introduced when:
- multiple actions compete
- timing affects outcome
- pressure becomes acute
- escalation is imminent

Hard time uses:
- turns
- rounds
- structured windows
- explicit order

Hard time should feel *different*.

---

## Announcing the Shift

Always announce when shifting modes.

Examples:
- “We’re moving into hard time.”
- “Timing matters now.”
- “Let’s slow this into turns.”

Clarity prevents anxiety.

---

## Why Hard Time Exists

Hard time is not just for combat.

It exists to:
- sharpen decision-making
- prevent dominance by fast talkers
- surface trade-offs
- make hesitation meaningful

Use it sparingly and deliberately.

---

## Common Triggers for Hard Time

Consider shifting to hard time when:
- violence begins or seems inevitable
- a countdown is active
- characters race for the same goal
- a mistake cascades rapidly

Do not wait for chaos.
Shift before clarity is lost.

---

## Returning to Soft Time

Hard time should end when:
- the immediate threat passes
- options widen again
- pressure resolves or disperses

Announce the return:
- “We’re back in soft time.”
- “The moment passes.”

Let players breathe.

---

## Hybrid Scenes

Some scenes move back and forth.

Example:
- Soft time negotiation
- Sudden interruption → hard time
- Resolution → soft time aftermath

This is healthy.
Do not force consistency for its own sake.

---

## Using Hard Time Without Turns

Hard time does not always require initiative.

Alternatives include:
- countdown clocks
- action limits
- escalating consequences per beat

Choose the lightest structure that supports clarity.

---

## Managing Spotlight

Hard time helps manage spotlight fairly.

In soft time:
- invite quieter players explicitly
- rotate attention consciously

In hard time:
- order handles this for you

Use each mode to support inclusion.

---

## Common Pitfalls

Avoid:
- staying in hard time too long
- shifting without explanation
- using hard time to “control” players
- using soft time to avoid consequence

Modes serve play, not authority.

---

## A Practical Heuristic

Ask yourself:
- Does order matter right now?
- Would structure clarify or constrain?
- Are players talking past each other?

If yes, consider hard time.

If no, stay soft.

---

## A Final Orientation

Soft time gives space.
Hard time gives weight.

Knowing when to shift — and doing so cleanly — is one of the most important facilitation skills in *Future Sky*.

Master this, and the system will feel effortless.
