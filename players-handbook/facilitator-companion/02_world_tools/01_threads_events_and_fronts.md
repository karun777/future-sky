# Threads, Events, and Fronts

Threads are how the world remembers what is unresolved.

They track pressure, intention, and instability over time without forcing outcomes. Events emerge from threads. Fronts organise multiple threads into larger forces.

This chapter explains how to use all three together.

---

## Threads: Persistent Tension

A **thread** represents an ongoing tension in the world.

It is not a plot.
It is not a task.
It is not a promise.

A thread exists because something is:
- unresolved
- unstable
- contested
- approaching

Examples:
- A faction quietly mobilising
- A location degrading
- A rumour spreading
- A comet’s influence growing

Threads persist whether players engage with them or not.

---

## What a Thread Needs

A thread only requires three elements:

1. **Name**  
   A short phrase that captures the tension.

2. **Pressure Level**  
   A rough indicator of intensity or proximity.

3. **Vector**  
   The direction it tends to move if left alone.

That is enough.

---

## Advancing Threads

Threads advance when:
- players interact with them
- players ignore them
- related actions occur
- time passes under pressure

Advancement does not always mean escalation.
It means *change*.

---

## Visible vs Invisible Threads

Some threads are obvious.
Others operate in the background.

Visible threads:
- help players orient
- invite engagement
- clarify stakes

Invisible threads:
- build suspense
- reward attention
- allow delayed consequence

You may move a thread from invisible to visible at any time.

---

## Events: When Threads Express

An **event** is a moment when a thread surfaces.

Events are:
- observable
- impactful
- time-bound

An event might be:
- a public announcement
- an attack
- a collapse
- a discovery
- a shift in access or safety

Events do not need to involve players directly to matter.

---

## Triggering Events

Events may be triggered by:
- pressure thresholds
- player actions
- accumulated neglect
- facilitator choice

You do not need to pre-plan events.
Let threads tell you when they are ready.

---

## Fronts: Organised Pressure

A **front** is a collection of related threads driven by a shared force.

Fronts represent:
- major factions
- world-scale threats
- long arcs
- systemic change

A front might include:
- multiple locations
- several factions
- overlapping timelines

Fronts give shape to big stories without scripting them.

---

## Using Fronts Lightly

You do not need many fronts.

One or two active fronts can sustain a long campaign.

Track:
- what the front wants
- what advances it
- what resists it

Let everything else remain flexible.

---

## Player Interaction with Threads

Players do not need to “pick up” threads.

They may:
- disrupt them
- accelerate them
- divert them
- resolve them unintentionally

When players ask:
> “What happens if we ignore this?”

The answer should be:
> “The world keeps moving.”

---

## Recording Threads

Record threads briefly.

A few words is enough.

Example:
- “Market unrest — pressure 2 — spreading”
- “Comet resonance — pressure 4 — converging”

Over-documentation reduces responsiveness.

---

## Resolving Threads

Threads resolve when:
- the tension dissipates
- the outcome becomes irreversible
- the world state changes permanently

Resolution may be:
- quiet
- catastrophic
- ambiguous

All are valid.

---

## When Threads Interact

Threads can:
- reinforce each other
- collide
- compete
- cancel out

These interactions often produce the most interesting events.

You do not need to plan them.
Notice them.

---

## A Facilitator Heuristic

When unsure what to introduce next, ask:

- Which thread is ripest?
- Which has been ignored longest?
- Which would complicate things most right now?

Choose one and let it express.

---

## A Final Orientation

Threads allow you to prepare *without predicting*.

They hold tension gently.
They release it honestly.

If you are tracking threads, the world will feel alive.
