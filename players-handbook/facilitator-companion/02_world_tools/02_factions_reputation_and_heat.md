# Factions, Reputation, and Heat

Factions are how the world acts with memory.

They respond to what players do, what they fail to do, and what they are seen to represent. Reputation tracks relationship. Heat tracks attention and risk.

Together, these systems ensure that actions echo beyond the immediate scene.

---

## What a Faction Is

A faction is any organised group with:
- shared interests
- the ability to act
- imperfect information
- internal tension

Factions are not monolithic.
They have priorities, limits, and blind spots.

Examples include:
- governments
- crews or gangs
- research collectives
- spiritual orders
- corporations
- communities

---

## Factions Act Independently

Factions do not wait for players.

They:
- pursue goals
- respond to threats
- misinterpret signals
- compete with each other

Even when offscreen, factions are doing something.

---

## Reputation: Relationship Memory

Reputation represents how a faction perceives a character or group.

It reflects:
- trust
- respect
- fear
- resentment

Reputation is contextual.
A character may be admired by one faction and despised by another.

---

## Shifting Reputation

Reputation shifts when:
- players help or hinder faction goals
- actions become public
- alliances are honoured or broken
- consequences spread through rumour

Small actions produce small shifts.
Public or symbolic actions produce larger ones.

---

## Heat: Attention and Risk

Heat represents how much scrutiny or pressure a character or group is under.

Heat increases when:
- actions are loud or disruptive
- systems are stressed
- authority is challenged publicly
- patterns emerge

Heat is not moral judgement.
It is attention.

---

## What Heat Does

As heat increases:
- responses become faster
- tolerance decreases
- mistakes cost more
- resources are mobilised

High heat narrows options.

Low heat creates space.

---

## Managing Heat

Players may manage heat by:
- lying low
- shifting locations
- redirecting blame
- leveraging reputation
- resolving underlying causes

Reducing heat often costs time or opportunity.

---

## Heat vs Reputation

Reputation and heat are related but distinct.

A character may have:
- high reputation and high heat (famous, controversial)
- low reputation and high heat (unknown troublemaker)
- high reputation and low heat (trusted insider)
- low reputation and low heat (invisible)

Each state invites different consequences.

---

## Faction Internal Conflict

Factions are rarely unified.

Internal divisions may:
- interpret player actions differently
- create allies and enemies within the same group
- fracture under pressure

Use internal conflict to avoid binary outcomes.

---

## Recording Factions Lightly

For each faction, track:
- goal
- current stance
- reputation trend
- heat level (rough)

A few notes is enough.

Avoid turning factions into spreadsheets.

---

## Using Factions to Generate Play

Factions generate play by:
- offering opportunities
- creating obstacles
- escalating threads
- responding unexpectedly

If play feels static, let a faction act.

---

## When Factions Collide

Faction conflict is fertile ground.

When factions clash:
- players are forced to choose
- neutrality becomes difficult
- consequences multiply

Do not resolve faction conflict too quickly.

---

## A Facilitator Prompt

When unsure how the world responds, ask:

- Who notices this?
- Who benefits?
- Who feels threatened?
- Who overreacts?

Answering these questions points to a faction.

---

## A Final Orientation

Factions ensure that the world is not passive.

They remember.
They adapt.
They push back.

If factions are alive, the world never feels empty.
