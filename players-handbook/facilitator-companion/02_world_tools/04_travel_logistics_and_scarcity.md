# Travel, Logistics, and Scarcity

Movement changes things.

Travel is not just how characters get from place to place — it is how pressure shifts, resources deplete, information spreads, and consequences propagate through the world.

This chapter explains how to handle travel and scarcity in a way that supports tension without turning play into accounting.

---

## Travel Is a Decision

Travel should matter because it:
- takes time
- exposes characters to risk
- consumes resources
- shifts attention and heat

If travel is frictionless, distance loses meaning.

---

## Abstract First, Detail Second

Begin with abstraction.

Ask:
- How far is this?
- How dangerous is it?
- What is unstable along the way?

Only add detail if:
- tension increases
- choice is introduced
- consequence is at stake

---

## Zones, Not Maps

Future Sky works best with zones rather than precise grids.

A zone represents:
- a region
- a route
- a boundary
- a threshold

Zones may differ by:
- safety
- access
- surveillance
- environmental hazard

Movement between zones is where tension lives.

---

## Travel Time as Pressure

Time spent travelling advances:
- threads
- faction activity
- ambient mood
- pressure windows

Long journeys are an opportunity for:
- events
- encounters
- reflection
- complication

Do not fast-forward blindly.
Decide what moves while they do.

---

## Risk on the Move

Risk during travel may include:
- discovery
- exhaustion
- environmental threat
- interception
- resource loss

Not every journey needs danger.
But danger should be possible.

---

## Scarcity Creates Choice

Scarcity is not about punishment.

It exists to:
- force prioritisation
- create trade-offs
- reward preparation
- encourage collaboration

If resources never run low, decisions flatten.

---

## What to Track (Lightly)

You do not need to track everything.

Focus on:
- supplies that gate action
- resources tied to pressure
- assets players care about

Ignore minutiae unless it becomes narratively relevant.

---

## Running Out Is Interesting

When something runs low:
- signal it early
- offer alternatives
- allow creative solutions

Running out should open paths, not close the game.

---

## Abodes and Logistics

Abodes:
- reduce travel friction
- stabilise resources
- create safe recovery points

They are not immunity.
They are leverage.

---

## Trade-offs, Not Denial

Scarcity should ask:
- What do you spend?
- What do you save?
- What do you leave behind?
- What do you risk to gain more?

Avoid flat denial (“you can’t”).
Prefer conditional cost (“you can, but…”).

---

## Travel as Story Space

Travel is a natural place for:
- character conversation
- memory formation
- mood reinforcement
- subtle foreshadowing

Use it intentionally.

---

## Ending a Journey

A journey ends when:
- the context changes
- new pressure applies
- stakes shift

Mark the transition.
Let the world feel different.

---

## A Facilitator Prompt

When planning travel, ask:
- What changes because they are gone?
- What notices their movement?
- What becomes harder or easier?

Answering these makes distance meaningful.

---

## A Final Orientation

Travel gives scale to the world.
Scarcity gives weight to choice.

Use both lightly, and they will carry far more than you expect.
