# How Much to Explain — and When

Explanation is a powerful tool.

Used well, it builds trust.
Used too freely, it dissolves mystery.
Used too sparingly, it creates frustration.

This chapter helps you decide how much to explain, when to do it, and when silence is the better choice.

---

## Explanation Is a Resource

Treat explanation like any other limited resource.

Every explanation you give:
- clarifies one thing
- but closes off others
- and reshapes how players engage with uncertainty

Once explained, a mystery cannot be unexplained.

---

## The Three Layers of Understanding

Most situations have three layers:
1. **What characters perceive**
2. **What players infer**
3. **What the system knows**

You do not need to reveal all three.

Often, revealing the first is enough.

---

## Explain the Immediate, Not the Total

Players need to understand:
- what just happened
- how it affects them
- what options remain open

They do not need to understand:
- the full cause
- the hidden structure
- the ultimate meaning

Let understanding unfold over time.

---

## Use Explanation to Protect Agency

Explain when:
- a ruling might feel arbitrary
- a consequence is severe
- a rule exception is invoked
- player intent was misunderstood

Clarity protects trust.

---

## Withhold Explanation to Preserve Depth

Withhold explanation when:
- mystery is the point
- consequences are still unfolding
- patterns have not yet emerged
- explanation would shortcut discovery

Silence can be generous.

---

## Answering Player Questions

When players ask “why,” consider:
- are they confused?
- are they curious?
- are they testing boundaries?

Respond accordingly.

Sometimes the best answer is:
- “You’re not sure yet.”
- “It doesn’t make sense — yet.”
- “Something about this feels wrong.”

---

## Explaining Meta Without Collapsing It

If you must explain meta elements:
- translate them into world language
- anchor them in character experience
- avoid system jargon

Never say:
- “This is how the game works.”

Always say:
- “This is how the world responds.”

---

## Between Sessions vs At the Table

Explanation is often safer:
- between sessions
- after arcs conclude
- during debriefs

At the table, prioritise momentum.
Between sessions, prioritise clarity.

---

## Trust the Long Game

Players do not need immediate answers.

They need:
- consistency
- responsiveness
- signals that meaning exists

If those are present, patience follows.

---

## Over-Explaining as a Symptom

Over-explaining often comes from:
- facilitator anxiety
- fear of losing control
- desire to impress

None of these help play.

Restraint is confidence.

---

## A Facilitator Check

Before explaining, ask:
> “Does this explanation serve the players — or my comfort?”

If it serves only you, wait.

---

## Final Orientation

Explanation should feel like illumination — not exposure.

If players leave a scene understanding *more* but still sensing depth beyond reach, you’ve struck the balance.

Mystery survives not through secrecy, but through timing.
