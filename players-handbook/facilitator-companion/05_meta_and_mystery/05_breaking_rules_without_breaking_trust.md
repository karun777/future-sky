# Breaking Rules Without Breaking Trust

Future Sky is a living system.

No written ruleset can account for every situation, emergent pattern, or emotional truth that will arise in play. Facilitators will, at times, need to bend, stretch, or momentarily break the rules.

This chapter explains how to do so without damaging trust.

---

## Rules Are Tools, Not Shields

Rules exist to:
- support fair play
- create shared expectations
- generate meaningful tension

They do not exist to:
- protect facilitators from judgment
- override narrative sense
- punish creativity

When rules stop serving play, they can be adjusted — but carefully.

---

## Trust Is the Real System

Every table runs on trust.

Players trust that:
- outcomes matter
- rulings are made in good faith
- consequences are consistent
- the facilitator is not acting adversarially

Once trust breaks, no ruleset can repair it.

---

## When Rule-Bending Is Appropriate

Consider bending rules when:
- the written outcome feels unjust
- fiction and mechanics strongly diverge
- a moment of emotional truth demands space
- the system cannot express what is happening

Do not bend rules to:
- “win” against players
- protect a planned outcome
- conceal mistakes
- assert control

---

## Make the Bend Visible (But Not Mechanical)

When you bend a rule:
- acknowledge it in fiction
- signal that something unusual occurred
- let players feel the shift

You do not need to expose the mechanical change.
You *do* need to honour its impact.

---

## Cost Is the Currency of Trust

Rule-breaking without cost feels cheap.

If you bend a rule, consider introducing:
- delayed consequence
- narrative complication
- resource strain
- future vulnerability

Cost preserves meaning.

---

## Consistency Over Accuracy

Players forgive mistakes.
They do not forgive inconsistency.

If you bend a rule:
- remember it
- apply similar logic later
- note it for yourself

Consistency builds reliability even in improvisation.

---

## Invite, Don’t Dictate

Whenever possible:
- ask players how something might resolve
- invite interpretation
- share narrative authority briefly

Shared authorship strengthens buy-in.

---

## The Cube as Permission Space

The Cube can justify:
- exceptions
- anomalies
- broken patterns
- rule distortions

Use it to explain *why* the rules bent — not to erase the fact that they did.

---

## Owning Mistakes Openly

If a ruling was wrong:
- say so
- correct it going forward
- honour consequences already felt

Owning mistakes increases trust more than hiding them.

---

## Emergency Rule-Bending

In moments of:
- emotional overload
- safety concerns
- player distress

Always prioritise people over systems.

Stop play.
Reset if needed.
No fiction is more important than care.

---

## A Facilitator Question

Before breaking a rule, ask:
> “Am I doing this to serve the table — or to serve myself?”

If the answer is unclear, pause.

---

## Final Orientation

Rules can be broken.

Trust cannot.

If players believe you are protecting the integrity of their choices — even when bending the system — they will follow you into uncertainty willingly.
