# Cosmic Events, Comets, and Sky Changes

Not all events begin on the ground.

Some arrive from the sky.

Cosmic events — comets, eclipses, stellar anomalies, atmospheric shifts — are among the most powerful tools in Future Sky. They operate above factions, beyond borders, and across eras.

This chapter explains how to use cosmic events to shape play without railroading it.

---

## The Sky Is a System

In Future Sky, the sky is not decoration.

It is:
- a clock
- a warning system
- a mythic archive
- a pressure amplifier

When the sky changes, the world responds — whether the players act or not.

---

## Comets as Long-Form Pressure

Comets are not encounters.
They are arcs.

A comet may:
- appear months before impact
- alter tides, dreams, and behaviour
- fracture timelines
- activate dormant systems
- provoke political, spiritual, or technological responses

The key is duration.

Comets should be felt long before they are understood.

---

## Telegraphed, Not Sudden

Cosmic events work best when:
- foreshadowed early
- referenced indirectly
- seen from multiple perspectives

Signs may include:
- unusual weather
- prophetic dreams
- malfunctioning tech
- animals behaving strangely
- factions interpreting omens differently

Let players notice before they know.

---

## Multi-Era Resonance

A single cosmic event may:
- echo across eras
- manifest differently in each timeline
- leave scars that persist

The same comet might be:
- worshipped in one era
- weaponised in another
- archived as data in a third

This reinforces parallelism without repetition.

---

## Sky Changes as Permission

Changes in the sky can justify:
- rule shifts
- altered magic or tech behaviour
- new abilities emerging
- old systems failing

The sky gives you permission to change the rules *temporarily* — or permanently.

---

## Player Choice Still Matters

Cosmic inevitability does not negate agency.

Players may:
- mitigate impact
- redirect outcomes
- prepare survivors
- uncover alternatives
- choose what is preserved

The event happens.
Its meaning is chosen.

---

## Comets and the Cube

The Cube often reacts before anyone else.

It may:
- display anomalies
- unlock warnings
- distort stored data
- refuse certain actions

Do not explain this immediately.
Let players infer importance.

---

## Avoiding Apocalypse Fatigue

Not every cosmic event ends the world.

Use variation:
- some events pass harmlessly
- some affect only certain regions
- some are narrowly avoided
- some reshape rather than destroy

Restraint preserves awe.

---

## Sky Events as Social Catalysts

Cosmic events can:
- radicalise factions
- create cult movements
- justify crackdowns
- inspire unity
- fracture alliances

The sky gives cover to human decisions.

---

## Facilitator Timing

Cosmic events work best when:
- introduced during relative stability
- allowed to simmer
- intersect with player arcs
- climax when players are already invested

Never rush the sky.

---

## A Facilitator Question

When introducing a cosmic event, ask:
> “What does this reveal about the world — not just threaten it?”

Let revelation guide design.

---

## Final Orientation

The sky remembers things the world forgets.

When it changes, history bends toward meaning.

Use cosmic events sparingly, patiently, and with reverence — and players will look up long before you ask them to.
