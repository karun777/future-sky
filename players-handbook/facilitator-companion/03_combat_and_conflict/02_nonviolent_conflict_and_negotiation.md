# Nonviolent Conflict and Negotiation

Most conflicts do not begin with violence.

In Future Sky, negotiation, pressure, deception, leverage, and compromise are not alternatives to “real” play — they *are* real play.

This chapter gives you tools to run nonviolent conflict with clarity, tension, and consequence.

---

## What Nonviolent Conflict Is

Nonviolent conflict occurs when:
- interests collide
- resources are contested
- authority is challenged
- trust is strained

No one needs to draw a weapon for stakes to be real.

---

## Why It Matters

When nonviolent conflict is treated seriously:
- violence becomes a choice, not a default
- social skills gain weight
- preparation and leverage matter
- the world feels responsive

Players should feel that words, positioning, and timing can change outcomes.

---

## Establishing Stakes

Before negotiation begins, clarify:
- what each side wants
- what each side fears
- what each side can offer
- what happens if talks fail

Stakes give dialogue teeth.

---

## Leverage Is the Real Currency

Negotiation is rarely about persuasion alone.

Leverage may include:
- information
- timing
- reputation
- alliances
- threats (implicit or explicit)
- scarcity

A character without leverage can still speak — but leverage shapes outcomes.

---

## Running a Negotiation Exchange

Treat negotiation as a series of exchanges:
1. Position is stated
2. Pressure is applied
3. Resistance responds
4. Balance shifts

Roll only when the balance is genuinely uncertain.

---

## When to Roll

Roll when:
- trust is contested
- deception is risky
- power is evenly matched
- consequences are real

Do not roll when:
- one side clearly dominates
- terms are already decided
- characters are operating within uncontested authority

---

## Partial Success and Compromise

Most negotiations do not end in total victory.

Success may mean:
- reduced cost
- delayed consequence
- partial access
- temporary alliance
- fragile agreement

These outcomes create future tension — which is good.

---

## Failure Without Violence

Failure does not require a fight.

Failure may result in:
- lost opportunity
- raised heat
- damaged reputation
- time pressure
- new conditions

Violence is only one possible escalation.

---

## Social Pressure Over Time

Repeated interactions matter.

Track:
- trust gained or lost
- reputation shifts
- remembered slights
- unfulfilled promises

The world remembers how players behave.

---

## De-escalation as Victory

Walking away:
- can preserve resources
- can shift timing
- can create future leverage

Treat de-escalation as an outcome, not a retreat.

---

## Facilitator Prompt

When negotiation stalls, ask:
> “What changes if this fails right now?”

Introduce that pressure openly or through the world.

---

## Final Orientation

Nonviolent conflict is not softer play.

It is often riskier — because consequences linger.

If players leave the table debating what they *should* have said, you ran it well.
