# Aftermath, Injuries, and Fallout

Conflict does not end when the action stops.

What matters most often happens after — in the injuries sustained, the attention drawn, the trust broken, and the stories that begin to circulate.

This chapter helps you carry consequence forward without grinding play to a halt.

---

## Why Aftermath Matters

Without aftermath:
- combat becomes disposable
- choices lose weight
- danger feels abstract

Aftermath is how the world remembers what just happened.

---

## Injury as Narrative State

Injury is not only a number.

Injuries may:
- limit options
- alter behaviour
- change how NPCs react
- require time or resources to address

Not every injury needs mechanical tracking.
Many are best handled fictionally.

---

## Types of Injury

Consider injuries in layers:
- **Minor** — bruises, fatigue, shaken nerves
- **Serious** — wounds, fractures, burns, trauma
- **Lasting** — scars, chronic pain, psychological impact

Use severity to guide tone and consequence, not punishment.

---

## When to Apply Mechanical Effects

Apply mechanical effects when:
- an injury meaningfully restricts action
- it creates interesting decisions
- it reinforces the stakes of the conflict

Avoid stacking penalties that remove agency.
Prefer trade-offs.

---

## Recovery Is a Process

Healing may require:
- rest
- resources
- medical attention
- safe environments
- time

Instant recovery should be rare and meaningful.

Let recovery itself become play when appropriate.

---

## Fallout Beyond the Body

Aftermath is not limited to injuries.

Fallout may include:
- witnesses
- rumours
- faction responses
- changes in access or reputation
- escalation of pressure

Ask: who noticed?

---

## Social and Political Fallout

Violence is loud.

Consider:
- how authorities respond
- how allies react
- who takes advantage of the chaos

Even justified conflict can have consequences.

---

## Emotional Fallout

Characters may feel:
- guilt
- triumph
- fear
- numbness
- resolve

You do not need to force emotional scenes.
But make space for them if players lean in.

---

## Giving Players Space

After a major conflict:
- slow the pace briefly
- offer reflection
- allow regrouping

This contrast makes the next escalation hit harder.

---

## Using Fallout to Create New Threads

Fallout is fertile ground for:
- new enemies
- new obligations
- new opportunities
- unresolved questions

A conflict well resolved often creates two more.

---

## When to Fade Out

Not all aftermath needs to be played in detail.

If:
- consequences are clear
- players understand the impact
- nothing new is at stake

You may summarise and move on.

---

## Facilitator Prompt

After conflict, ask:
> “What is different now?”

Name at least one thing.
Let it matter later.

---

## Final Orientation

Aftermath is not about punishment.

It is about continuity.

If the world feels altered by what just occurred, the conflict truly mattered.
