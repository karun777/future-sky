# Fast Combat Procedures

Combat should feel tense, decisive, and alive — not slow, procedural, or overburdened.

These procedures exist to help you run combat quickly while preserving meaning, agency, and consequence.

---

## The Core Principle

Combat is a conversation with pressure.

Procedures exist to:
- maintain momentum
- clarify intent
- resolve uncertainty
- move the fiction forward

They are not meant to simulate every detail.

---

## Step 1: Frame the Conflict Clearly

Before any rolls:
- identify who is involved
- clarify immediate threats
- establish what is at stake

Players should understand:
> “Why are we fighting right now?”

If that is unclear, pause and clarify.

---

## Step 2: Establish Intent, Not Just Actions

Ask players:
- What are you trying to achieve?
- Who or what are you focusing on?
- Are you advancing, holding, escaping, or controlling?

Intent matters more than exact positioning.

---

## Step 3: Set the Exchange Rhythm

Combat proceeds in **exchanges**, not micro-actions.

An exchange represents:
- movement
- action
- reaction

Each exchange should meaningfully change the situation.

---

## Step 4: Roll Only When Control Is Contested

Roll when:
- advantage is unclear
- resistance is meaningful
- harm or loss is possible

Do not roll for:
- inevitable outcomes
- repeated dominance
- cosmetic actions

Resolve those narratively.

---

## Step 5: Resolve Decisively

When a roll resolves:
- describe the outcome clearly
- apply consequence immediately
- update the situation state

Avoid partial resolution that requires multiple follow-ups unless tension demands it.

---

## Step 6: Keep Turns Short

Encourage:
- brief declarations
- clear targets
- decisive movement

If a player hesitates:
- prompt them
- offer a default defensive or supportive action
- keep the exchange moving

Momentum is more important than optimisation.

---

## Step 7: Escalate or De-escalate Intentionally

Combat should not stay flat.

Consider:
- reinforcements arriving
- terrain changing
- morale breaking
- objectives shifting

These changes keep combat dynamic.

---

## Step 8: Know When Combat Is Over

Combat ends when:
- one side disengages
- objectives are met
- resistance collapses
- circumstances change

Do not drag out cleanup rounds.
End decisively and move to aftermath.

---

## Running Combat Without a Map

Future Sky supports abstract positioning.

Use:
- zones
- ranges
- relative advantage

Describe movement in terms of intent and consequence, not squares.

---

## Handling Large Groups

For speed:
- group similar enemies
- resolve allied NPCs quickly
- focus spotlight on player impact

Do not roll for every body if it does not change the outcome.

---

## A Facilitator Shortcut

When unsure, ask:
> “What is the most interesting thing that could happen next?”

Resolve toward that.

---

## Final Orientation

Fast combat is not rushed combat.

It is clear, intentional, and decisive.

If everyone understands what happened and why it mattered, you did it right.
