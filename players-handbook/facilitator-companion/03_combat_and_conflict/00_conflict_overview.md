# Conflict Overview

Conflict is not a subsystem.

It is what happens when intention meets resistance.

In Future Sky, combat is one expression of conflict — not its default form, not its highest form, and not its most interesting one by necessity.

This section helps you run conflict that feels consequential, expressive, and responsive to player agency.

---

## What Conflict Is

Conflict occurs when:
- goals collide
- resources are contested
- values oppose one another
- timing creates friction

Violence is one possible outcome.
It is not the definition.

---

## Why This Matters

If combat is treated as the primary resolution tool:
- creativity narrows
- outcomes become predictable
- non-combat play is devalued

Future Sky works best when players believe:
> “We chose this conflict — it wasn’t the only option.”

---

## Combat as a Choice

Combat should feel like:
- escalation
- commitment
- risk acceptance

When combat begins, something has already gone wrong — or something has become worth fighting for.

That framing changes how players engage with it.

---

## The Spectrum of Conflict

Conflict exists on a spectrum:
- social pressure
- negotiation
- manipulation
- pursuit
- sabotage
- standoff
- violence

Facilitators should feel free to slide conflict along this spectrum at any time.

---

## Clear Stakes Before Dice

Before resolving conflict, clarify:
- what each side wants
- what each side risks
- what changes if one side wins

Dice resolve uncertainty.
They should not replace clarity.

---

## Consequence First, Mechanics Second

Always ask:
- What happens if this goes badly?
- What happens if this goes well?

Then choose mechanics that support that outcome.

This keeps the fiction in front of the system.

---

## Fair Does Not Mean Safe

Conflict should be:
- understandable
- legible
- consistent

It does not need to be safe.

Players should be able to see danger coming — not be protected from it.

---

## When Not to Fight

It is valid for players to:
- flee
- surrender
- negotiate
- change the situation

Treat these as outcomes, not failures.

A conflict that ends without violence is still resolved.

---

## Ending Conflict

Conflict ends when:
- goals change
- pressure dissipates
- one side disengages
- circumstances shift

Not every conflict ends in victory or defeat.
Some end in compromise, loss, or delay.

---

## Facilitator Authority

As facilitator, you:
- frame the conflict
- present resistance honestly
- enforce consequence
- protect agency

You do not need to “win.”
You need to be consistent.

---

## A Table Check

After a conflict, ask:
- Did choices matter?
- Were stakes felt?
- Did the world change?

If yes, the conflict worked.

---

## Orientation Going Forward

The chapters that follow provide tools for:
- violent conflict
- nonviolent resolution
- escalation and de-escalation
- group dynamics
- aftermath and fallout

Use what serves the moment.

Conflict is a tool — not a mandate.
