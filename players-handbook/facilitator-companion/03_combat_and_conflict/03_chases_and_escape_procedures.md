# Chases and Escape Procedures

Chases are conflict under motion.

They are about momentum, terrain, pressure, and decision-making — not perfect movement math.

This chapter helps you run chases that feel fast, cinematic, and consequential.

---

## When to Use a Chase

Use chase procedures when:
- pursuit is active
- distance matters
- capture or escape is uncertain
- the environment plays a role

Do not use chases for trivial movement.
They exist for moments of urgency.

---

## Define the Goal First

Clarify immediately:
- who is chasing
- who is fleeing
- what escape or capture means

A chase ends when the goal is achieved — not when someone’s HP runs out.

---

## Zones Over Distances

Use zones instead of exact measurements.

Zones represent:
- relative distance
- environmental shifts
- opportunity thresholds

Examples:
- “within reach”
- “closing”
- “breaking line of sight”
- “nearly gone”

---

## The Chase Exchange

Each exchange:
- presents a choice
- introduces a risk
- changes position or pressure

Ask each side:
- How are you gaining ground or creating space?
- What are you willing to risk?

---

## Terrain as a Participant

Terrain should:
- block
- complicate
- offer shortcuts
- introduce danger

Let the environment do work.
It keeps chases dynamic without extra mechanics.

---

## Rolling in a Chase

Roll when:
- terrain is contested
- risk is escalating
- escape is uncertain

Do not roll every exchange.
Roll when the situation could turn.

---

## Consequences of Failure

Failure in a chase may mean:
- losing ground
- exhaustion
- injury
- dropped gear
- unwanted attention

Capture is only one possible outcome.

---

## Escape Is an Outcome, Not a Reward

Escaping does not mean:
- no consequences
- total safety
- narrative reset

Escaped characters may:
- be wounded
- be followed later
- have left something behind

That tension carries forward.

---

## Ending a Chase

A chase ends when:
- distance stabilises
- line of sight is broken
- pursuit is abandoned
- conditions force resolution

End cleanly.
Move into aftermath immediately.

---

## Facilitator Shortcut

If the chase is dragging, ask:
> “What is the most dangerous moment right now?”

Resolve toward that moment and conclude.

---

## Final Orientation

Chases should feel breathless, not technical.

If players feel relief, regret, or triumph when it ends, the chase worked.
