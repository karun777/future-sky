# Quick Reference

This section provides fast reminders for common procedures during play.

It is designed to be consulted mid-session without breaking flow.

---

## Core Loop (At a Glance)

1. **Situation is described**
2. **Player declares intent**
3. **Table assesses risk and stakes**
4. **Resolution check (if needed)**
5. **Outcome is narrated**
6. **Consequence is recorded**
7. **Pressure or time advances**

Repeat as needed.

---

## When to Roll

Roll only when:
- The outcome is uncertain
- The stakes matter
- Failure would change the situation

Do not roll for:
- Routine actions
- Pure narration
- Actions with no meaningful consequence

---

## Action Types

- **Simple Action** — resolves without roll  
- **Risky Action** — roll required  
- **Strained Action** — costs strain or mana  
- **Pressured Action** — occurs during hard time  
- **Desperate Action** — failure escalates consequence  

---

## Resolution Check (Standard)

1. Roll **d20**
2. Add relevant **ability modifier**
3. Add **skill or proficiency bonus** (if applicable)
4. Compare to **difficulty**

### Outcomes
- **Strong Success** — exceed difficulty significantly
- **Success** — achieve intent
- **Partial Success** — succeed with cost
- **Failure** — intent fails, consequence applies
- **Critical Failure** — failure escalates or distorts

---

## Advantage & Disadvantage

- **Advantage** — roll twice, take higher
- **Disadvantage** — roll twice, take lower

Multiple sources do not stack.

---

## Strain & Mana

- Spend **mana** to enhance, sustain, or exceed limits
- Accrue **strain** when pushing beyond alignment
- High strain increases risk of distortion or failure

---

## Soft Time vs Hard Time

**Soft Time**
- Default mode
- Order abstracted
- Emphasis on flow and narration

**Hard Time**
- Turn-based or structured windows
- Order matters
- Pressure escalates
- Common in combat or critical events

---

## Combat Snapshot

1. Establish intent and positioning
2. Determine initiative (if needed)
3. Resolve actions in order
4. Apply damage, conditions, or effects
5. Check morale, surrender, or retreat
6. Advance pressure or end conflict

Combat is one form of conflict, not the only one.

---

## Death & Return (Summary)

- Death is possible
- Return is likely, but not free
- Loss, distortion, or change may occur
- The world remembers

---

## Downtime Actions

During downtime, characters may:
- Recover health and strain
- Train skills or abilities
- Work on projects
- Tend abodes
- Reflect or research
- Engage factions

Downtime abstracts time but not consequence.

---

## The Cube (Quick Notes)

- Every character has one
- Functions unlock through play
- The Cube records, stores, and bends rules
- Damage or alteration matters

---

## Facilitator Prompts

If unsure what happens next, ask:
- “What are you trying to achieve?”
- “What happens if this fails?”
- “Who or what notices this?”
- “What does this cost?”

---

## When in Doubt

Choose:
- Clarity over complexity
- Consequence over negation
- Flow over precision
- Agreement over enforcement

The game works because the table does.
