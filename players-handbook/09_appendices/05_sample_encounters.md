# Sample Encounters

These sample encounters demonstrate how Future Sky systems function in play.

They are not scripts.  
They are *patterns*.

Use them to:
- Learn the flow of play
- Teach new players
- Stress-test tone
- Adapt for your own tables

Each example highlights different mechanics.

---

## Encounter 1: The Flickering Node  
*(Soft Time · Investigation · Ambient Pressure)*

**Context**  
The players arrive at a location where power intermittently surges. Lights flicker. Old devices hum. Locals avoid the area.

**What’s Happening**  
A memory node has destabilised due to accumulated strain from recent events. It is not hostile — but it is volatile.

**Player Options**
- Investigate the source
- Stabilise the node
- Extract information
- Ignore it and move on

**Mechanics in Play**
- Perception and investigation checks  
- Chakra alignment (Ether / Spirit)  
- Ambient effects increasing strain over time  

**Outcomes**
- Stabilisation reduces future pressure in the area  
- Extraction provides clues but adds distortion  
- Ignoring it allows escalation later  

**Key Lesson**
Not every encounter demands resolution — but unresolved systems remember.

## Encounter 2: The Broken Negotiation  
*(Social Conflict · Reputation · Consequence)*

**Context**  
A faction meeting collapses after an insult — accidental or deliberate. Voices rise. Trust frays.

**What’s Happening**  
The conflict is social, not violent, but the stakes are real. Resources, safety, and alliances are on the line.

**Player Options**
- De-escalate
- Reframe the conversation
- Shift blame
- Walk away

**Mechanics in Play**
- Social skills and reputation  
- Advantage from preparation or leverage  
- Partial success with lingering resentment  

**Outcomes**
- Successful mediation improves standing  
- Partial success secures short-term gains but seeds a thread  
- Failure fractures relationships  

**Key Lesson**
Winning an argument does not always mean winning trust.

---

## Encounter 3: Ambush at the Crossing  
*(Hard Time · Combat · Initiative)*

**Context**  
While travelling, the group is ambushed at a narrow crossing point.

**What’s Happening**  
Enemies want something specific — not necessarily blood.

**Mechanics in Play**
- Initiative and positioning  
- Cover and terrain  
- Focus fire and group tactics  
- Morale and surrender checks  

**Player Options**
- Fight
- Hold ground
- Break through
- Negotiate mid-conflict

**Outcomes**
- Combat victory yields salvage and information  
- Retreat preserves resources but escalates pursuit  
- Surrender opens narrative complications  

**Key Lesson**
Combat is fast, loud, and consequential — but rarely clean.

---

## Encounter 4: The Long Night  
*(Downtime · Strain · Abodes)*

**Context**  
After multiple stressful events, the group takes shelter.

**What’s Happening**  
This is not a rest — it is a reckoning.

**Player Options**
- Recover and reflect
- Tend projects
- Argue or reconcile
- Train or plan

**Mechanics in Play**
- Strain recovery  
- Abode features  
- Relationship changes  
- Memory formation  

**Outcomes**
- Full recovery requires time or support  
- Ignored strain carries forward  
- Bonds strengthen or fracture  

**Key Lesson**
Rest is active, not passive.

---

## Encounter 5: Pressure Window — The Sirens  
*(World Event · Time Pressure · Choice)*

**Context**  
Sirens signal an approaching disaster. Evacuation windows are limited.

**What’s Happening**  
A comet-linked event is entering a pressure window. Not everything can be saved.

**Player Options**
- Evacuate civilians
- Secure vital assets
- Investigate the cause
- Save themselves

**Mechanics in Play**
- Hard time escalation  
- Limited actions  
- Trade-offs  
- Long-term consequence tracking  

**Outcomes**
- Choices define future world state  
- Unchosen paths close permanently  
- The world responds visibly  

**Key Lesson**
Agency matters most when it costs something.

---

## Encounter 6: The Cube Reacts  
*(Meta-System · Anomaly · Discovery)*

**Context**  
A character’s Cube activates unexpectedly.

**What’s Happening**  
The Cube has detected a convergence. It offers access — at a price.

**Player Options**
- Enter the Cube
- Delay activation
- Override safeguards
- Seek external help

**Mechanics in Play**
- Unlock conditions  
- Distortion risk  
- Narrative exception handling  

**Outcomes**
- New capabilities unlocked  
- Altered Cube behaviour  
- Memory recorded permanently  

**Key Lesson**
The Cube is a tool — but not a servant.

## Using These Encounters

These examples are meant to be:
- Reused
- Reframed
- Recombined

Change the setting, the faces, the stakes.

The structure remains.

---

## Facilitator Note

If players ask:
> “What are we supposed to do?”

Answer:
> “What do you want to risk?”

That question always moves the game forward.
