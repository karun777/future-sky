# Classes Overview

In Future Sky, a class is not a profession.

It is a **way of engaging with the world**.

Classes represent enduring archetypes — patterns of perception, response, and transformation that recur across history, collapse, and frontier alike. They are not bound to a single era, technology level, or aesthetic. Instead, they express themselves differently depending on context.

The same archetype faces different pressures in different worlds.
Those pressures shape mastery.

---

## Archetype Before Role

If you are familiar with d20 roleplaying games, you may be used to classes defining:
- Combat roles
- Party functions
- Mechanical niches

Future Sky classes still support these functions, but they are not defined by them.

Each class answers a deeper question:

- How do you respond to uncertainty?
- Where do you place your attention?
- What do you change when the world resists you?

Two characters of the same class may behave very differently, especially across eras, but they share a common **orientation**.

---

## Classes Across Eras

Future Sky unfolds across multiple eras:
- A mythic, elemental past
- A fractured, collapsing present
- A hybrid, frontier future

Each era presents similar *evolutionary challenges* — survival, meaning, agency, responsibility — but under radically different conditions.

A class persists across eras because the challenge persists.

What changes is:
- The available tools
- The dominant threats
- The cost of failure
- The form mastery takes

Classes are stable.
Expressions are adaptive.

---

## Era Expression

Every class has **era expressions**.

An era expression is not a subclass in the traditional sense. It does not lock you into a fixed branch or replace your core identity.

Instead, it describes how the same archetype manifests under different pressures.

For example:
- A class that relies on intuition may appear as a shaman, a visionary, or a neuro-seer depending on era.
- A class oriented toward resistance may be a tribal defender, a covert organiser, or a systems saboteur.

The archetype remains.
The expression evolves.

---

## Resonance and Inner Development

Classes in Future Sky are also defined by **resonance**.

Each class aligns more naturally with certain:
- Chakras
- Elemental mana flows
- Modes of attention and action

This does not mean other paths are inaccessible.
It means they are **costlier**.

Growth involves learning when to lean into alignment and when to strain beyond it. Mastery is not just external competence — it is internal balance.

---

## Progression as Trajectory

Advancement in Future Sky is not purely linear.

Classes progress by:
- Deepening their relationship to their archetype
- Responding to crisis points
- Making consequential choices
- Developing mastery under pressure

Every class includes a **signature moment** — an example of peak resonance where the archetype is fully expressed. This is not a guaranteed outcome, but a possibility earned through play.

---

## Choosing a Class

When choosing a class, do not ask:
- “What does the most damage?”
- “What is optimal at high levels?”

Instead, ask:
- “How does this class face the world?”
- “What kinds of challenges does it invite?”
- “What would growth look like for this character?”

You are choosing a **trajectory**, not a toolkit.

## The Classes

The following classes are available in Future Sky:

- **Dreamer-Technician** — Navigators of perception, memory, and altered states  
- **Mystic-Kin** — Embodied intermediaries between human, animal, and elemental worlds  
- **Rebel-Architect** — Designers of resistance, structure, and counter-systems  
- **Primal-Rider** — Bonded movers, guardians, and frontier survivors  
- **Glyph-Speaker** — Interpreters of symbol, language, and encoded reality  
- **Shadow-Echo** — Operatives of absence, reflection, and unseen consequence  
- **Astro-Cartographer** — Mappers of space, time, and probability  
- **Commotionist** — Catalysts of chaos, creativity, and systemic disruption  
- **Unwritten** — Those who resist definition and develop outside known paths

Each class is explored in detail in the following sections, including:
- Class fantasy
- Role in the world
- Era expressions
- Resonance and affinities
- Signature moments
- Advancement themes

---

## A Final Orientation

No class in Future Sky is complete on its own.

Each exists in relation to:
- The era
- The world state
- Other characters
- The choices you make under pressure

You are not selecting who your character *is* forever.

You are choosing how they begin.

> ### Sidebar: If You’ve Played Other d20 Games
>
> If you have experience with other d20-based roleplaying games, you may find it helpful to orient yourself by comparing familiar class instincts to the archetypes used in Future Sky.
>
> This comparison is **not a conversion guide** and not a statement of equivalence. It exists only to help you find your footing as you begin. Once play is underway, these reference points quickly fall away.
>
> Think of this as a **translation of posture**, not mechanics.
>
> ---
>
> #### From Roles to Orientations
>
> In many d20 systems, classes primarily answer the question:
>
> *“What does my character do in a fight or challenge?”*
>
> In Future Sky, classes instead answer:
>
> *“How does my character relate to uncertainty, power, and change?”*
>
> The tools may look familiar at first, but the emphasis is different. Classes describe enduring ways of engaging with the world — patterns that persist across eras, technologies, and cultural collapse.
>
> ---
>
> #### Familiar Instincts, New Expressions
>
> If you have enjoyed playing characters who rely on **physical presence, endurance, and embodied skill**, you may feel at home with the **Primal-Rider**. This archetype carries forward instincts associated with fighters, rangers, and other frontier-oriented characters — but expresses them through movement, terrain, and bonded relationships rather than static combat roles.
>
> If you gravitate toward **stealth, leverage, and indirect influence**, the **Shadow-Echo** may resonate. This archetype reflects instincts familiar to players of rogues and similar figures, but reframes them around absence, delayed consequence, and systemic pressure rather than simple evasion or damage.
>
> If you enjoy **magic, symbolism, or knowledge as power**, several paths may appeal:
> - The **Glyph-Speaker** reflects a relationship to encoded reality — symbols, language, and meaning shaping the world.
> - The **Dreamer-Technician** channels altered perception, intuition, and contact with other intelligences.
> - The **Mystic-Kin** embodies mediation between forces — human, elemental, animal, or spiritual.
>
> These archetypes overlap in surface aesthetics but differ in how they approach understanding and influence.
>
> If you prefer **planning, structure, or system-level problem solving**, the **Rebel-Architect** or **Astro-Cartographer** may feel intuitive. These classes focus on design, navigation, and the shaping of larger patterns — whether social, technological, or temporal.
>
> Finally, if you are drawn to **disruption, creativity, and catalytic change**, the **Commotionist** embraces instability as a force rather than a flaw, turning chaos into momentum.
>
> ---
>
> #### Why the Mapping Is Imperfect (By Design)
>
> You may notice that no Future Sky class maps cleanly to a single traditional role.
>
> This is intentional.
>
> Classes are not designed to occupy fixed niches such as “damage dealer,” “support,” or “tank.” Instead, they describe **trajectories of development** — how a character grows in response to pressure, context, and consequence.
>
> The same archetype may feel very different depending on:
> - The era in which play occurs
> - The systems currently under strain
> - The choices you make under uncertainty
>
> Familiar instincts help you begin. They do not define where you end.
>
> ---
>
> #### Use This as a Starting Point — Then Let It Go
>
> If this sidebar helps you choose a class, it has done its job.
>
> Once play begins, pay more attention to:
> - How your character responds when plans fail
> - What kinds of situations draw them forward
> - Where they feel aligned or strained
>
> Those experiences will teach you far more about your class than any comparison ever could.
>
> Future Sky is designed so that understanding emerges through participation.
>
> Begin with what feels familiar.
> Then allow the world to change how you play.
