# Era Expression

In Future Sky, a character’s class does not change across eras.

What changes is **how that class is expressed**.

Era expression describes how the same archetypal orientation responds to different historical, technological, and cultural pressures. It is not a cosmetic overlay, and it is not a rigid subclass system.

It is context made legible.

---

## Archetype Is Stable, Context Is Not

Classes in Future Sky represent enduring ways of engaging with the world.

These archetypes persist because the core challenges of existence persist:
- Survival
- Meaning
- Agency
- Relationship
- Change

What differs between eras is the *shape* of those challenges.

A Dreamer navigating mythic spirits faces different risks than one navigating corrupted data streams. A Primal-Rider crossing elemental terrain develops differently than one piloting frontier craft.

The archetype remains.
The pressures evolve.

---

## What Era Expression Is (and Is Not)

Era expression **is**:
- A lens for interpretation
- A contextual modifier
- A narrative and mechanical emphasis

Era expression **is not**:
- A separate class
- A permanent branch choice
- A restriction on identity

You are not choosing a new role.
You are responding to a new world.

---

## The Three Core Eras

Future Sky currently operates across three primary eras. Each one emphasises different survival logics and mastery paths.

### Jyotzon Era — The Ancient Future

A mythic, elemental world shaped by ritual, lineage, and living systems.

Knowledge is embodied.
Power is relational.
Technology and spirituality are inseparable.

Mastery here often develops through:
- Ritual practice
- Ancestral memory
- Embodied skill
- Symbolic action

---

### Omnicron Era — The Fracturing Now

A collapsing present marked by systemic failure, surveillance, and cultural fragmentation.

Information is abundant but unreliable.
Institutions fail faster than they can be replaced.
Improvisation becomes survival.

Mastery here often develops through:
- Adaptation
- Parallel systems
- Community resilience
- Tactical intervention

---

### Manzo Era — The Far Frontier

A hybrid future of biodomes, star travel, and temporal complexity.

Scale increases.
Consequences propagate widely.
Technology amplifies intent.

Mastery here often develops through:
- Systems thinking
- Responsibility at distance
- Hybrid intelligence
- Long-term planning

---

## How Era Expression Affects Play

Era expression influences:
- Which challenges appear most often
- Which abilities are under pressure
- What kinds of decisions carry weight
- How advancement feels narratively

It does **not** invalidate earlier development.

A character carries their history forward.
They adapt — they do not reset.

---

## Moving Between Eras

Characters may:
- Begin in one era
- Transition between eras
- Experience multiple eras across a single lifetime

When this happens, era expression shifts gradually.

New pressures reveal different aspects of the same archetype. Old habits may help — or hinder.

Growth comes from noticing what still works, and what must change.

---

## Design Intent

Era expression exists to:
- Preserve character identity across radical context shifts
- Avoid subclass explosion
- Support long-form, multi-era play
- Keep mastery adaptive rather than prescriptive

The system assumes that no single era represents the “true” form of a class.

Each reveals something different.

---

## A Final Orientation

Do not think of era expression as a choice you must perfect.

Think of it as a **conversation** between your character and the world they inhabit.

The world changes.
You respond.
The archetype endures.
