# Starting State and First Session

When play begins, your character is already in motion.

They are not freshly assembled.
They are not fully prepared.
They are not waiting for destiny to arrive.

They are *mid-trajectory*.

---

## The Starting State

Your starting state represents your character’s current position in the world — materially, emotionally, and situationally.

It includes:
- Your location
- Your immediate circumstances
- What you have access to
- What you lack
- What pressures are already present

You do not begin in equilibrium.
You begin *inside a situation*.

---

## No Prologue Required

Future Sky does not require an introductory quest or scripted opening scene.

The world is already active.
Systems are already under strain.
Events are already unfolding.

Your first session is not about discovering the plot.
It is about discovering **where you are standing**.

---

## What the Game Will Ask of You First

Early play will tend to ask you to:
- Observe rather than optimise
- Respond rather than plan
- Act with incomplete information
- Make small choices that carry texture

You will rarely be given a perfect option.
You will often be given several *viable* ones.

Pay attention to how your character feels when choosing.

---

## Uncertainty Is Intentional

You may not immediately understand:
- The full implications of your abilities
- The limits of your resources
- The nature of certain systems or artefacts
- The significance of recurring details

This is not a failure of explanation.
It is a feature of the world.

Understanding accrues through engagement.

---

## First Actions Matter

Your earliest decisions help establish:
- How you approach risk
- What you prioritise
- How you treat uncertainty
- What you ignore

The world remembers patterns.

Small actions rarely end stories.
They often begin them.

---

## Failure Is Informative

Early failure is not punitive.

It is a signal:
- Of misalignment
- Of underestimated cost
- Of unseen pressure

Failure introduces information.
What you do with that information matters more than the failure itself.

---

## The Role of the Cube (At the Beginning)

If your character has access to a Cube, it will not reveal itself fully.

At most, it may:
- Respond to simple interaction
- Store or retrieve limited items
- Display incomplete information
- Behave in ways that feel suggestive but unclear

This is normal.

Do not attempt to exhaust the Cube.
Let it unfold.

---

## Group Play and Asynchrony

Future Sky supports both synchronous and asynchronous play.

You may:
- Act alone
- Respond later
- Be affected by events you did not witness
- Influence situations indirectly

Your absence does not pause the world.
Your presence changes it.

---

## What to Focus On

In your first session, focus on:
- Orientation rather than mastery
- Relationships rather than leverage
- Observation rather than explanation

Trust that clarity will emerge.
Do not force it.

---

## When the First Session Ends

A first session may end without resolution.

That is acceptable.

If you can answer:
- *Where am I?*
- *What pressures are present?*
- *What did I choose to engage with?*

Then the session has done its work.

---

## Why This Matters

This approach ensures that:
- Players are not rushed into optimisation
- Discovery feels earned
- The world feels active and persistent
- Identity emerges through action

You are not here to solve the game.
You are here to inhabit it.

---

## A Final Orientation

You do not need to be certain.

You need only be present.

The world will meet you there.
