# Backgrounds and Origin

Your background describes where your character comes from.

Your origin describes what that place taught them.

In Future Sky, backgrounds are not rigid templates or exhaustive histories. They are **contextual starting points** — sources of familiarity, access, and instinct that shape how a character enters the world.

They explain *why certain things feel natural*.

---

## Background Is Context, Not Destiny

A background does not define who your character must be.

It defines:
- What they have been exposed to
- What kinds of environments they understand
- What skills or habits they developed early
- What assumptions they may carry into new situations

Background provides momentum.
It does not determine trajectory.

---

## Origin as Lived Experience

Origin is not just geography or culture.

It includes:
- Economic conditions
- Social norms
- Environmental pressures
- The presence or absence of authority
- The degree of stability or collapse

Two characters from the same place may carry very different origins depending on how they lived within it.

---

## Backgrounds Are Broad by Design

Future Sky backgrounds are intentionally **broad and porous**.

Examples might include:
- Nomadic or itinerant life
- Institutional upbringing
- Frontier settlement
- Urban density
- Isolated or liminal communities

These are not exhaustive lists.
They are starting frames.

Broad backgrounds allow characters to:
- Fit multiple eras
- Adapt across timelines
- Accumulate meaning through play rather than pre-definition

---

## Mechanical Role of Backgrounds

Backgrounds typically provide:
- Skill proficiencies or familiarity
- Starting resources or contacts
- Contextual advantages in certain environments
- Narrative access to specific situations

They should never overshadow class or identity.
They support them.

A background answers the question:
> *“Why does this character know how to do this?”*

---

## Backgrounds and Era

The same background expresses differently across eras.

A frontier upbringing in a mythic world emphasises survival and ritual. In a collapsing present, it may emphasise improvisation and scarcity. In the far frontier, it may involve hybrid ecologies and engineered environments.

Backgrounds persist.
Their relevance shifts.

---

## Combining Background and Class

Background and class interact, but neither replaces the other.

- Background explains *where you learned*
- Class explains *how you engage*
- Identity explains *why you act*

A Rebel-Architect raised in institutional order will approach resistance differently than one raised on the margins. A Mystic-Kin from an urban collapse will embody care differently than one from a land-based lineage.

These differences matter.

---

## Leaving Space for Discovery

You are encouraged to leave gaps.

You do not need to:
- Name every mentor
- Define every belief
- Explain every transition

Unanswered questions are invitations for play.

The world will provide answers — sometimes gently, sometimes under pressure.

---

## Backgrounds and Change

Backgrounds are not static.

As characters move between eras, cultures, and systems, their origin becomes:
- A point of contrast
- A source of conflict
- A reservoir of resilience

Characters may outgrow their background.
They may return to it.
They may reinterpret it.

All of these are valid.

---

## Recording Your Background

You may record your background as:
- A short description
- A few key influences
- One formative environment
- A tension between origin and current life

Examples:
- *“Raised in scarcity, uncomfortable with excess.”*
- *“Learned cooperation before independence.”*
- *“Knows institutions from the inside.”*

Keep it concise.
You will add depth through play.

---

## Why This Matters

Backgrounds ensure that:
- Characters feel grounded
- Skills feel justified
- Consequences feel personal
- Worlds feel lived-in

They give the system history without freezing it.

---

## A Final Orientation

Choose a background that creates **interesting friction** with your class and identity.

Comfort is static.
Friction generates story.
