# Core Identity

Before abilities, skills, or class features, there is **identity**.

Core identity describes how your character tends to move through the world — what they notice, how they respond to pressure, and what feels meaningful or intolerable to them.

This is not a backstory.
It is not a personality quiz.
It is a starting orientation.

---

## What Core Identity Is

Core identity captures patterns rather than details.

It answers questions like:
- What does your character pay attention to?
- What kinds of situations draw them forward?
- What do they avoid, resist, or distrust?
- What feels worth the cost?

You do not need to explain *why* these tendencies exist yet.
You only need to notice that they do.

---

## Identity Before Narrative

Future Sky does not require a complete backstory at character creation.

In many cases, detailed histories can limit growth by fixing identity too early. Instead, this system encourages you to begin with **disposition**, not explanation.

Your character’s past will emerge through play:
- Through what they remember
- Through what they refuse
- Through how they react when systems fail

Identity precedes story.
Story accrues.

---

## Anchors, Not Labels

Core identity should be anchored, but flexible.

Avoid rigid labels such as:
- “Always calm”
- “Never trusts anyone”
- “Only motivated by power”

Instead, think in terms of tendencies:
- “Often seeks clarity before acting”
- “Struggles with authority”
- “Feels responsible for outcomes beyond their control”

These anchors give the world something to push against.

---

## Tension Is Useful

A strong core identity includes **internal tension**.

Consider:
- A value your character holds that is difficult to live by
- A fear that conflicts with their role
- A desire that complicates their obligations

Tension creates motion.
Motion creates growth.

Characters without tension often feel inert, regardless of power.

---

## Identity and Class

Your class describes *how* you engage the world.

Your core identity describes *why*.

Two characters of the same class may behave very differently because their identities pull them toward different interpretations of the archetype.

Let identity guide:
- How you express your class
- Which abilities feel natural
- When you strain beyond alignment

---

## Identity Is Not a Contract

Your core identity is not binding.

It is a starting point that will be tested, challenged, and reshaped.

You may discover:
- Beliefs that fail under pressure
- Values that deepen unexpectedly
- Aspects of self that were dormant until challenged

Change is expected.
Resistance to change is itself a form of identity.

---

## Recording Core Identity

You may record your core identity as:
- A short paragraph
- A few bullet points
- One or two guiding questions

Examples:
- *“What do I do when plans collapse?”*
- *“Whose safety do I prioritise?”*
- *“What am I willing to sacrifice?”*

Keep it brief.
You will revisit it.

---

## Why This Matters

Core identity affects play by:
- Guiding decisions under uncertainty
- Making consequences feel personal
- Informing resonance and imbalance
- Creating continuity across eras

It gives the system something to respond to besides statistics.

---

## A Final Orientation

Do not overthink this step.

Choose an orientation that feels interesting to explore rather than safe to inhabit.

The world will do the rest.
