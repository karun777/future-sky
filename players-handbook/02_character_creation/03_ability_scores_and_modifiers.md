# Ability Scores and Modifiers

Ability scores describe what your character can reliably bring to bear when circumstances demand action.

They represent broad, familiar competencies — physical, mental, and social — and function in ways that will feel immediately recognisable to players of d20-based games.

What differs in Future Sky is not how ability scores work, but **how much they are allowed to decide**.

---

## The Role of Ability Scores

Ability scores provide:
- A baseline for action
- A way to resolve uncertainty
- A shared mechanical language at the table

They do **not** define your character’s identity.
They do **not** determine your narrative importance.
They do **not** replace judgement, context, or consequence.

They are one input among many.

---

## The Six Abilities

Future Sky uses six core ability scores.

These map cleanly to traditional d20 expectations while remaining flexible enough to support multiple eras and play styles.

### Strength

Physical power, force, and the ability to impose yourself on the material world.

Strength governs lifting, striking, grappling, and resisting physical opposition through raw force.

---

### Dexterity

Coordination, reflex, and controlled movement.

Dexterity governs balance, evasion, precision, stealth, and tasks requiring fine motor control.

---

### Constitution

Endurance, resilience, and capacity to withstand strain.

Constitution governs stamina, resistance to harm, and the ability to function under prolonged stress.

---

### Intelligence

Reasoning, analysis, and the capacity to understand complex systems.

Intelligence governs problem-solving, recall, pattern recognition, and technical understanding.

---

### Wisdom

Perception, intuition, and situational awareness.

Wisdom governs noticing details, reading environments and people, and acting with practical judgement.

---

### Charisma

Presence, expression, and the ability to influence others.

Charisma governs communication, leadership, deception, inspiration, and emotional impact.

---

## Modifiers

Each ability score generates a **modifier**.

Modifiers represent how much your raw ability meaningfully affects outcomes. They are added to rolls when relevant and applied consistently across the system.

If you are familiar with d20 games, you already understand this process.

Future Sky uses standard modifier progression.

---

## Assigning Ability Scores

You may assign ability scores using any method agreed upon by your group or platform, including:
- Standard array
- Point buy
- Rolled scores

No method is privileged by the system.

Choose the approach that best supports the tone and fairness your table wants.

---

## Ability Scores and Context

Ability scores do not exist in isolation.

Their impact is shaped by:
- Environment
- Tools and preparation
- Emotional state
- Resonance and mana alignment
- Narrative positioning

A high ability score does not guarantee success.
A low ability score does not forbid it.

Context matters.

---

## Strain, Cost, and Overreach

Ability scores represent *capacity*, not immunity.

Repeated exertion, misalignment, or sustained imbalance can strain even high abilities. Pushing beyond your limits is possible — but costly.

Future Sky encourages players to notice:
- When reliance on a strength becomes a crutch
- When a weakness invites creative solutions
- When strain accumulates invisibly

This is where growth begins.

---

## Abilities and Advancement

As characters develop, ability scores may improve.

However, advancement in Future Sky is not primarily about maximising numbers. Growth often appears as:
- Expanded options
- Reduced cost through resonance
- Greater narrative leverage
- Improved judgement under pressure

Ability scores support these developments.
They do not replace them.

---

## Ability Scores Over Time

In Future Sky, ability scores are **not immutable**.

They represent current capacity, not permanent limits.

Characters change as they act, train, strain, recover, and adapt. Physical abilities respond to use and injury. Mental abilities deepen through learning and pattern recognition. Social presence evolves through experience, confidence, and consequence.

This process is gradual.
It is uneven.
It is contextual.

---

## Growth Through Use and Training

Ability scores may improve through:
- Repeated application under pressure
- Sustained training or practice
- Narrative milestones that reshape capacity
- Long-term adaptation to environment or role

Growth is not automatic.
It reflects *how* a character has lived, not just how long.

Likewise, neglect, injury, or imbalance may slow growth — or temporarily reduce effectiveness.

---

## Why This Matters

This approach allows characters to:
- Develop organically over long play
- Reflect lived experience rather than static builds
- Change in ways that feel earned
- Avoid early optimisation traps

A character is not locked into who they were at creation.
They are shaped by what they endure.

---

## A Note for Experienced d20 Players

If you are used to fixed ability scores, think of this system as an extension rather than a replacement.

Ability scores still function as expected in moment-to-moment play. Their evolution happens slowly, deliberately, and with narrative weight.

You do not need to plan for it.
You will notice it when it matters.
