# Skills, Proficiencies, and Mastery

Skills describe **what your character has learned to do**.

They are the primary way characters grow breadth, adapt to new challenges, and develop capabilities beyond their initial orientation. Where class defines *how you engage the world*, skills define *what you have practiced within it*.

In Future Sky, skills are where evolution happens.

---

## Skills as Lived Capability

A skill is not a static bonus.

It represents:
- Repeated action
- Attention over time
- Learning through success and failure
- Adaptation under pressure

Skills grow because they are used.
They stagnate when they are ignored.

---

## Proficiency

Proficiency indicates familiarity and reliability.

Being proficient means:
- You know the basic forms
- You understand common failure modes
- You can act without hesitation in familiar conditions

Proficiency does not imply mastery.
It implies competence.

---

## Mastery

Mastery reflects depth rather than breadth.

A mastered skill:
- Costs less to use
- Performs more reliably under strain
- Reveals options others cannot see
- Carries narrative authority in its domain

Mastery is not achieved through levels alone.
It emerges through sustained engagement.

---

## Skills and Class Identity

Skills interact with class, but they do not replace it.

Your class shapes:
- Which skills feel natural
- Which skills cost less to develop
- How skills express themselves under pressure

However, **no skill is locked behind a class**.

A character may develop skills traditionally associated with other archetypes — but doing so reflects learning, not transformation.

You do not become another class.
You expand your range.

---

## Sideways Growth (Not Multiclassing)

Future Sky does not use traditional multiclassing.

Characters do not split levels or archetypes. Instead, they grow **sideways** through skills, resonance, and experience.

This allows:
- Hybrid capability without identity dilution
- Emergent playstyles shaped by context
- Trade-offs rather than optimisation shortcuts

A Dreamer-Technician who masters physical traversal does not become a Primal-Rider — but they may move through the world with surprising competence.

A Rebel-Architect who develops deep relational skills does not become Mystic-Kin — but their structures may begin to heal rather than dominate.

This growth is earned.
It carries cost.
It leaves marks.

---

## Cost and Misalignment

Developing skills outside your class’s natural alignment is possible.

It is also harder.

Such growth may involve:
- Increased mana expenditure
- Greater strain or imbalance
- Slower mastery progression
- Narrative complications

These costs are intentional.

They ensure that hybridity feels *earned*, not assembled.

---

## Skill Use and Resonance

Skills interact dynamically with resonance.

When skill use aligns with your internal state and context:
- Cost is reduced
- Outcomes are clearer
- Growth is sustainable

When misaligned:
- Effects may be distorted
- Strain accumulates
- Consequences propagate unpredictably

Pay attention to how your character feels when using certain skills.
That feedback matters.

---

## Skills Over Time

Skills change as characters do.

They may:
- Improve through repetition
- Atrophy through neglect
- Transform in expression across eras
- Gain new meaning under different pressures

A skill learned for survival may later become one of leadership.
A skill learned for efficiency may later carry ethical weight.

---

## Why This Matters

This system ensures that:
- Characters remain coherent
- Growth reflects lived experience
- Archetypes remain meaningful
- Adaptation is possible without fragmentation

You are not assembling a build.
You are becoming someone.

---

## A Final Orientation

Do not ask:
- *“What class should I dip into?”*

Ask instead:
- *“What has my character learned to do — and what did it cost?”*

The answer will shape who they are becoming.
