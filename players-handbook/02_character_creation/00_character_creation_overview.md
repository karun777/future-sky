# Character Creation Overview

Creating a character in Future Sky should feel familiar.

You will choose abilities, background, and a class. You will define what your character is good at, where they come from, and how they tend to act when the world pushes back.

What makes Future Sky different is not *what* you choose — it is **how those choices unfold over time**.

A character in Future Sky is not a static build.
They are an evolving response to changing worlds.

---

## Archetypes That Endure

Classes in Future Sky are **archetypal**.

They represent enduring ways of engaging with reality: dreaming, resisting, navigating, shaping, protecting, disrupting, remembering. These archetypes recur across eras because the challenges they address recur.

A Dreamer in an ancient world, a collapsing present, or a far frontier future is still a Dreamer — but the tools, pressures, and expressions of that role change.

The archetype remains.
The context transforms it.

---

## Era Shapes Expression

Every character exists within an era, and that era matters.

The same class will feel different depending on whether the world is:
- Mythic and elemental
- Fragmented and collapsing
- Hybrid, frontier, and post-collapse

This is not cosmetic.

Each era emphasises different survival pressures, moral tensions, and paths to mastery. Characters evolve by responding to those pressures, not by following a single linear progression.

Growth is contextual.
Mastery is adaptive.

---

## Identity Before Optimisation

Future Sky encourages players to begin with **identity**, not efficiency.

Before thinking about optimal builds or long-term advancement, you are invited to consider:
- Who your character is
- What they care about
- What they are trying to understand, protect, or escape

The system supports optimisation, but it does not centre it. Characters who are mechanically efficient but narratively brittle often struggle to adapt as the world changes.

Resonance matters.
Attention matters.
Intent matters.

---

## Layers of Character

A character in Future Sky is defined across several layers:

- **Core identity** — who you are and how you tend to respond
- **Abilities and skills** — what you can do reliably
- **Class archetype** — how you engage the world at a fundamental level
- **Era expression** — how that archetype manifests in context
- **Resonance** — chakra alignment, elemental flow, and internal balance
- **Fate influences** — subtle tendencies shaped by timing and circumstance

You do not need to master all of these at once.
They will reveal themselves through play.

---

## Familiar Choices, Deeper Consequences

If you are experienced with d20 roleplaying games, much of the structure here will feel recognisable.

Ability scores function as expected.
Skills and proficiencies operate within familiar bounds.
Classes provide direction and identity.

What changes is **what those choices commit you to**.

A class choice is not just a role in combat.
It is a trajectory of development.
A background is not just flavour.
It grants access, context, and consequence.

---

## Character Creation as Beginning, Not Completion

Creating a character in Future Sky does not complete them.

It initiates them.

You are choosing a starting posture toward the world, not a finished form. Growth will involve trade-offs, moments of crisis, shifts in identity, and encounters that force reevaluation.

Some characters harden.
Some transform.
Some fracture and reassemble.

All of these are valid outcomes.
