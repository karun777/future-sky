# Starting Equipment and Resources

Starting equipment represents what your character has access to **right now**.

Not what they will acquire.
Not what they deserve.
Not what they are optimised for.

It reflects circumstance, preparation, and recent history.

---

## Equipment as Context

In Future Sky, equipment is situational.

Tools, gear, and resources matter because they shape:
- What actions are possible
- How costly those actions are
- Which risks are acceptable
- How the world responds to you

Equipment does not replace skill or judgement.
It amplifies or constrains them.

---

## Starting Light

Characters begin play with **modest, functional equipment**.

This typically includes:
- Personal clothing appropriate to environment
- A small number of tools relevant to background or class
- Basic means of travel or movement
- Limited consumable resources

You are not fully prepared.
That is intentional.

---

## Class and Equipment

Your class influences:
- What kinds of equipment feel natural
- What you know how to use effectively
- What tools you instinctively maintain

It does not guarantee access.

A Primal-Rider without proper gear is still capable.
A Dreamer-Technician without tools is still dangerous — but differently so.

---

## Background and Resources

Background often determines:
- What you start with
- What you know how to source
- Who you might call upon
- What you lack

Someone raised in scarcity values redundancy.
Someone raised in infrastructure assumes access until it fails.

These assumptions will be tested.

---

## Equipment Is Not Static

Gear degrades.
Resources deplete.
Tools break, are lost, or become obsolete.

Maintenance matters.
Adaptation matters more.

Improvised solutions are often as important as specialised tools.

---

## Currency and Value

Value in Future Sky is contextual.

What counts as currency depends on:
- Location
- Era
- Culture
- Situation

You may begin with:
- Trade goods
- Favour-based access
- Information
- Salvage
- Formal currency

None of these guarantee leverage everywhere.

---

## Carrying Capacity and Attention

What you carry affects how you move.

Heavy load limits speed and endurance.
Excess equipment draws attention.
Minimalism trades safety for agility.

These are not penalties.
They are trade-offs.

---

## Access Over Ownership

Many resources in Future Sky are not owned.

You may have:
- Temporary access
- Shared use
- Conditional permission
- Borrowed tools

Access can be revoked.
Ownership can be challenged.

Pay attention to how you rely on what is not yours.

---

## Equipment and Advancement

Progression does not assume constant gear upgrades.

Improvement often comes from:
- Better use of existing tools
- Reduced reliance on equipment
- Increased adaptability
- Learning what to leave behind

New equipment changes options.
It also introduces new dependencies.

---

## Why This Matters

This approach ensures that:
- Characters feel grounded and vulnerable
- Logistics remain meaningful
- Improvisation is rewarded
- Power is not front-loaded

You begin capable, not complete.

---

## A Final Orientation

Start with what makes sense.

Trust that the world will provide what you need — or demand that you adapt without it.

Both outcomes are meaningful.

## The Cube

Every character in Future Sky has some relationship to a Cube.

You may begin play with one.
You may have recently recovered it.
You may know it only imperfectly.
You may not yet understand what it contains.

But it exists.

---

## What the Cube Is (and Is Not)

The Cube is not a container in the ordinary sense.

It is not merely storage.
It is not simply technology.
It is not fully understood — even by those who rely on it.

The Cube functions as:
- A personal vault
- A record of experience
- A navigational and memory device
- A liminal space your character can enter
- A system that unlocks capabilities over time

It does not obey all the rules of the world.
That is intentional.

---

## Access, Not Control

Possessing a Cube does not mean mastering it.

Some functions are immediately available.
Others require:
- Specific conditions
- Narrative triggers
- Alignment or resonance
- Time, loss, or return

The Cube responds to engagement.
It does not reveal itself all at once.

---

## The Cube and Equipment

Certain items may:
- Be stored within the Cube
- Only function when interfaced through it
- Appear to belong to the Cube rather than the world

Other items may resist it entirely.

What can and cannot be contained is not arbitrary.
It will become clearer through play.

---

## Entering the Cube

At times, a character may:
- Enter the Cube
- Experience internal environments
- Encounter stored impressions, maps, or echoes
- Become temporarily unreachable from the outside world

These moments are rare, significant, and often transformative.

The Cube is not always safe.
It is always meaningful.

---

## Loss, Damage, and Recovery

Cubes can be:
- Lost
- Separated from their bearer
- Damaged
- Inaccessible for periods of time

This does not erase progress.
It changes the terms of engagement.

Recovering a Cube — or discovering a new layer within it — is often a pivotal moment in a character’s story.

---

## Why the Cube Matters

The Cube allows Future Sky to:
- Preserve continuity across eras
- Support persistent character state
- Introduce new systems without retcon
- Tie mechanics directly to narrative discovery

For players, it is a companion, a mystery, and a responsibility.

For the world, it is a relic of something larger.

---

## A Final Orientation

You do not start knowing what your Cube can do.

You start knowing that it matters.

Pay attention when it changes.
