# Movement, Navigation, and Zones

Movement in Future Sky is not just positional.

It represents attention, intention, exposure, and time spent moving through the world. This chapter explains how navigation works, how space is structured, and why movement choices matter.

---

## Zones Over Grids

Future Sky favours zones over precise grids.

A zone represents:
- A meaningful area of action
- Shared environmental conditions
- Narrative continuity
- A coherent space for interaction

Zones may be:
- A room
- A street segment
- A forest clearing
- A starship deck
- A digital or liminal space

Precision matters only when it matters.

---

## Movement as Commitment

Moving into a new zone:
- Exposes you to new pressures
- Advances time
- Triggers environmental checks
- Risks encounters or discovery

Movement is a form of commitment.

---

## Soft vs Hard Movement

There are two modes of movement.

### Soft Movement
Used when:
- Time pressure is low
- No immediate threat is present
- The journey is routine or familiar

Soft movement is abstracted.
Details are summarised unless attention is directed.

### Hard Movement
Used when:
- Time matters
- Danger is present
- Precision is required
- Surveillance or pursuit is active

Hard movement brings detail, turns, and risk into focus.

---

## Navigation and Orientation

Navigation relies on:
- Landmarks
- Maps and records
- Local knowledge
- Environmental cues
- Technological or mystical aids

Getting lost is possible.
So is misreading the terrain.

---

## Discovery and Hidden Routes

Not all paths are obvious.

Hidden routes may require:
- Observation
- Prior knowledge
- Specific tools
- Social access
- Risk-taking

Discovery rewards curiosity.

---

## Vertical and Layered Space

Zones may be layered.

Examples include:
- Rooftops, streets, and sublevels
- Surface, underwater, and underground
- Physical, digital, and symbolic layers

Changing layers often carries cost or risk.

---

## Travel Distance and Scale

Travel may be:
- Immediate (within a zone)
- Local (between nearby zones)
- Regional (across large areas)
- Interstellar or cross-era

As scale increases:
- Abstraction increases
- Logistics matter more
- Preparation becomes critical

---

## Navigation Failure

Failure to navigate may result in:
- Delay
- Exposure
- Resource loss
- Unexpected encounters
- Entry into hostile or unknown zones

Failure moves the story forward.

---

## Why This Matters

This system ensures that:
- Movement has weight
- Exploration remains meaningful
- Time pressure can emerge naturally
- Scale does not break play
- Travel supports narrative pacing

Where you go matters.

---

## A Final Orientation

Do not ask:
- “How far can I move?”

Ask instead:
- “What am I committing to?”
- “What might notice me?”
- “What changes if I go this way?”

Movement reveals intention.
