# Perception, Investigation, and Secrets

The world of Future Sky is layered.

Not everything is immediately visible, and not everything wants to be found. This chapter explains how characters notice details, uncover hidden information, and interact with secrets — without reducing discovery to a single roll.

---

## Perception as Attention

Perception is not passive.

It reflects:
- What you are paying attention to
- What you expect to see
- What you are emotionally or cognitively primed for
- What you have learned to notice over time

You do not perceive everything.
You perceive what you are oriented toward.

---

## Passive Awareness

Characters always have a baseline awareness.

Passive perception may reveal:
- Obvious changes
- Environmental mood shifts
- Signs of disturbance
- Familiar patterns
- Things that *do not belong*

Passive awareness rarely reveals full truth.
It provides prompts, not answers.

---

## Active Investigation

Investigation begins when attention is focused.

Active investigation involves:
- Asking specific questions
- Examining physical details
- Cross-referencing information
- Testing assumptions
- Spending time and effort

Investigation costs time.
Time invites consequence.

---

## Degrees of Revelation

Information is rarely binary.

Discovery may reveal:
- Partial truth
- Ambiguous signals
- Contradictory clues
- Emotional or symbolic impressions
- Leads rather than answers

Secrets often unfold in stages.

---

## Secrets as Systems

Secrets are not just hidden facts.

They may be:
- Actively protected
- Socially enforced
- Culturally normalised
- Psychologically avoided
- Environmentally concealed

Understanding *why* something is hidden is often more important than uncovering it.

---

## False Positives and Misinterpretation

Not all conclusions are correct.

Misinterpretation may result from:
- Bias
- Incomplete data
- Environmental interference
- Emotional projection
- Intentional deception

Incorrect conclusions still shape action.

---

## Tools and Aids

Perception and investigation may be supported by:
- Equipment
- Technology
- Training
- Ritual
- Companions
- Prior experience

Tools expand reach.
They do not guarantee clarity.

---

## Social Secrets

People are often the most complex environments.

Social secrets may involve:
- Lies
- Omissions
- Conflicting loyalties
- Internal contradictions
- Half-truths presented as certainty

Trust is a form of investigation.

---

## When Secrets Resist

Some secrets resist discovery.

This may be due to:
- Insufficient access
- Incomplete context
- Narrative timing
- Structural protection
- Deliberate obfuscation

Resistance is information.

---

## Why This Matters

This approach ensures that:
- Discovery feels earned
- Investigation drives play
- Secrets retain mystery
- Players engage thoughtfully
- Knowledge has consequence

Not knowing is part of play.

---

## A Final Orientation

Do not ask:
- “What do I roll to find this?”

Ask instead:
- “What am I actually looking for?”
- “Why might this be hidden?”
- “What happens if I’m wrong?”

The world reveals itself to those who listen carefully.
