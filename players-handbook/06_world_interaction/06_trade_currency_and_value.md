# Trade, Currency, and Value

Value in Future Sky is contextual.

Currency exists, but it is only one way of measuring worth. Trust, access, information, favours, and scarcity often matter more than numbers on a ledger. This chapter explains how trade works, why money is never neutral, and how value shifts across worlds and eras.

---

## Currency Is a Tool, Not the System

Currency simplifies exchange.

It:
- Reduces friction
- Enables distance trade
- Standardises value within a context

It does not replace relationships, reputation, or leverage.

---

## Multiple Forms of Value

Value may take many forms:
- Currency or credits
- Barter goods
- Information
- Access or introductions
- Protection
- Favour or obligation
- Cultural or symbolic items

What is valuable depends on who you are dealing with.

---

## Local Economies

Most economies are local.

Local economies are shaped by:
- Scarcity
- Environment
- Political control
- Cultural norms
- Recent events

An item prized in one place may be worthless elsewhere.

---

## Barter and Negotiation

Barter is common.

Successful trade often depends on:
- Understanding needs
- Timing
- Reputation
- Willingness to compromise
- Accepting uneven exchanges

A “fair” trade is a social agreement.

---

## Information as Currency

Information is often the most valuable commodity.

It may:
- Open restricted access
- Reduce risk
- Shift power dynamics
- Expose hidden systems
- Create obligation

Selling information creates ripples.

---

## Illicit Trade and Contraband

Some goods exist outside the law.

Contraband may:
- Be highly valuable
- Carry severe risk
- Attract attention
- Imply faction involvement
- Trigger long-term consequences

Illicit trade is never isolated.

---

## Debt and Obligation

Not all transactions are settled immediately.

Debt may:
- Bind characters to factions
- Create narrative hooks
- Complicate future choices
- Be called in at inconvenient times

Unpaid debts accumulate pressure.

---

## Economic Pressure

Scarcity creates tension.

Economic pressure may:
- Force risky decisions
- Encourage alliances
- Drive exploration
- Shape moral choices

Wealth does not remove pressure.
It changes its form.

---

## Inflation, Collapse, and Shifts

Economies change.

Events may:
- Devalue currency
- Collapse trade routes
- Create sudden scarcity
- Shift power to new groups

Stability is temporary.

---

## Player Impact on Economies

Player actions can influence markets.

This may include:
- Disrupting supply
- Introducing new goods
- Hoarding or flooding resources
- Empowering or destabilising factions

The economy reacts.

---

## Why This Matters

This approach ensures that:
- Trade supports story
- Wealth creates choices, not solutions
- Scarcity drives play
- Social systems remain central
- Value feels lived-in

You are not just buying things.

You are participating in a system.

---

## A Final Orientation

Do not ask:
- “How much does this cost?”

Ask instead:
- “Who controls this?”
- “Who loses if I have it?”
- “What does this bind me to?”

Every exchange leaves a trace.
