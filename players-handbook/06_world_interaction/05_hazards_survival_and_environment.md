# Hazards, Survival, and Environment

Not all threats wear a face.

In Future Sky, the environment itself applies pressure. Weather, scarcity, terrain, systems, and neglect can be just as dangerous as enemies — often more so. This chapter explains how hazards function, how survival is tested, and why the world itself must be respected.

---

## The Environment as an Actor

The environment is not neutral.

It:
- Shapes available choices
- Imposes limits
- Punishes neglect
- Rewards preparation
- Accumulates consequence

Ignoring the environment is a decision.

---

## Types of Hazards

Hazards may be physical, social, or systemic.

Common examples include:
- Extreme weather or climate conditions
- Hostile terrain
- Environmental decay or instability
- Toxic, irradiated, or corrupted zones
- Surveillance-heavy or controlled spaces
- Scarcity of food, water, air, or power

Some hazards are obvious.
Others reveal themselves over time.

---

## Exposure and Accumulation

Many hazards do not resolve instantly.

Exposure may:
- Accumulate strain
- Reduce effectiveness
- Alter perception
- Trigger delayed consequences
- Force retreat or adaptation

Survival often depends on recognising when to stop.

---

## Preparation and Mitigation

Preparation matters.

Mitigation may involve:
- Appropriate gear
- Knowledge of the environment
- Timing and route choice
- Alliances or local support
- Rest and recovery beforehand

Preparation reduces risk.
It does not eliminate it.

---

## Survival as Ongoing Play

Survival is not a binary state.

It includes:
- Managing fatigue
- Rationing resources
- Maintaining equipment
- Monitoring health and morale
- Making trade-offs under pressure

Small failures compound.

---

## Environmental Clues

The world provides warnings.

Signs may include:
- Subtle changes in sound or light
- Unnatural stillness
- Signs of prior failure
- Inconsistent systems
- Behavioural shifts in inhabitants

Ignoring clues accelerates consequence.

---

## Cascading Failures

Hazards often cascade.

One failure may:
- Expose another vulnerability
- Force a worse decision later
- Reduce recovery options
- Lock out safe paths

Cascades are rarely random.
They are logical.

---

## Safe Zones and False Safety

Safe zones exist — but they are contextual.

Safety may depend on:
- Time of day
- Political alignment
- Environmental stability
- Resource availability

Safety can degrade.

---

## Environmental Mastery

With experience, characters learn to:
- Read conditions quickly
- Anticipate danger
- Exploit environmental features
- Survive longer with less
- Teach others what to watch for

Mastery is quiet competence.

---

## Why This Matters

This approach ensures that:
- The world feels alive
- Non-combat danger is meaningful
- Preparation is rewarded
- Neglect carries cost
- Survival stories emerge naturally

You do not conquer the environment.

You negotiate with it.

---

## A Final Orientation

Do not ask:
- “Can we survive this?”

Ask instead:
- “For how long?”
- “At what cost?”
- “What happens if we stay?”

The environment always has the final word.
