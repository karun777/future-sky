# Social Play, Reputation, and Factions

In Future Sky, people remember.

They remember what you did, how you did it, who you sided with, and who paid the price. Social play is not a secondary system — it is one of the primary ways characters exert influence on the world.

This chapter explains how reputation forms, how factions function, and why social choices carry long-term consequence.

---

## Social Interaction as Action

Speaking is an action.

Listening is an action.

Choosing silence is an action.

Social play includes:
- Conversation
- Negotiation
- Deception
- Intimidation
- Solidarity
- Withdrawal

Each carries risk and intent.

---

## Reputation as Accumulated Signal

Reputation is not a single score.

It is an accumulation of signals:
- Behaviour over time
- Public vs private actions
- Consistency or contradiction
- Visible outcomes
- Stories told about you

Different groups may hold different versions of your reputation.

---

## Local vs Global Reputation

Reputation exists at multiple scales.

- **Local reputation** reflects how a specific community views you  
- **Faction reputation** reflects standing within organised groups  
- **Wider reputation** reflects how stories travel beyond direct contact  

Your name may mean safety in one place and danger in another.

---

## Factions as Living Entities

Factions are not monoliths.

They have:
- Internal divisions
- Conflicting priorities
- Limited information
- Emotional memory
- Capacity for change

You interact with people, not abstractions.

---

## Gaining and Losing Standing

Standing shifts through:
- Actions taken publicly
- Private dealings revealed later
- Who you protect
- Who you abandon
- Whose rules you break

Reputation changes slowly — until it doesn’t.

---

## Trust, Fear, and Obligation

Social relationships operate along multiple axes:
- Trust
- Fear
- Respect
- Debt
- Obligation

High trust can compensate for low authority.
High fear can substitute for reputation — briefly.

---

## Social Leverage

Leverage comes from:
- Information
- Timing
- Alliances
- Demonstrated competence
- Willingness to accept consequence

Leverage is situational.
It expires.

---

## Social Conflict

Not all conflict is violent.

Social conflict may involve:
- Public humiliation
- Political manoeuvring
- Reputation damage
- Exile or exclusion
- Forced compromise

Social defeat can be harder to recover from than physical loss.

---

## Masks and Personas

Characters may present different personas.

This may:
- Protect identity
- Enable infiltration
- Create narrative tension
- Backfire when exposed

Maintaining a mask requires effort.

---

## Redemption and Rupture

Reputation can change.

Redemption may require:
- Visible sacrifice
- Time
- Changed behaviour
- Acceptance of loss

Some ruptures cannot be repaired.
The world remembers.

---

## Why This Matters

This system ensures that:
- Social choices matter
- Influence has texture
- Factions feel alive
- Consequence outlasts scenes
- Power is not purely physical

Who you are to others shapes what becomes possible.

---

## A Final Orientation

Do not ask:
- “What should I say to pass this?”

Ask instead:
- “Who am I to them?”
- “What do they need right now?”
- “What am I willing to be known for?”

Every interaction leaves a trace.
