# Tools, Items, and Light Crafting

In Future Sky, items are not power by default.

They are leverage.

Tools extend capability, objects create opportunity, and crafted items encode intention. This chapter explains how equipment functions, how improvisation is rewarded, and how crafting exists without turning play into an inventory management exercise.

---

## Items as Context Shifters

Items rarely act alone.

They:
- Change what is possible in a situation
- Modify risk
- Alter approach
- Enable new interactions
- Create narrative hooks

An item is most powerful when it changes *how* you act, not just how hard you hit.

---

## Categories of Items

Items generally fall into a few broad categories:

- **Tools** — Enable or enhance actions (lockpicks, scanners, ritual implements)
- **Gear** — Worn or carried equipment that modifies approach
- **Consumables** — Temporary effects with trade-offs
- **Relics and Artifacts** — Narrative-heavy objects with layered meaning
- **Contraband** — Valuable, dangerous, or illegal items

An item’s category often matters more than its numeric effect.

---

## Improvisation and Creative Use

Creative use is encouraged.

An item used in an unexpected way may:
- Bypass a challenge
- Reduce cost or strain
- Introduce new consequences
- Shift narrative positioning

Improvisation may increase risk.
That risk is part of play.

---

## Quality, Condition, and Reliability

Not all items are equal.

Items may vary by:
- Craft quality
- Maintenance
- Age
- Environmental compatibility
- Cultural significance

Failure does not mean useless.
It means unpredictable.

---

## Light Crafting Philosophy

Crafting in Future Sky is intentionally lightweight.

It focuses on:
- Modifying or combining existing items
- Creating situational tools
- Repairing or repurposing equipment
- Encoding personal style or intent

Crafting is about problem-solving, not production chains.

---

## Materials and Access

Crafting depends on:
- Available materials
- Tools on hand
- Environment
- Time and safety
- Knowledge or training

Scarcity is contextual, not universal.

---

## Temporary vs Persistent Creations

Some crafted items are:
- Temporary
- Fragile
- Single-use

Others persist and evolve.

Persistence often carries:
- Maintenance cost
- Visibility
- Narrative consequence

---

## Cultural and Symbolic Value

Items may carry meaning beyond function.

Symbolic items can:
- Affect social interaction
- Signal affiliation
- Unlock or restrict access
- Trigger memory or resonance

Value is not always monetary.

---

## Limits and Load

Carrying capacity exists to encourage choice, not punishment.

Limits:
- Force prioritisation
- Encourage storage and planning
- Create vulnerability
- Reward preparation

What you leave behind matters.

---

## Why This Matters

This approach ensures that:
- Items support creativity
- Gear does not overshadow skill
- Crafting stays focused
- Inventory remains readable
- Objects remain narratively alive

You are not defined by what you carry.

---

## A Final Orientation

Do not ask:
- “Is this item optimal?”

Ask instead:
- “What problem does this solve?”
- “What risk does it introduce?”
- “What story does it carry?”

Tools shape the way you meet the world.
