# Death, Loss, and Return

In Future Sky, death matters.

So does loss.
So does return.

None of these are treated lightly — and none are used to erase play.

---

## What Death Is (and Is Not)

Death is not a fail state.
It is not a punishment.
It is not a reset button.

Death is a **major consequence** — one that changes the terms under which a character continues to exist in the world.

Sometimes death is final.
Sometimes it is not.
The difference is never arbitrary.

---

## Risk Is Real

Because death is not trivialised, risk retains meaning.

Violence, neglect, and reckless escalation can lead to outcomes that cannot be undone. Players are not protected from consequence — but they are respected by the system.

The game does not seek to surprise you with death.
It allows death when warning signs have been ignored or risks consciously embraced.

---

## Loss Beyond Death

Not all loss involves dying.

Characters may lose:
- Access to people or places
- Equipment or resources
- Reputation or trust
- Abodes or anchors
- Pieces of certainty

These losses often matter more than hit points.

Loss reshapes priorities.

---

## States of Being

Between life and death, other states may exist.

A character may be:
- Incapacitated
- Displaced
- Fragmented
- Trapped
- Unreachable
- Sustained only through intervention

These states are not lesser outcomes.
They are different trajectories.

---

## Return Is Not Free

When return is possible, it carries cost.

Return may involve:
- Lingering strain
- Altered resonance
- Changed relationships
- Gaps in memory
- New obligations
- Shifts in identity

Coming back is an event — not a convenience.

---

## The Role of the Cube

The Cube complicates death.

It may:
- Preserve echoes
- Record fragments
- Enable return under specific conditions
- Become inaccessible or damaged
- Demand something in exchange

The Cube does not guarantee survival.
It guarantees continuity.

How that continuity expresses itself is never neutral.

---

## Death Across Eras

Death does not behave identically across eras.

In some eras, return is mythic.
In others, it is technological.
In others, it is contested or forbidden.

The rules remain consistent.
The expression changes.

---

## Choosing Risk

Players may knowingly choose dangerous paths.

This is allowed.
It is respected.

Choosing risk means accepting the possibility of:
- Permanent change
- Lasting loss
- Transformation beyond expectation

Bravery is not immunity.

---

## Ending a Character

Sometimes a character’s story ends.

This may occur through:
- Final death
- Voluntary departure
- Transformation into something unplayable
- Narrative completion

An ending is not a failure.
It is a conclusion.

Characters who end still matter.
Their actions persist.

---

## Why This Matters

This approach ensures that:
- Stakes feel real
- Investment is rewarded
- Loss has narrative weight
- Return is meaningful
- Trust between player and system is maintained

You are allowed to care because the game will not cheapen that care.

---

## A Final Orientation

Do not ask:
- “Can I come back from this?”

Ask instead:
- “What am I willing to risk?”
- “What will change if I don’t return?”
- “What does this moment demand of me?”

The answer may be survival.
Or it may be something deeper.
