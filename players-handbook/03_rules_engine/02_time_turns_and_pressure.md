# Time, Turns, and Pressure

Time in Future Sky is not uniform.

Most of the time, it flows loosely.
Sometimes, it tightens suddenly.
Occasionally, it snaps into focus.

Understanding this rhythm is essential to play.

---

## Soft Time and Hard Time

Future Sky operates using two modes of time:

- **Soft Time** — ambient, flexible, asynchronous
- **Hard Time** — structured, urgent, consequential

The game moves fluidly between these modes.

Neither is more “real” than the other.

---

## Soft Time: The Default State

In soft time:
- Players may act asynchronously
- The world continues to evolve
- Actions resolve without strict turn order
- Time passes in narrative blocks rather than seconds

Soft time supports:
- Exploration
- Social interaction
- Investigation
- Preparation
- Downtime activity

Most play occurs here.

---

## Hard Time: When Pressure Arrives

Hard time is triggered when:
- Stakes escalate rapidly
- Events demand immediate response
- Multiple intents conflict directly
- Delay itself becomes meaningful

Examples include:
- Combat
- Chases
- Emergencies
- Social flashpoints
- Time-sensitive rituals or systems

In hard time, the system becomes more explicit.

---

## Turns as Attention, Not Seconds

Turns do not represent fixed durations.

A turn represents:
- A window of attention
- A chance to act meaningfully
- A moment where choice matters

Some turns are quick.
Some represent sustained effort.

What matters is **who can act, and when**.

---

## Initiative as Priority

When order matters, initiative determines priority.

Initiative may be influenced by:
- Awareness
- Preparation
- Positioning
- Resonance
- Context

Initiative is not a reward for speed alone.
It reflects readiness.

---

## Delayed Action Is Still Action

Choosing not to act immediately is a decision.

Delay may:
- Preserve safety
- Allow information to emerge
- Reduce strain
- Miss opportunities

The system treats delay as meaningful.
It is never neutral.

---

## Compression and Expansion

Time may compress or expand dynamically.

A complex action may resolve in a single turn.
A simple action may unfold over many.

This flexibility allows:
- Cinematic moments
- Tactical clarity
- Narrative pacing
- Mechanical fairness

Rigid timekeeping is avoided unless required.

---

## Pressure Windows

Certain moments create **pressure windows**.

During these windows:
- Options narrow
- Costs increase
- Consequences sharpen
- Hesitation becomes visible

Pressure windows are not constant.
They are events.

Learning to recognise them is part of mastery.

---

## Asynchrony and Presence

You do not need to be present at all times.

The world does not pause for absence.
But absence has texture.

You may return to find:
- Changed circumstances
- Resolved conflicts
- New pressures
- Lingering consequences

Your presence matters most during pressure.

---

## Why This Matters

This approach allows:
- Long-form, persistent play
- Meaningful urgency without burnout
- Tactical clarity when needed
- Narrative flow without micromanagement

Time becomes a mechanic without becoming a burden.

---

## A Final Orientation

Do not ask, “How many seconds do I have?”

Ask:
- “Is this moment tightening?”
- “What happens if I wait?”
- “What pressure am I under right now?”

The answer tells you how time is behaving.
