# Actions and Intent

In Future Sky, what matters most is **what you are trying to do**.

Not the button you press.
Not the rule you invoke.
Not the optimal sequence.

Intent comes first.
Mechanics respond to it.

---

## Intent Before Mechanics

An action begins when you state your intent.

Intent answers the question:
> *“What change am I trying to bring about in the world?”*

Examples:
- “I want to get past the guards without escalating.”
- “I’m trying to understand what this machine is actually doing.”
- “I want to slow them down long enough for us to escape.”
- “I’m testing whether this person can be trusted.”

You do not need to phrase intent in mechanical terms.
Clarity matters more than precision.

---

## Actions Are Not Always Singular

Many meaningful actions are **composite**.

An action may include:
- Movement
- Observation
- Interaction
- Communication
- Preparation

The system does not require you to isolate each micro-step unless time or pressure demands it.

Describe what you are doing.
The system will determine how it resolves.

---

## Declared Action vs Desired Outcome

It is important to distinguish between:
- **What you attempt**
- **What you hope happens**

You can declare an action with a clear goal and still fail to achieve the outcome.

This is not a trick.
It is how uncertainty enters the world.

---

## Passive and Background Actions

Not all actions are active.

Characters are always:
- Noticing
- Maintaining posture
- Carrying assumptions
- Exerting low-level effort

These background actions influence:
- Surprise
- Readiness
- Information flow
- Strain accumulation

You do not need to constantly declare them.
They are inferred from context.

---

## Preparation as Action

Preparation is one of the most powerful actions in Future Sky.

Preparing may involve:
- Gathering information
- Positioning resources
- Establishing relationships
- Reducing uncertainty
- Creating fallback options

Prepared actions often:
- Reduce cost
- Improve outcomes
- Avoid rolls entirely

Time spent preparing is rarely wasted.
But it is still time.

---

## When Time Compresses

Under pressure, actions become more granular.

In moments of urgency:
- Actions may be limited
- Order may matter
- Delay becomes meaningful
- Partial success becomes common

This is not a punishment.
It reflects the reality of constrained attention.

---

## Assisted Actions

Actions may be assisted by others.

Assistance:
- Must be justified in fiction
- May reduce cost or increase clarity
- Can also increase risk if coordination fails

Too many hands can help.
Too many hands can also make things worse.

---

## Conflicting Intents

When multiple intents collide:
- Priority is determined by timing, positioning, and context
- Resolution may involve opposed checks
- Partial outcomes are common

Conflict does not always mean combat.
It means incompatible goals.

---

## Why This Matters

This approach ensures that:
- Players think in terms of goals, not exploits
- Creativity is rewarded
- Systems respond coherently
- The world feels reactive rather than scripted

You are not navigating menus.
You are acting within a living system.

---

## A Final Orientation

State what you are trying to do.

Trust the system to meet you there.

If the world pushes back, pay attention to how — it is telling you something.
