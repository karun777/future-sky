# Rest, Recovery, and Downtime

In Future Sky, recovery is not automatic.

Rest is an action.
Recovery is a process.
Downtime is where change becomes possible.

Ignoring these rhythms has consequences.

---

## Rest Is Not a Reset

Rest does not return a character to a perfect state.

It reduces strain.
It stabilises imbalance.
It restores capacity.

What it does not do is erase consequence.

Injuries leave marks.
Choices leave echoes.
Patterns persist.

---

## Types of Rest

Not all rest is equal.

Rest may be:
- Brief pauses between pressure
- Extended periods of recovery
- Structured downtime with intention
- Forced rest due to exhaustion or injury

The depth of rest determines what can be restored.

---

## What Rest Restores

Depending on duration and quality, rest may:
- Reduce accumulated strain
- Restore mana alignment
- Improve clarity and focus
- Allow injuries to heal
- Prevent escalation of imbalance

Some effects require more than sleep.
Some require change.

---

## Recovery Requires Conditions

Effective recovery depends on context.

Recovery is influenced by:
- Safety
- Environment
- Resources
- Support from others
- Mental and emotional state

Rest taken under threat is rarely complete.

---

## Downtime as Opportunity

Downtime is not empty time.

It is when characters may:
- Train or practice skills
- Reflect on recent events
- Repair or modify equipment
- Rebalance chakras
- Strengthen relationships
- Prepare for future pressure

Downtime actions shape who your character becomes.

---

## Training and Practice

Training is a form of downtime.

It involves:
- Repetition
- Feedback
- Time investment
- Willingness to strain without breaking

Training improves capacity gradually.
Shortcuts are rare.

---

## Neglect and Burnout

Ignoring rest has consequences.

Sustained neglect may:
- Increase strain thresholds
- Slow recovery
- Distort judgement
- Cause cascading failure
- Force involuntary downtime

Burnout is not a single event.
It is an accumulation.

---

## Shared Downtime

Downtime can be shared.

Group recovery:
- Builds trust
- Enables coordination
- Reduces individual burden
- Creates narrative space

Not all growth is solitary.

---

## Downtime Across Eras

Downtime looks different across eras.

In some contexts it is ritual.
In others it is maintenance.
In others it is survival.

The function remains the same:
to integrate experience.

## Abodes and Places of Return

Most characters have — or seek — a place to return to.

An abode may be:
- A bunk or berth
- A shared room or shelter
- A vehicle or vessel
- A temporary hideout
- A long-held home
- Something newly claimed

It does not need to be permanent.
It does not need to be impressive.

It needs to be *yours* — for now.

---

## What an Abode Provides

An abode is not a simulation.

It provides context.

Depending on its nature, an abode may:
- Improve the quality of rest
- Enable certain downtime actions
- Provide storage or security
- Allow reflection without interruption
- Anchor a character in a location or community

These benefits are situational, not guaranteed.

---

## Safety, Stability, and Vulnerability

Not all abodes are equally safe.

Some are hidden.
Some are fragile.
Some are shared.
Some attract attention.

An abode can be:
- Improved
- Compromised
- Lost
- Abandoned
- Reclaimed

These changes are meaningful events, not housekeeping tasks.

---

## Abodes Across Eras

Where and how characters live changes across eras.

A home in one era may be:
- Impossible in another
- Recontextualised
- Remembered rather than inhabited
- Rebuilt or rediscovered

The idea of “home” persists even when the place does not.

---

## Abode and the Cube

The Cube and the abode serve different roles.

- The Cube travels with you.
- The abode waits for you.

The Cube remembers.
The abode reflects.

Both matter.

---

## Why This Matters

Abodes give characters:
- A sense of return
- A place where consequences settle
- A reason to care about location
- A rhythm between movement and rest

They make downtime feel lived-in rather than abstract.


---

## Why This Matters

This system ensures that:
- Strain has weight
- Rest has value
- Growth feels earned
- Long-term play remains viable
- Characters feel human

Recovery is part of the story.

---

## A Final Orientation

Do not ask:
- “How fast can I get back in?”

Ask instead:
- “What do I need to carry forward?”
- “What must I let settle?”
- “What am I preparing for?”

Rest is not an absence of play.
It is where play deepens.
