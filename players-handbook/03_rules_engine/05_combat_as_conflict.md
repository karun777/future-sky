# Combat as Conflict

Combat is one way conflict expresses itself.

It is not the only way.
It is not always the best way.
But when it happens, it matters.

In Future Sky, combat is dangerous, consequential, and shaped by context rather than isolated mechanics.

---

## Combat Is a State, Not a Mode

Combat is not a separate game system.

It is a **pressure state** — a moment where:
- Intent collides directly
- Time tightens
- Risk escalates
- Consequence sharpens

The rules that govern combat are extensions of the same principles that govern all action.

---

## Why Combat Happens

Combat usually emerges when:
- Goals become incompatible
- Negotiation fails
- Retreat is blocked
- Pressure windows close
- Systems collapse into force

Most fights are not planned.
They are arrived at.

Pay attention to how conflicts escalate — that knowledge often prevents the next one.

---

## Entering Combat

Combat begins when:
- Multiple actors pursue incompatible outcomes
- Delay would change the outcome materially
- Force becomes the deciding factor

Initiative determines who can act first — not who is fastest, but who is **most ready**.

Preparation, awareness, and positioning matter more than reflex alone.

---

## Turns in Combat

Combat turns represent windows of focused action.

A turn may include:
- Movement
- Attack or defence
- Use of abilities
- Environmental interaction
- Communication

You are not limited to “attack or wait”.
Creative action is expected.

---

## Positioning and Environment

Where you are matters.

Combat is shaped by:
- Cover and elevation
- Light, sound, and visibility
- Crowds and obstacles
- Weather and terrain
- Systems that can be disrupted

Ignoring the environment is often more dangerous than the enemy.

---

## Damage Is Not the Only Outcome

Harm is one consequence of combat — not the only one.

Combat may result in:
- Injury
- Exhaustion
- Disarmament
- Separation
- Capture
- Loss of resources
- Escalation of wider conflict

Ending a fight does not always mean winning it.

---

## Momentum and Morale

Combat has momentum.

Early success can:
- Reduce enemy resolve
- Open tactical options
- Shorten conflict

Early failure can:
- Increase pressure
- Narrow choices
- Force retreat

Morale matters — for enemies and allies alike.

---

## Retreat Is a Valid Outcome

Disengaging is not failure.

Choosing to withdraw may:
- Preserve life
- Reduce long-term cost
- Protect objectives
- Prevent escalation

Knowing when to retreat is a mark of experience.

---

## Group Combat

Group combat rewards coordination.

Working together can:
- Reduce individual strain
- Enable complex manoeuvres
- Control space
- Shift momentum

Poor coordination amplifies risk.

More people does not always mean more safety.

---

## Combat and Consequence

Combat always leaves residue.

After a fight, expect:
- Injury or strain
- Attention drawn
- Resources depleted
- New enemies or obligations
- Shifts in reputation

Violence is rarely invisible.

---

## Why This Matters

This approach ensures that:
- Combat feels intense without being constant
- Tactical thinking matters
- Preparation is rewarded
- Failure has weight
- Conflict remains meaningful

Combat is respected — not glorified.

---

## A Final Orientation

Do not ask:
- “How do I win this fight?”

Ask instead:
- “What does this fight change?”
- “What will it cost if I stay?”
- “What happens if I walk away?”

Sometimes the bravest move is not to strike.
