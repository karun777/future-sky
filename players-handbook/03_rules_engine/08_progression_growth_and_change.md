# Progression, Growth, and Change

In Future Sky, characters grow.

They do not simply become stronger.
They become more capable, more specialised, and more changed.

Progression is not a straight line.
It is a negotiation between breadth and depth.

---

## What Progression Means

Progression represents:
- Increased capacity to act
- Greater resilience under pressure
- Deeper mastery of chosen paths
- Expanded options in unfamiliar situations

It does not represent invincibility.
Limits remain — but they move.

---

## Breadth and Depth

Growth occurs along two axes.

### Breadth
Breadth represents versatility.

It includes:
- Learning new skills
- Gaining basic competence in new domains
- Adapting across contexts and eras
- Responding creatively to unfamiliar problems

Breadth widens what you *can attempt*.

---

### Depth
Depth represents mastery.

It includes:
- Refinement of core abilities
- Reduced cost for aligned actions
- Increased reliability under pressure
- Greater narrative authority in chosen domains

Depth deepens how well you *perform*.

---

## The Tension Between Them

Breadth and depth compete for attention.

Pursuing depth narrows focus.
Pursuing breadth slows mastery.

Neither is superior.
Both shape identity.

Characters who ignore this tension tend to fracture or stagnate.

---

## Growth Is Earned Through Use

Progression does not occur through abstraction alone.

Characters grow by:
- Acting under pressure
- Repeating meaningful actions
- Training deliberately
- Integrating failure
- Resting when necessary
- Surviving consequence

Growth reflects what you *do*, not just what you choose.

---

## Ability Scores and Capacity

Ability scores are not fixed.

They represent:
- Physical conditioning
- Cognitive flexibility
- Emotional regulation
- Perceptual acuity
- Willingness to endure strain

Scores may improve slowly through:
- Sustained practice
- Long-term training
- Significant life events
- Transformative experience

They may also degrade if neglected or damaged.

---

## Skills and Mastery

Skills represent applied knowledge.

As skills advance, characters may:
- Reduce strain cost
- Act faster or more precisely
- Attempt more complex manoeuvres
- Succeed more reliably under pressure

High mastery does not remove risk.
It changes the *shape* of risk.

---

## Chakras and Resonance

Chakras represent alignment.

Growth here reflects:
- Improved flow of energy
- Reduced distortion under stress
- Greater clarity of intent
- Deeper integration of experience

Resonance amplifies what is aligned.
Misalignment increases cost.

---

## Class as Ongoing Expression

Your class is not a cage.

It represents:
- A primary developmental trajectory
- A pattern of challenge
- A mode of engagement with the world

As you grow, your relationship to your class deepens.
You do not outgrow it — you reinterpret it.

---

## Multidisciplinary Growth

Characters may develop beyond a single archetype.

This is not free.

Cross-development requires:
- Time
- Practice
- Narrative justification
- Willingness to accept trade-offs

Breadth introduces flexibility.
Depth preserves coherence.

Unexamined expansion leads to dilution.

---

## Change Is Not Always Positive

Growth can wound.

Progression may introduce:
- New vulnerabilities
- Increased expectations
- Higher stakes
- Identity tension
- Responsibility you did not seek

Power changes the terrain you stand on.

---

## Why This Matters

This system ensures that:
- Characters feel distinct
- Choices remain meaningful
- Growth reflects lived experience
- Optimisation does not erase identity
- Long-term play remains interesting

You are not building a character.
You are becoming one.

---

## A Final Orientation

Do not ask:
- “What should I maximise?”

Ask instead:
- “What am I becoming?”
- “What does this cost me?”
- “What must I now carry?”

Growth is not about numbers going up.
It is about what those numbers now allow — and demand.
