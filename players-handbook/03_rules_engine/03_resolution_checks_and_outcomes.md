# Resolution, Checks, and Outcomes

Resolution determines **what actually happens** when intent meets uncertainty.

In Future Sky, checks are used sparingly, deliberately, and only when the outcome meaningfully matters.

The goal is not to simulate every action.
The goal is to resolve moments of consequence.

---

## When a Check Is Required

A check is called for when:
- The outcome is uncertain
- The stakes are real
- The result will change future options

If success is obvious, no roll is needed.
If failure would be meaningless, no roll is needed.

Rolling is a signal that the moment matters.

---

## The Anatomy of a Check

Most checks combine:
- A relevant ability modifier
- A relevant skill or proficiency
- Contextual modifiers (positive or negative)
- A d20 roll

The result is compared against a difficulty or opposing action.

This structure will feel familiar.
Its application is where Future Sky differs.

---

## Context Modifies Everything

Context is not flavour.

It actively affects resolution.

Context includes:
- Environment
- Preparation
- Tools and resources
- Emotional state
- Resonance and mana alignment
- Timing and pressure
- Narrative positioning

Good context may remove the need for a roll.
Poor context may increase cost even on success.

---

## Degrees of Outcome

Outcomes are rarely binary.

Most checks resolve along a spectrum:
- Clean success
- Success with cost
- Partial success
- Failure with information
- Compounding failure

A failed roll does not mean “nothing happens”.
It means *something else* happens.

---

## Success With Cost

Sometimes you succeed — but pay for it.

Cost may include:
- Strain or mana expenditure
- Loss of time
- Exposure or attention
- Resource depletion
- Unintended side effects

These costs are not punishments.
They are trade-offs.

---

## Failure as Information

Failure is informative.

A failed check may:
- Reveal hidden constraints
- Expose a new threat
- Clarify what preparation is missing
- Force a different approach

Repeated failure often signals misalignment — not incompetence.

Pay attention to patterns.

---

## Opposed Checks

When two intents collide directly, resolution may be opposed.

Opposed checks consider:
- Positioning
- Readiness
- Relevant skills
- Contextual leverage

Winning an opposed check does not always end conflict.
It may simply shift advantage.

---

## Hidden Rolls and Uncertainty

Not all checks are visible.

Some information is withheld intentionally:
- To preserve tension
- To prevent meta-optimisation
- To allow delayed consequence

You may act without knowing the full result immediately.

This mirrors how the world works.

---

## Checks Under Pressure

During hard time:
- Fewer rolls occur
- Each roll carries more weight
- Partial outcomes are common
- Cost increases rapidly

Efficiency matters.
So does restraint.

---

## Why This Matters

This approach ensures that:
- Dice support narrative rather than override it
- Preparation is rewarded
- Failure remains interesting
- Players stay engaged with context
- Outcomes feel earned

The roll is not the story.
It is a hinge.

---

## A Final Orientation

Do not ask:
- “What do I need to roll?”

Ask:
- “What am I risking?”
- “What changes if this goes wrong?”
- “Is this the right moment to push?”

The dice will answer — but only if you ask the right question.
